## 1. Setup and operator conventions

Let

\[
S=\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)=\mathbf F_p\sqcup\{A\},
\qquad A=\infty,
\qquad q=|S|=p+1,
\qquad m=p-1,
\]

and let \(U=\mathbf F_p\setminus\{-1\}\). The annealed kernel is

\[
K(x,y)=\frac1m\#\{u\in U:F_u(x)=y\}.
\]

All Hilbert-space norms and adjoints are on

\[
\mathcal H=L^2(S,\pi),
\qquad \pi(x)=\frac1q,
\]

and

\[
\Pi f=\pi(f)\,1.
\]

For fixed \(u\), \(F_u\) is a permutation of \(S\). Hence \(K\) is an average of permutation matrices.

### Step 1.1: stochasticity

**[PROVED]** \(K\) is doubly stochastic:

\[
K1=1,
\qquad
K^*1=1.
\]

It follows that \(K\Pi=\Pi K=\Pi\), \(K\) is an \(L^2(\pi)\)-contraction, and \(K^*\) is the reverse kernel. Since \(\pi\) is uniform,

\[
K^*(x,y)=K(y,x).
\]

Put

\[
T=K-\Pi.
\]

Then, for every \(n\ge1\),

\[
\boxed{T^n=K^n-\Pi.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

This follows immediately from \(K\Pi=\Pi K=\Pi\).

## 2. The exact Gram kernel and its row sums

Let

\[
H=KK^*.
\]

### Step 2.1: exact codegree identity

**[PROVED]** For all \(x,x'\in S\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
H(x,x')
&=\sum_{y\in S}K(x,y)K^*(y,x')\\
&=\sum_{y\in S}K(x,y)K(x',y)\\
&=\frac1{m^2}\#\{(u,v)\in U^2:F_u(x)=F_v(x')\}\\
&=\boxed{\frac{C_p(x,x')}{(p-1)^2}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Thus \(H\) is symmetric and positive semidefinite.

### Step 2.2: exact row-sum bookkeeping

**[PROVED]** For every fixed \(x\in S\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{x'\in S}C_p(x,x')
&=\sum_{u,v\in U}\#\{x'\in S:F_v(x')=F_u(x)\}\\
&=\sum_{u,v\in U}1\\
&=m^2,
\end{aligned}
\]

because each \(F_v\) is a permutation. Hence every row of \(H\) sums to one. Symmetry gives the column sums as well. Therefore \(KK^*\) is doubly stochastic.

### Step 2.3: exact infinity block

**[PROVED]** Since \(F_u(A)=0\) for every allowed \(u\),

\[
K(A,\cdot)=\delta_0.
\]

For finite \(x\), \(F_u(x)=0\) would force \(u=-1\), which is excluded. Hence

\[
K(x,0)=0\qquad(x\in\mathbf F_p).
\]

Consequently

\[
H(A,A)=1,
\qquad
H(A,x)=H(x,A)=0\quad(x\in\mathbf F_p).
\tag{2.2}
\]

The finite \(p\times p\) block \(H_F\) is therefore itself symmetric, positive semidefinite, and doubly stochastic.

## 3. Exact decomposition \(KK^*=J+\mathcal B_p+D_p+E_p\)

The generic finite off-diagonal value is close to \(1/p\), not \(1/q\). The difference is forced by the isolated infinity block. This is where the dense balancing part of the boundary correction enters.

### Step 3.1: the correct boundary projection

For any state \(z\in S\), write

\[
h_z=1_{\{z\}}-\frac1q1.
\]

All such vectors have

\[
\|h_z\|_2^2=\frac{p}{q^2}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Set

\[
a=\frac{h_A}{\|h_A\|_2}.
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal B_p=P_a
\]

be the orthogonal projection onto \(\mathbf Ca\). In the Markov-kernel matrix convention,

\[
P_a(x,y)=\frac1q a(x)a(y),
\]

so explicitly

\[
\mathcal B_p(x,y)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{p}{q},&x=y=A,\\[4pt]
-\dfrac1q,&\text{exactly one of }x,y\text{ is }A,\\[4pt]
\dfrac1{pq},&x,y\in\mathbf F_p.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Let \(J=\Pi\), so \(J(x,y)=1/q\). Then

\[
Q:=J+\mathcal B_p
\]

has the exact matrix

\[
Q(A,A)=1,
\quad
Q(A,x)=Q(x,A)=0,
\quad
Q(x,x')=\frac1p\quad(x,x'\in\mathbf F_p).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus \(Q\) is the orthogonal projection onto the functions that are constant separately on \(\{A\}\) and on \(\mathbf F_p\).

This is the required stochasticity correction. Keeping only the \((A,A)\) spike would give incorrect row sums. In the notation requested in the question, the coefficient of \(J\) is exactly

\[
\boxed{c_p=1.}
\]

### Step 3.2: diagonal excess

Define

\[
d_p:=\frac{2p}{(p-1)^2}-\frac1p
=\frac{p^2+2p-1}{p(p-1)^2}
=\frac1p+O(p^{-2}),
\]

and let

\[
D_p(x,x')=d_p\,1_{\{x=x'\in\mathbf F_p\}}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\|D_p\|_{2\to2}=d_p=O(p^{-1}).}
\tag{3.5}
\]

### Step 3.3: fluctuation matrix

Define \(E_p\) exactly by

\[
H=J+\mathcal B_p+D_p+E_p.
\tag{3.6}
\]

The infinity row and column of \(E_p\) vanish. For finite states,

\[
E_p(x,x')=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{C_p(x,x')}{m^2}-\dfrac1p,&x\ne x',\\[8pt]
\dfrac{C_p(x,x)-2p}{m^2},&x=x'.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.7}
\]

The banked codegree theorem gives, uniformly for \(p>17\):

- \(C_p(x,x')=p+O(\sqrt p)\) for every finite off-diagonal pair, including the \(x=-5\) or \(x'=-5\) lines after the excluded boundary component is removed;
- \(C_p(x,x)=2p+O(\sqrt p)\) for every finite diagonal point, including \((-5,-5)\).

Also

\[
\frac{p}{(p-1)^2}-\frac1p
=\frac{2p-1}{p(p-1)^2}
=O(p^{-2}).
\]

Therefore every finite entry of \(E_p\) satisfies

\[
\boxed{|E_p(x,x')|\le C p^{-3/2}}
\tag{3.8}
\]

with an absolute constant \(C\).

Because the measure \(\pi\) is uniform, the \(L^2(\pi)\) operator norm is the usual Euclidean matrix norm. The Frobenius estimate gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|E_p\|_{2\to2}
&\le \|E_p\|_{\rm F}\\
&\le \bigl(p^2\cdot C^2p^{-3}\bigr)^{1/2}\\
&\le Cp^{-1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.9}
\]

The crude row-sum estimate gives the same order. Frobenius does **not** improve the exponent beyond \(p^{-1/2}\) from the available entrywise input, but no improvement is needed.

Put

\[
G_p:=D_p+E_p=H-Q.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\|G_p\|_{2\to2}\le Cp^{-1/2}.}
\tag{3.11}
\]

Moreover \(G_p\ge0\). Indeed, on \(\mathbf C1_A\oplus\mathbf C1_{\mathbf F_p}\), \(H\) and \(Q\) both act as the identity, while on finite mean-zero functions \(Q=0\) and \(G_p=H_F\ge0\).

Finally,

\[
TT^*=KK^*-\Pi
=\boxed{P_a+G_p}.
\tag{3.12}
\]

This is the exact Gram decomposition needed for the power argument.

## 4. The unique exceptional singular pair

Let

\[
b=\frac{h_0}{\|h_0\|_2}.
\]

### Step 4.1: exact one-step isometry

**[PROVED]** From \(K(x,0)=1_{\{x=A\}}\),

\[
K h_0=h_A.
\]

Since \(h_0,h_A\) are mean zero,

\[
\boxed{Tb=a.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The vectors \(a,b\) are unit vectors. Since \(T\) is a contraction and \(\|Tb\|=\|b\|=1\), equality in the contraction inequality gives

\[
T^*Tb=b.
\]

Using \(Tb=a\),

\[
\boxed{T^*a=b.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus \(a,b\) are the left and right singular vectors for the unique exceptional singular value \(1\).

Define the rank-one partial isometry

\[
S:=a\otimes b,
\qquad
Sf=a\langle b,f\rangle,
\tag{4.3}
\]

and the bulk operator

\[
N:=T-S.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Then

\[
Nb=0,
\qquad
N^*a=0.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The cross terms in \(TT^*=(S+N)(S+N)^*\) vanish, and \(SS^*=P_a\). Comparing with (3.12),

\[
\boxed{NN^*=G_p.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\|N\|_{2\to2}\le Cp^{-1/4}.}
\tag{4.7}
\]

This is the correct interpretation of the one-step codegree estimate. It does not say \(\|T\|=O(p^{-1/4})\); in fact \(\|T\|=1\). It says that all singular directions other than the explicit restart pair have norm \(O(p^{-1/4})\).

The exceptional operator \(S\) has rank one, while its dynamical carrier is the at-most-two-dimensional span \(\operatorname{span}\{a,b\}\). That span is not invariant, which is exactly why a naive restriction-and-power argument would be invalid.

## 5. Transience on both sides of the singular pair

The forward estimate was already banked with the restart computation. The reverse estimate is the small additional calculation supplied by the diagonal codegree.

### Step 5.1: forward leakage from \(a\)

For finite \(x\), put

\[
r_x=\#\{u\in U:P(u)=x\}.
\]

Since \(P\) is cubic, \(0\le r_x\le3\), and

\[
K(x,A)=\frac{r_x}{m},
\qquad
K(A,A)=0.
\]

Therefore

\[
(Kh_A)(x)=K(x,A)-\frac1q
\]

has absolute value \(O(p^{-1})\) at every state. Hence

\[
\|Kh_A\|_2=O(p^{-1}).
\]

Using \(\|h_A\|_2=\sqrt p/q\),

\[
\boxed{\|Ta\|_2
=\frac{\|Kh_A\|_2}{\|h_A\|_2}
=O(p^{-1/2}).}
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Step 5.2: reverse leakage from \(b\)

Since \(K^*(y,x)=K(x,y)\),

\[
K^*h_0(y)=K(0,y)-\frac1q.
\]

Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|K^*h_0\|_2^2
&=\frac1q\sum_y\left(K(0,y)-\frac1q\right)^2\\
&=\frac1q\left(\sum_yK(0,y)^2-\frac1q\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]

By the exact Gram identity,

\[
\sum_yK(0,y)^2=H(0,0)=\frac{C_p(0,0)}{m^2}.
\]

Since \(0\ne-5\) for \(p>17\), the diagonal codegree theorem gives

\[
C_p(0,0)=2p+O(\sqrt p).
\]

Dividing (5.2) by \(\|h_0\|_2^2=p/q^2\) gives the exact normalized formula

\[
\boxed{
\|T^*b\|_2^2
=\frac{q}{p}\left(\frac{C_p(0,0)}{(p-1)^2}-\frac1q\right)
=O(p^{-1}).}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\|T^*b\|_2=O(p^{-1/2}).}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is the minimal extra computation that turns the rank-one exceptional singular value into a quantitatively transient pair.

### Step 5.3: overlap of the two singular vectors

For distinct singleton states \(0\) and \(A\),

\[
\langle h_0,h_A\rangle=-\frac1{q^2}.
\]

Since both norms squared equal \(p/q^2\),

\[
\boxed{\rho:=\langle b,a\rangle=-\frac1p.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Consequently

\[
S^2=\rho S,
\qquad
\|S^2\|=p^{-1}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Also

\[
Na=Ta-Sa=Ta-\rho a,
\]

so (5.1) and (5.5) give

\[
\boxed{\|Na\|=O(p^{-1/2}).}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Similarly

\[
N^*b=T^*b-S^*b=T^*b-\rho b,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{\|N^*b\|=O(p^{-1/2}).}
\tag{5.8}
\]

## 6. The non-normal power estimate

The generally false inequality

\[
\|K^{2r}\|\le\|KK^*\|^r
\]

is not used. Nor do we claim that \(\operatorname{span}\{a,b\}\) is invariant. Instead we expand the exact partial-isometry decomposition.

### Step 6.1: two-step contraction

**[PROVED]** Since \(T=S+N\),

\[
T^2=S^2+SN+NS+N^2.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The four terms satisfy

\[
\|S^2\|=p^{-1},
\]

\[
SN=a\otimes N^*b,
\qquad
\|SN\|=\|N^*b\|=O(p^{-1/2}),
\]

\[
NS=Na\otimes b,
\qquad
\|NS\|=\|Na\|=O(p^{-1/2}),
\]

and

\[
\|N^2\|\le\|N\|^2=\|NN^*\|=\|G_p\|=O(p^{-1/2}).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\|T^2\|_{2\to2}\le C_2p^{-1/2}.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

This is the technical heart of the bridge.

### Step 6.2: fixed powers

**[PROVED]** For every integer \(r\ge1\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|K^{2r}-\Pi\|_{2\to2}
&=\|T^{2r}\|_{2\to2}\\
&=\|(T^2)^r\|_{2\to2}\\
&\le C_2^r p^{-r/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Similarly, using \(\|T\|\le1\),

\[
\|T^{2r+1}\|\le C_2^r p^{-r/2}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

### Step 6.3: BM\((\theta)\)

Let \(\theta>0\) be fixed and choose

\[
r_\theta=\lceil3+2\theta\rceil,
\qquad
L_\theta=2r_\theta.
\]

Then \(r_\theta/2\ge3/2+\theta\), so (6.3) gives

\[
\boxed{
\|K^{L_\theta}-\Pi\|_{2\to2}
\le C_\theta p^{-3/2-\theta}.}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Since \(L_\theta\) is fixed, it certainly satisfies \(L_\theta\le C_0\log p\) for all sufficiently large \(p\). Hence:

\[
\boxed{\text{BM}(\theta)\text{ holds for every fixed }\theta>0.}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Two useful concrete choices are

\[
L=8,\quad \theta=\frac12,
\qquad
\|K^8-\Pi\|=O(p^{-2}),
\tag{6.7}
\]

and

\[
L=10,\quad \theta=1,
\qquad
\|K^{10}-\Pi\|=O(p^{-5/2}).
\tag{6.8}
\]

The standard point-mass \(L^2\)-to-TV conversion from Q6456 is

\[
d_p(L):=\sup_x\|\delta_xK^L-\pi\|_{\rm TV}
\le\frac12\sqrt p\,\|K^L-\Pi\|_{2\to2}.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Thus

\[
d_p(8)=O(p^{-3/2}),
\qquad
d_p(10)=O(p^{-2}).
\tag{6.10}
\]

This is exactly the \(d_p(L)=O(p^{-1-\theta})\) strength required by the Chen--Stein proof.

## 7. Audit of all proposed exceptional modes

### (a) Infinity/restart pair

**[PROVED]** This is the sole large singular mode. In the Gram operator it is the rank-one projection \(P_a\). In the original operator it is the rank-one partial isometry \(S=a\otimes b\), carried by the two vectors \(b=h_0/\|h_0\|\) and \(a=h_A/\|h_A\|\). It is transient because

\[
b\xmapsto{T}a,
\qquad
\|Ta\|=O(p^{-1/2}),
\]

and the reverse leakage is also \(O(p^{-1/2})\).

### (b) Diagonal excess

**[PROVED]** \(D_p\) is a scalar diagonal operator on the finite coordinates with

\[
\|D_p\|=O(p^{-1}).
\]

It is not an exceptional eigenmode. In the exact positive operator \(G_p=H-Q\), it is absorbed together with \(E_p\), and the whole nonexceptional Gram norm is \(O(p^{-1/2})\).

### (c) The state \(x=-5\)

**[PROVED]** Off the diagonal, the excluded boundary line contributes no allowed \((u,v)\), leaving \(p+O(\sqrt p)\). At \((-5,-5)\), the allowed diagonal plus the allowed residual give \(2p+O(\sqrt p)\). After subtracting the same finite baseline and diagonal excess used elsewhere, the \(-5\) row and column have the same \(O(p^{-3/2})\) residual entry bounds as every other finite row and column. There is no \(-5\) mode.

### (d) Any other mode

**[PROVED]** The banked codegree classification has no other finite off-diagonal component. After removing constants and \(P_a\), every remaining Gram eigenvalue lies in \(G_p\), whose norm is \(O(p^{-1/2})\). For sufficiently large \(p\), no further unit or near-unit exceptional mode exists.

Therefore the exceptional dynamical space has dimension at most two, and its operator is the explicit rank-one transient partial isometry above.

## 8. Unconditional annealed Poisson theorem

### [THEOREM-ANNEALED-APERY-UNCONDITIONAL]

**[PROVED]** Fix \(t>0\). For every sufficiently large prime \(p>17\), let \((X_j)_{j\ge0}\) be the annealed Apéry chain

\[
X_{j+1}=F_{U_j}(X_j),
\]

where the \(U_j\) are independent and uniform on \(\mathbf F_p\setminus\{-1\}\). The initial state \(X_0=x\in\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\) is arbitrary. Put

\[
n_p=\lfloor tp\rfloor,
\qquad
W_p=\sum_{j=0}^{n_p-1}1_{\{X_{10+j}=\infty\}},
\qquad
\lambda_p=\frac{n_p}{p+1}.
\]

Then, uniformly in \(x\),

\[
\boxed{
d_{\rm TV}\bigl(\mathcal L_x(W_p),\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_p)\bigr)
\le \frac{C_t}{p}.}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Furthermore

\[
|\lambda_p-t|\le\frac{t+1}{p+1},
\]

and the elementary Poisson coupling bound gives

\[
d_{\rm TV}(\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_p),\operatorname{Poisson}(t))
\le|\lambda_p-t|.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
d_{\rm TV}\bigl(\mathcal L_x(W_p),\operatorname{Poisson}(t)\bigr)
\le \frac{C_t'}p.}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Thus the strongest exponent supplied by the already-proved quantitative Chen--Stein estimate, together with the present fixed-power bridge, is

\[
\boxed{\eta=1.}
\]

#### Proof

**[PROVED]** Equation (6.8) is BM\((1)\) with the fixed block length \(L=10\). The quantitative theorem already proved in `problems/3.2/chatgpt-answers/Q6456.md` states

\[
d_{\rm TV}\bigl(\mathcal L_x(W_p),\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_p)\bigr)
\le C_t\left(\frac{L}{p}+p^{-\theta}\right).
\]

Substituting \(L=10\) and \(\theta=1\) gives (8.1). The parameter comparison gives (8.2). \(\square\)

The ten-step burn-in is essential only for uniformity over arbitrary starting states. If a visit at time \(0\) were counted and \(X_0=\infty\), one deterministic visit would be present, so a uniform Poisson statement with no burn-in could not be true in that literal form.

### Conservative black-box version

If one uses only the coarse final corollary of Q6456, which records

\[
\eta=\min(\theta,1/2),
\]

then (6.7) supplies BM\((1/2)\) with \(L=8\), and one obtains immediately

\[
d_{\rm TV}=O_t(p^{-1/2}).
\]

There is no contradiction: Q6456 replaced the generic term \(L/p\), with \(L=O(\log p)\), by the coarse power \(p^{-1/2}\). Here \(L\) is actually fixed, so retaining the quantitative bound improves the rate to \(p^{-1}\).
