# Literature audit for the restricted Newton–Pascal block radical

## Executive verdict

I found **no published theorem** that proves the required estimate

```text
log rad_(D,D+N] gcd(
    G_(D-1,N)(C_M),
    binom(D+N,N)
) = o(N)
```

uniformly for the Problem 3.2 regime `D > N`, `N ≍ M^(1/3)`, with the shell nodes lying in one fixed quotient cell.

The new reduction is nevertheless important because it removes every generic-gcd ambiguity. The binomial coefficient is only a squarefree **interval-prime mask**: for each prime `q` in `(D,D+N]`,

```text
v_q binom(D+N,N) = 1,
G_(D-1,N)(C_M) ≡ C_M(q-1) mod q.
```

Thus the remaining problem is exactly a horizontal, prime-varying zero problem for one distinguished Newton evaluation. It is not a denominator problem, not a generic gcd of two unrelated P-recursive sequences, and not a question about the prime factors of the factorial ratio itself.

The two closest published theorem families are:

1. **Rowland–Yassawi, Theorem 5.1:** coefficients of a fixed partial diagonal modulo `p^alpha` form a `p`-automatic multidimensional sequence.
2. **Mellit–Vlasenko, Theorem 1 in the preprint / Theorem 1.1 in the journal version:** Dwork ratio congruences for the origin constant terms of powers of a fixed Laurent polynomial whose Newton polytope has the origin as its unique interior lattice point.

Both are vertical theorems with a fixed characteristic. Neither supplies a bound uniform as the prime itself varies through a moving interval. The minimal missing hypothesis is a **horizontal nonconcentration theorem** for the Cartier/Newton output:

```text
sum_{D<q<=D+N, q prime,
     G_(D-1,N)(C_M) ≡ 0 mod q} log q = o(N),
```

uniformly, or in the weaker total-ledger form required by the block cover. No theorem located in the searched literature has this conclusion under diagonal, p-Lucas, Dwork, Hadamard-grade, factorial-ratio, or Padé hypotheses.

# 1. Exact project interface and hypotheses

The requested base state is commit
[`0c33e35`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/commit/0c33e35a3c740a9e92b3514c3eea30abdb711e4c), especially Sections 48, 54, 56, and 60–64 of
[`Q32_SEPARATION_ANALYSIS.md`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/blob/0c33e35a3c740a9e92b3514c3eea30abdb711e4c/problems/3.2/research/working_notes/Q32_SEPARATION_ANALYSIS.md).

The exact terminal formulation was added immediately afterward in commit
[`7b6abb0`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/commit/7b6abb007172be3c8cea42e4316d7991dcc6e1ad), Section 65.3. For an integer sequence `Y`, define

```text
G_(d,L)(Y)
 = sum_{i=0}^L (-1)^i
     binom(d+i,i) binom(d+L+1,L-i) Y_(d+i).
```

For `D>N>=1`, put

```text
A_(D,N) = G_(D-1,N)(Y),
B_(D,N) = binom(D+N,N),
Q_(D,N) = product of primes q with D<q<=D+N.
```

The project proves the exact identity

```text
gcd(A_(D,N),Q_(D,N))
 = rad_(D,D+N] gcd(A_(D,N),B_(D,N))
 = product_{D<q<=D+N, q | Y_(q-1)} q.
```

The proof is elementary and complete:

- Newton interpolation at `-1` gives `A_(D,N) ≡ Y_(q-1) mod q`.
- Since `q>D>N`, the interval `(D,D+N]` contains exactly one multiple of `q`.
- The denominator `N!` is a `q`-unit.
- Therefore `v_q(B_(D,N))=1`.

For `Y_d=C_M(d)` and a block contained in one quotient-`a` cell, Section 48 gives

```text
q | C_M(q-1)  iff  q | b_(M-a(q-1)).
```

Hence there are no interval-prime nuisance factors. The desired estimate is simply the weighted count of the actual target primes.

Because all such primes satisfy `q ≍ M`, the pointwise estimate is equivalent to

```text
number of target primes in the block = o(N/log M).
```

An averaged theorem over the bounded-overlap block cover with total logarithmic weight `o(M^(1/3))` would also suffice; pointwise `o(N)` is stronger than strictly necessary.

The earlier generic theorem audit is already recorded in
[`Q632`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/blob/0c33e35a3c740a9e92b3514c3eea30abdb711e4c/problems/3.2/chatgpt-answers/Q632.md). I did not repeat its BCZ, Corvaja–Zannier, Levin, Grieve–Wang, Xiao, Vojta, dynamics, or fixed-torus analysis here. This report tests only the narrower theorem classes named in Q5738.

# 3. Finite differences and Newton-series theorems

## 3.1 Congruence-preserving functions

Cégielski, Grigorieff, and Guessarian prove two exact results in
[arXiv:1506.00133](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.00133):

- **Theorem 2.5:** every function from `Z/nZ` to `Z/mZ` has a unique expansion in the binomial/Newton basis `P_k(x)=binom(x,k)`.
- **Theorem 3.2:** such a function is congruence preserving precisely when `lcm(1,...,k)` divides its `k`th Newton coefficient modulo `m`.

This is formally close to the Problem 3.2 Newton language, but the key hypothesis is absent. The paper assumes

```text
x-y divides f(x)-f(y)
```

in the relevant residue-class sense. No such global property is proved for `f(d)=C_M(d)`. The exact shell congruences are characteristic-specific projections at prime nodes, not congruence preservation for arbitrary pairs of nodes.

Even if this hypothesis were added, Theorem 3.2 identifies **forced lcm content** in Newton coefficients. It does not bound the primes for which an evaluated Newton polynomial happens to vanish. In the present reduction the forced Pascal content has already been isolated as `B_(D,N)`; the remaining question is precisely the accidental intersection with `A_(D,N)`.

## 3.2 Closure of diagonals under finite-difference operations

Finite differences, binomial transforms, sections, diagonals, and Hadamard products preserve D-finiteness in the expected fixed-parameter settings. This can provide a rational-diagonal or holonomic presentation of the multi-parameter carrier. It does not provide primitive-content control.

The obstruction is quantitative and horizontal. An exact finite-order recurrence can show that a sufficiently long zero run forces a singular determinant. It does not show that the distinguished solution has few isolated zeros when both the modulus and the evaluated parameter change together as `q=D+i`.

Therefore the missing input is not another closure theorem for diagonals. It is a theorem converting that fixed-complexity presentation into a prime-varying nonconcentration estimate.

# 4. p-Lucas, Cartier, Dwork, and automaticity

## 4.1 Mellit–Vlasenko

Mellit and Vlasenko’s
[Dwork’s congruences for the constant terms of powers of a Laurent polynomial](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.5811)
proves in **Theorem 1** of the preprint, numbered **Theorem 1.1** in the journal version, that if the Newton polytope of a Laurent polynomial has the origin as its only interior integral point, then its origin constant-term generating series satisfies Dwork ratio congruences modulo `p^s`.

The project’s Apéry Laurent polynomial satisfies the unique-interior-point hypothesis, so the theorem applies to

```text
b_m = CT Lambda^m.
```

It does not directly apply to

```text
C_M(d) = sum over kappa of [X^(d kappa)] Lambda^M,
```

because the exponent `M` is fixed and the selected off-origin lattice changes with `d`. A roots-of-unity filter represents `C_M(d)`, but its order and coefficient field depend on `d`; it is not the fixed origin coefficient sequence of Mellit–Vlasenko.

More importantly, the theorem fixes a prime `p` and controls a vertical tower in the exponent. The target problem asks for a weighted count over many different primes `q` in a moving interval. The theorem gives no uniform bound on how often the first Cartier digit can vanish as `q` varies.

## 4.2 Delaygue

Delaygue’s
[Arithmetic properties of Apéry-like numbers](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.4131), published in *Compositio Mathematica* 154 (2018), contains the closest general p-Lucas and valuation results:

- **Theorems 1 and 2:** lower bounds for p-adic valuations of suitable factorial-ratio multisums and of constant terms of powers of Laurent polynomials, under the paper’s admissibility and differential-operator hypotheses.
- **Theorem 3:** an effective Landau-function criterion for factorial-ratio sequences to satisfy p-Lucas congruences for almost all primes.
- **Proposition 2:** a multisum p-Lucas criterion under the corresponding balancing and admissibility assumptions.

These theorems explain why ordinary Apéry-type arrays have strong vertical divisibility. They do not apply to the signed, moving-coefficient sum defining `G_(D-1,N)(C_M)` as one fixed factorial-ratio sequence. Decomposing `G` into factorial-ratio pieces does not help: lower valuation bounds for pieces do not bound cancellation or the number of primes dividing their sum.

The direction is also opposite to the desired estimate. Delaygue proves that certain digit patterns force large p-adic valuation. We need to prove that the set of characteristics producing even one zero digit has small total logarithmic weight.

## 4.3 Rowland–Yassawi and automatic zero sets

Rowland and Yassawi’s
[Automatic congruences for diagonals of rational functions](https://doi.org/10.5802/jtnb.901)
contains two exact candidates:

- **Theorem 5.1:** coefficients of a fixed partial diagonal of a rational function over `Z_p`, modulo `p^alpha`, form a p-automatic multidimensional sequence.
- **Theorem 5.2:** a Lucas product follows for coefficients of `Q^(-1/s)` when `p=1 mod s`, `Q(0)=1`, and all relevant Cartier images of `Q^((p-1)/s)` are constant modulo `p`.

Adamczewski and Bell’s
[Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.4091)
gives the related statement that the zero set of coefficients of a multivariate algebraic series over a field of characteristic `p` is p-automatic. Derksen’s theorem gives automaticity for zero sets of constant-coefficient recurrences in characteristic `p`.

These results remain the closest published structural tools, but the gap is exact:

1. They fix `p`.
2. The automaton and its state set may depend substantially on `p`.
3. They give a description or decision procedure for zeros inside one characteristic.
4. They give no bound uniform over the varying primes `q` in `(D,D+N]`.

A p-automatic set may be large or highly structured. Automaticity by itself does not imply that among `N` characteristics near `M` only `o(N/log M)` produce a zero.

A usable strengthening would need a **p-independent bounded Cartier state space plus a nonzero output polynomial of bounded degree in the moving residue**, or an equivalent uniform root bound. None of Theorems 5.1, 5.2, Adamczewski–Bell 1.4, or Derksen supplies this.

## 4.4 Lucas congruences modulo p squared

Rowland, Yassawi, and Krattenthaler’s
[Lucas congruences for the Apéry numbers modulo p squared](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04801)
proves in **Theorem 2** that

```text
A(d+pn) ≡ A(d) A(n) mod p^2 for every n
```

holds exactly for digits `d` in a prime-dependent set `D(p)`. Their **Theorems 6–8** restate and extend Gessel’s modulo-`p`, modulo-`p^3`, and first-derivative formulas.

This is stronger than ordinary p-Lucas, but it still concerns the origin Apéry sequence. The set `D(p)` itself varies with `p`, and the theorem does not identify the first Cartier digit of the matched shell with a fixed function whose prime zeros can be counted. The project’s Q5731 counterexample at `(n,p)=(20,17)` already shows that depth two of the Apéry target does not force depth two of the matched shell.

# 5. Diagonal and Hadamard grade

Harder and Kramer-Miller’s 2026 paper
[The Diagonal and Hadamard Grade of Hypergeometric Functions](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnag040)
proves:

- **Theorem 3.3:** the indicated nonresonant hypergeometric functions have diagonal and Hadamard grade equal to their rank.
- **Example 3.9:** the Apéry generating function has diagonal grade exactly 3; Straub’s four-variable rational representation is minimal.
- **Proposition 4.1:** diagonal grades add subadditively under Hadamard product.

These statements are useful for ruling out an unrealistically low-dimensional diagonal representation. They do not control integral content. Diagonal grade measures the minimum geometric/monodromy complexity of a representation, not the gcd of coefficients, the number of zero reductions, or the primitive content of a Padé/Newton row.

In particular, “grade 3” does not imply a rank-three lattice with a unit determinant at every prime. Q5727 and Q5731 show that the actual local observation lattice can still have one unsaturated target scalar even when the ambient holonomic rank is bounded.

# 6. Factorial ratios and binomial gcd theorems

## 6.1 The factorial side is already completely known

For `q` in `(D,D+N]` with `D>N`, Kummer’s carry theorem—or the direct numerator-interval argument—gives

```text
v_q binom(D+N,N) = 1.
```

Thus there is no unknown factorial-ratio radical to estimate. The binomial coefficient contains every candidate interval prime exactly once and acts as a deterministic sieve mask.

## 6.2 Published gcds of binomial families do not address the cross gcd

For example, Hong’s 2016 paper
[The greatest common divisor of certain binomial coefficients](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crma.2016.06.001)
proves the exact identity

```text
gcd { binom(mn,k) : 1<=k<=mn, gcd(k,m)=1 }
 = m times an explicit product over primes dividing gcd(m,n).
```

This and related Pascal-row gcd identities compute gcds *within a binomial family*. They provide no relation between `binom(D+N,N)` and the distinguished number `G_(D-1,N)(C_M)`.

Recent binomial gcd criteria have the same limitation. They refine the known support and valuation of factorial ratios, while the missing issue is the correlation

```text
q | binom(D+N,N)  and  q | G_(D-1,N)(C_M).
```

The first condition is automatic for all interval primes; only the second carries arithmetic information.

# 7. G-function denominators, Padé content, and the classical Apéry sources

## 7.1 G-function denominator theorems

Fischler and Rivoal’s
[On the denominators of the Taylor coefficients of G-functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.00706)
shows, conditionally for general G-operators and unconditionally for geometric operators, that the common denominator of the first `n` coefficients divides a quantity of the form

```text
D_(bn)^s C^(n+1).
```

The shell and Newton values in the present interface are integers. Denominator clearing is therefore vacuous. The theorem has no numerator-content conclusion.

## 7.2 Zudilin and classical Padé approximants

Zudilin’s
[An elementary proof of Apéry’s theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0202159)
proves in **Lemma 1** that his linear form has the shape

```text
F_n = u_n zeta(3) - v_n,
u_n is integral,
D_n^3 v_n is integral.
```

This is exactly an integrality/denominator statement. It does not assert that the cleared pair is primitive, that its gcd is small, or that its gcd has small radical in an interval of primes. The same is true of the standard Beukers integral constructions and later denominator improvements used in irrationality proofs.

Moreover, `G_(D-1,N)(C_M)` is not one of the classical numerator or denominator sequences of the Apéry approximants. A content lemma for those approximants would require an additional integral map to the fixed-moment shell carrier.

## 7.3 Beukers, Peters, Stienstra

The classical geometric sources are:

- Beukers–Peters, [A family of K3 surfaces and zeta(3)](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1984.351.42).
- Beukers–Stienstra, [On the Picard–Fuchs equation and the formal Brauer group of certain elliptic K3-surfaces](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01455990).
- Peters–Stienstra, [A pencil of K3 surfaces related to Apéry’s recurrence](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0095972).
- Beukers, [Some congruences for the Apéry numbers](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(85)90047-2) and [Another congruence for the Apéry numbers](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(87)90025-4).

Beukers–Stienstra **Theorem 13.1**, repeatedly cited in the later supercongruence literature, is a formal-group congruence along prime-power towers. These papers identify the Picard–Fuchs equation, formal Brauer group, modular structure, and vertical Frobenius congruences. I found no theorem in them asserting primitive content for a Newton interpolation row, a gcd between a period coefficient and a Pascal coefficient, or a horizontal bound on primes in a moving interval.

## 7.4 Ahlgren–Ono

Ahlgren and Ono’s
[A Gaussian hypergeometric series evaluation and Apéry number congruences](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.2000.004)
proves in **Theorem 5** that for every odd prime `p`,

```text
A((p-1)/2) ≡ a(p) mod p^2,
```

where `a(p)` is a coefficient of a fixed eta-product.

This is a strong modular identification at one special Apéry index. It does not cover the moving residual index `M-a(q-1)`, the fixed-moment shell, or the block Newton carrier. Even if a modular form were found for each shell value, a separate theorem bounding zeros of its Fourier coefficients at primes in the required moving interval would still be needed.

### Classical-source conclusion

I found **no hidden primitive-content lemma** in the Apéry/Beukers/Peters/Stienstra/Ahlgren–Ono/Zudilin chain. The available arithmetic statements are integrality, denominator bounds, formal-group congruences, special-index supercongruences, or modular identifications—not a restricted interval-prime gcd estimate.

# 8. Closest exact theorem and the minimal missing hypothesis

## 8.1 Closest exact published theorem

The closest theorem at the level of the actual array is Rowland–Yassawi **Theorem 5.1**:

> A fixed partial diagonal of a rational function, reduced modulo a fixed prime power, is a finite-state multidimensional sequence.

The closest theorem at the level of p-adic propagation is Mellit–Vlasenko **Theorem 1 / 1.1**:

> Origin constant terms of powers of a suitable fixed Laurent polynomial satisfy Dwork ratio congruences through all powers of a fixed prime.

Together they strongly suggest that the shell carrier has a finite Cartier description in each characteristic. They do not imply any useful uniformity as the characteristic varies.

## 8.2 Exact missing hypothesis

A theorem sufficient for the current route can be stated without any generic gcd terminology.

### Horizontal Cartier–Newton nonconcentration hypothesis

For the fixed Apéry Laurent polynomial, let `C_M(d)` be its fixed-moment shell and let

```text
A(M,D,N) = G_(D-1,N)(C_M).
```

Assume `D>N`, `N=M^(1/3)+O(1)`, and `[D,D+N-1]` lies in one quotient cell. Then require

```text
sum_{D<q<=D+N, q prime, A(M,D,N)=0 mod q} log q = o(N)
```

uniformly, or require the corresponding sum over the bounded-overlap block cover to be `o(M^(1/3))`.

By the exact Newton–Pascal identity, this hypothesis is **equivalent** to the needed restricted-radical estimate. It is the minimal missing arithmetic statement.

A more structural sufficient hypothesis would be:

1. one fixed-complexity rational-diagonal/Cartier representation for `A(M,D,N)` in the parameters;
2. a Cartier orbit of rank bounded independently of `q`;
3. after primitive saturation, a nonzero output polynomial in the moving residue with degree bounded independently of `q`;
4. coefficient heights whose total over the block are `o(N)`.

Existing automaticity theorems give item 1 in favorable fixed representations and finite state for each fixed `q`, but not items 2–4 uniformly in `q`. Existing Dwork theorems give vertical Frobenius compatibility, but not the horizontal output-polynomial nonvanishing.

## 8.3 Why a per-pair or per-prime certificate still fails

Even a certificate of logarithmic height `O((log M)^2)` for each realized short pair is too expensive over approximately `M^(1/3)/log M` candidate cores. Its total height is at least of order

```text
M^(1/3) log M,
```

whereas the proof requires `o(M^(1/3))`. The missing result must reuse one block-level object or prove a genuinely averaged horizontal zero-density theorem.

# 9. Final assessment

The exact interface has reached a natural boundary of the current literature:

- **Factorial-ratio theory** identifies the candidate primes but does not correlate them with the Newton value.
- **Finite-difference theory** identifies universal lcm/Smith content but does not bound accidental zeros.
- **p-Lucas and Dwork theory** controls vertical digits for a fixed characteristic.
- **Automaticity** describes zero sets for a fixed characteristic.
- **Diagonal/Hadamard grade** measures representation complexity, not primitive content.
- **G-function and Padé results** control denominators and integrality, not numerator gcds.
- **Classical Apéry geometry and modularity** supplies special congruences but no horizontal interval-prime content lemma.

Therefore the honest verdict is:

> No standard or published theorem located in these categories proves `log restricted-radical = o(N)`. The closest exact theorem is fixed-prime automaticity of partial diagonals. The minimal missing hypothesis is uniform horizontal nonconcentration of the primitive Cartier/Newton output as the prime varies through the short interval.

That missing theorem is substantially narrower than a general holonomic gcd theorem, but it appears to be genuinely new.
