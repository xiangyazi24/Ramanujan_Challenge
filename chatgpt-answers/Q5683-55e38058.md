# Q5683 — pointwise spike transport for Apéry P3.2

## Verdict

There is no weight-preserving transport mechanism obtainable from the currently proved primewise symmetries alone. In fact there is a sharp no-go theorem.

For a fixed spike index `n`, the closure of one target prime `p` under p-Lucas repetition and reflection consists only of the two residue classes

`m ≡ n (mod p)` and `m ≡ -n-1 (mod p)`.

Consequently, for every other proposed index `m`, all spike primes transported to `m` divide the single quadratic carrier

`(m-n)(m+n+1)`.

If `m = O(n)`, that carrier has logarithmic height `O(log n)`. Thus it can contain only `O(log n)` total logarithmic target weight—indeed at most four primes `p > sqrt(N)`, and at most two top-half primes. This remains true even if `m` is chosen after seeing the entire spike, all folded indices, all slacks, and all branches.

The nearby reflected shifts therefore preserve the spike weight only after fragmenting it across essentially one distinct index per prime. They do not create another exceptional index. Additive energy of the shift set is unlabeled and does not create common prime incidences. A polynomial map of `n` is even more directly blocked by the same quadratic carrier. Gap continuants control an additional zero if one exists, but do not force one; the two-point reflection orbit is already closed.

So the correct answer is a no-go under the known symmetries. A successful transport theorem must add a genuinely new, cross-prime or coherent-third-zero relation. The exact sufficient form is a batch transport that moves a positive fraction of the spike weight into a family of common shifts whose total displacement is `o(N/log N)`. The codegree theorem would then give an immediate contradiction.

## 1. Spike notation

Fix `n` in `(N,2N]`. Let

`T(n) = { p > sqrt(N) : p <= n and p | b_(n mod p) }`

and let

`W(n) = sum_{p in T(n)} log p`.

The current reduction gives, after the already controlled small-prime and companion terms, that a spike `log G_n > epsilon n` forces

`#T(n) >= c_epsilon N/log N`

and therefore

`W(n) >= (1/2 log N) #T(n) >= eta_epsilon N`.

Thus the transport problem is correctly viewed as preserving a linear logarithmic weight, not merely moving one prime.

For `p in T(n)`, write

`r_p = n mod p`,

`j_p = min(r_p, p-1-r_p)`,

`c_p = p-2j_p`.

Then `c_p` is a positive odd integer and `p | b_(j_p)`. There are two branches:

- direct branch: `r_p = j_p`;
- reflected branch: `r_p = p-1-j_p`.

## 2. Exact symmetry closure of one target

### Lemma 2.1 — two-class closure

Starting from the fact `p | b_(n mod p)`, every lower-digit target at another index `m` that is forced solely by repeated use of p-Lucas and reflection lies in exactly one of the two congruence classes

`m ≡ n (mod p)`

or

`m ≡ -n-1 (mod p)`.

Conversely, each of these two congruences forces `p | b_(m mod p)`.

### Proof

The same-residue p-Lucas lift preserves the lower digit, hence gives `m ≡ n (mod p)`. Reflection replaces a residue `r` by `p-1-r`, hence gives

`m ≡ p-1-(n mod p) ≡ -n-1 (mod p)`.

Reflection is an involution and same-residue lifting does not alter the residue, so iterating these operations creates no third class. Conversely, either congruence makes `m mod p` equal to the original zero residue or its reflected zero residue. ∎

This closure statement already includes arbitrary quotient changes in the p-Lucas lift. It is not limited to the nearest reflected representative.

It is useful to name the forced orbit in a range `[0,X]`:

`O_p(n;X) = { m in [0,X] : m ≡ n or -n-1 (mod p) }`.

Any additional residue at which `b_r ≡ 0 (mod p)` is genuinely new information. It is not generated by the known symmetry closure.

## 3. The quadratic transport carrier

### Theorem 3.1 — single-index transport no-go

Let `S` be any subset of `T(n)`, and let `m != n` be a nonnegative integer such that every `p in S` is transported from `n` to `m` by the closure in Lemma 2.1. Then

`prod_{p in S} p | (m-n)(m+n+1)`.

In particular, if `0 <= m <= C N`, then

`sum_{p in S} log p <= log |m-n| + log(m+n+1) <= 2 log((C+3)N)`.

Hence the transported logarithmic weight at one nontrivial `O(n)` index is `O(log N)`.

### Proof

Partition `S = S_+ union S_-`, where

- `p in S_+` means `m ≡ n (mod p)`;
- `p in S_-` means `m ≡ -n-1 (mod p)`.

Then

`prod_{p in S_+} p | m-n`

and

`prod_{p in S_-} p | m+n+1`.

The two products are coprime because they use disjoint prime sets, so their product divides `(m-n)(m+n+1)`. The height estimate follows immediately. ∎

### Corollary 3.2 — bounded multiplicity

For fixed `C` and sufficiently large `N`, a nontrivial index `m in [0,CN]` can receive through Lucas/reflection at most

- four primes from `T(n)` when all primes exceed `sqrt(N)`;
- two primes when all primes exceed `N/2`.

Indeed, each of the two linear factors is `O(N)`. It contains at most two prime divisors exceeding `sqrt(N)`, and at most one prime divisor exceeding `N/2`.

This count bound is stronger than needed; the logarithmic-weight bound is the invariant statement.

### Corollary 3.3 — exact CRT size obstruction

Suppose a subproduct `P = prod_{p in S} p` has `log P >= eta N`, and one tries to choose one common Lucas/reflection lift `m` for all `p in S`, allowing the branch sign to depend adversarially on `p`. Then

`P | (m-n)(m+n+1)`.

Therefore at least one factor has absolute value at least `sqrt(P)`, and

`m >= exp(eta N/2) - O(N)`

unless `m=n` is the trivial same-residue solution for every prime.

Thus the statement “CRT lifts are too large” has an exact exponential form: a positive-weight spike cannot have a nontrivial common symmetry lift of size `O(n)`, or even of polynomial size.

## 4. The nearby reflected shift is essentially injective

For each `p in T(n)`, let `rho_p(n)` be the reflected representative in the same p-block. A direct calculation gives

- direct branch: `rho_p(n) = n + (c_p-1)`;
- reflected branch: `rho_p(n) = n - (c_p-1)`.

In both cases

`rho_p(n) + n + 1 = (2a_p+1)p`,

where `n = a_p p + r_p`. Hence `p | rho_p(n)+n+1`.

### Proposition 4.1 — reflected-shift fiber bound

For any fixed reflected image `m`,

`prod_{p in T(n): rho_p(n)=m} p | m+n+1`.

Consequently a reflected-image fiber has

- at most two primes `p > sqrt(N)`;
- at most one prime `p > N/2`;
- total logarithmic weight at most `log((C+3)N)` when `m=O(N)`.

This is the first exact failure of the proposed many-to-one slack collision argument. In the top-half channel, distinct target primes have distinct nearby reflected images, apart from the central fixed point `c_p=1`, which produces no new index at all. In the `p > sqrt(N)` channel the multiplicity is still at most two.

### Fixed-slack version

The same calculation also recovers the Section 41 carrier with its exact quotient:

- direct branch:
  `2n+c = (2a+1)p`;
- reflected branch:
  `2n+2-c = (2a+1)p`.

Thus for a fixed slack `c`, the direct-branch target product divides `2n+c`, the reflected-branch product divides `2n+2-c`, and the branch-blind product divides

`(2n+c)(2n+2-c)`.

Therefore each slack contributes only `O(log N)` weight. A linear spike necessarily uses

`Omega(N/log N)`

distinct slack/branch coordinates. The “adversarial distinct slacks” scenario is not a removable corner case; after the boundary carriers are discarded, it is the only possible spike geometry.

## 5. Why the proposed aggregation mechanisms fail

### 5.1 Many-to-one slack collisions

They fail at Proposition 4.1. Equal nearby reflected shifts force all colliding primes into one linear integer `m+n+1`. Its size is `O(N)`, so it cannot contain a positive-weight subproduct.

Allowing another representative of the same reflected residue does not help. Every common representative still satisfies `m ≡ -n-1 (mod p)` for all transported primes and is therefore covered by Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.3.

### 5.2 Additive energy

Let

`h_p = rho_p(n)-n = plus_or_minus(c_p-1)`.

A set of `K` distinct shifts in an interval of length `O(N)` has large additive energy simply by density. But an additive relation

`h_p + h_q = h_r + h_s`

contains no common characteristic. The codegree theorem counts primes that label two columns; additive energy counts equal sums of unlabeled column positions. There is no implication from one to the other.

The failure can be made exact. For a hypothetical spike set `T(n)`, define its minimal symmetry closure by

`T_star(m) = { p in T(n) : m ≡ n or -n-1 (mod p) }`.

For two distinct indices `m,m'`, every common label in `T_star(m) cap T_star(m')` divides

`(m-m')(m+m'+1)`.

Indeed, the two signs relative to `n` are either equal, giving `p | m-m'`, or opposite, giving `p | m+m'+1`. Therefore every pair of distinct `O(N)` columns in the minimal closure has only `O(log N)` common target weight and at most four common primes `p > sqrt(N)`.

The shift set can simultaneously have very large additive energy. Hence additive energy alone cannot imply a high-codegree pair or another spike. A new identity must attach prime labels to the additive relations.

### 5.3 A polynomial map of `n`

Let `m = F(n)` be any integer-valued output with `F(n)=O(n)`. It may be fixed in advance or chosen after inspecting all target primes and slacks. If the only proof that a spike prime remains bad at `F(n)` is Lucas/reflection, then

`prod transported p | (F(n)-n)(F(n)+n+1)`.

Thus the transported weight is `O(log n)`. More generally, if `F(n)=O(n^d)`, the weight is only `O(d log n)`.

This also covers a polynomial use of the reflection invariant `lambda=x(x+1)`. The congruence

`F(n)(F(n)+1) ≡ n(n+1) (mod p)`

factors as

`(F(n)-n)(F(n)+n+1) ≡ 0 (mod p)`.

The fiber of the quadratic invariant has exactly the two already known branches. It does not produce a third coherent image.

A family of `L` polynomial outputs can force at most `O(L log N)` total spike-prime incidence weight after summing over outputs, and no individual output is heavy. Covering a linear spike weight therefore needs `L = Omega(N/log N)` outputs, which recreates the one-prime-per-index fragmentation rather than contradicting the exceptional-set theorem.

### 5.4 Repeated Lucas/reflection operations

Iteration does not help because Lemma 2.1 describes the complete orbit. Once a prime is in one of the two residue classes, another reflection merely swaps the classes and another Lucas lift changes only the quotient. There is no orbit growth beyond the two residue classes modulo `p`.

### 5.5 Gap continuants

The gap continuant is conditional:

- if a third, non-reflected zero exists in the same characteristic, the relevant continuant vanishes modulo `p`;
- it does not assert that such a zero exists.

Starting with one target and its forced reflection, the known symmetry orbit is already complete. A minimal configuration with no additional zero is fully compatible with all currently used transport implications. Thus continuants can bound or certify a proposed extra zero locus, but cannot create the extra zero needed for transport.

A successful continuant route would first have to prove that a positive-weight subset of spike primes possesses additional zero residues whose displacements lie in a small common set. That coherence theorem, not the continuant calculation after it, is the missing input.

## 6. The codegree transport budget

The unconditional theorem in Section 42 can be converted into an exact budget for any proposed family of transported shifts.

Let `H` be a finite set of nonzero shifts such that `n+h` remains in a comparable dyadic range. Define the portion of the original spike that appears at at least one shifted index by

`T_H(n) = { p in T(n) : p is also a target at n+h for some h in H }`.

### Theorem 6.1 — transport budget

There is an absolute constant `C_cd` from the codegree theorem such that

`sum_{p in T_H(n)} log p <= C_cd log(4N) sum_{h in H} |h|`.

### Proof

The union weight is at most the sum of the intersection weights:

`sum_{p in T_H(n)} log p`

`<= sum_{h in H} sum_{p in T(n) cap T(n+h)} log p`.

For each `h`, the codegree theorem gives

`#(T(n) cap T(n+h)) <= C_cd |h|`.

Every relevant prime is at most `O(N)`, so the inner logarithmic weight is at most `C_cd |h| log(4N)`. Summing over `h` proves the claim. ∎

This gives the sharp threshold for a useful transport statement.

```text
Batch-transport criterion

Assume W(n) >= eta N.
Find nonzero common shifts H_n and subsets
    S_h subset T(n) cap T(n+h)
such that the union of the S_h carries at least delta W(n).

Then a spike is impossible whenever
    sum_{h in H_n} |h| = o(N/log N).

In particular, if there are L shifts with |h| <= H0,
then the sufficient condition is
    L * H0 = o(N/log N).
```

For one shifted index, it is enough to transport a positive fraction of the spike to a displacement

`|h| = o(N/log N)`.

For several shifted indices, the relevant complexity is not merely their number but their total displacement.

The known reflected shifts are on the wrong side of this threshold. After Section 41 removes small slacks, the unresolved core has `c_p > N/(log N)^2`. A spike with `K = Omega(N/log N)` distinct target primes then has reflected transport cost at least

`sum_p |h_p| = sum_p (c_p-1) = Omega(N^2/(log N)^3)`.

This is vastly larger than `N/log N`, so the codegree budget is not threatened. The transport is exact but far too dispersed.

## 7. A sharp logical no-go model

The preceding inequalities can be summarized by an anchored-star model built from the actual hypothetical spike set itself.

Fix `n` and its target set `T(n)`. On all indices `m` in `[0,CN]`, retain only the incidences forced by Lucas and reflection:

```text
p is marked bad at m
    iff
m ≡ n (mod p) or m ≡ -n-1 (mod p).
```

Then:

1. `n` retains the full spike.
2. Every p-Lucas lift and every reflection consequence is present.
3. Repeated symmetry operations add nothing further.
4. Every other individual index has only `O(log N)` marked logarithmic weight.
5. Every pair of distinct indices has only `O(log N)` common marked weight and `O(1)` common primes `p > sqrt(N)`.
6. Hence the codegree theorem is satisfied, in fact with a stronger `O(1)` bound for this marked subsystem.
7. The exceptional set consists of the single anchored spike.

This is not asserted to be a new Apéry sequence. It is a logical minimal completion of the information forced by the currently known symmetries. It proves that no monotone argument using only those forced incidences can exclude an isolated spike: all its hypotheses hold while its desired transport conclusion fails.

The gap-continuant theorem does not invalidate this model. It constrains an additional zero if one is introduced; the model introduces none beyond the actual reflected partner. Thus the first missing statement is precisely an existence/coherence theorem for non-reflected zeros or another cross-prime relation.

## 8. Exactly what extra relation would suffice

There are two clean sufficient forms.

### 8.1 Common-prime batch compression

It would suffice to prove the following Apéry-specific statement.

For every spike `n`, there exists a set of nonzero shifts `H_n` and a positive-weight subset of the spike primes such that each selected prime remains a lower-digit target at one of the indices `n+h`, and

`sum_{h in H_n} |h| = o(N/log N)`.

Equivalently, a map assigning a positive fraction of target primes to `L` common displacements of size at most `H0` is enough whenever

`L H0 = o(N/log N)`.

The codegree theorem then contradicts the spike immediately by Theorem 6.1.

A concrete special case would be a coherent third-zero law: for a positive fraction of the spike weight,

`p | b_(n mod p)  =>  p | b_((n+h) mod p)`

for one common nonzero `h = o(N/log N)`, or for one of a small family of such shifts. Reflection is not such a law because its shift is `plus_or_minus(c_p-1)` and therefore varies essentially injectively with `p`.

### 8.2 Local persistence of the full bad weight

To use the exceptional-set theorem rather than codegree, it would suffice to prove that a spike at `n` forces more than `C_epsilon (log N)^2` distinct comparable indices `m` with

`log G_m > epsilon' m`

for some fixed `epsilon'>0`.

For example, a local stability theorem of the form

`log G_(n+h) >= log G_n - o(N)`

for all `1 <= |h| <= (log N)^3`

would be more than enough. But the current adjacent-denominator analysis shows that such stability is essentially equivalent to the missing channel rather than an independent consequence of the recurrence.

### What the new relation must not be

It cannot be merely:

- another choice of CRT representatives;
- a statistic of the slack multiset;
- additive energy of reflected locations;
- a polynomial in the quadratic reflection invariant;
- a same-characteristic continuant conditional on an unproved extra zero.

All of these remain inside the two-class primewise closure and are blocked by Theorem 3.1.

The missing relation must couple different target primes or force coherent additional zero positions. In practical terms, it should produce one of:

- a large common-prime overlap at a small displacement;
- a small family of displacements with total displacement `o(N/log N)`;
- many genuinely heavy neighboring columns;
- or a target-blind nonzero integer of `o(N)` logarithmic height divisible by a positive-weight subproduct of the spike primes.

The last alternative would bypass transport entirely and directly contradict the spike radical.

## 9. Final conclusion

The known Apéry symmetries do preserve every target prime, but only along a prime-dependent two-class orbit. Their union preserves total weight; no individual new index preserves more than `O(log n)` weight. For reflected nearby representatives the map is essentially injective, and in the unresolved large-slack core its total displacement is enormous. Additive energy does not change the prime labels, and a polynomial map is trapped by the exact factorization

`m(m+1)-n(n+1) = (m-n)(m+n+1)`.

Therefore the current codegree and exceptional-set theorems are fully compatible with an isolated spike. There is no valid transport contradiction from p-Lucas, reflection, slack collisions, additive energy, or gap continuants alone.

The precise next theorem to seek is a coherent batch relation outside the two-class closure—most sharply, a positive-weight common-prime transport with total displacement `o(N/log N)`. Without such a relation, isolated spikes remain logically possible under all presently proved symmetries.
