4. The raw series \(\sum_n u_nw_n^{(0)}\) has a useful but different meaning:

   \[
   c_0(u)=0
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   \sum_{n\ge0}u_nw_n^{(0)}
   \text{ converges}.
   \]

   If \(c_0(u)\ne0\), its partial sums grow linearly, up to logarithmic corrections. When it converges, however, its value is generally **not zero**. 5. The Casoratian satisfies the exact formula

   with the known Pochhammer product \(h_N\). This determines the **determinant line** \(\bigwedge^3V\), not the particular recessive plane in \(V\). It therefore does not select \(c_0=0\).

6. There is already a genuine creative-telescoping/WZ construction for the inner AESZ/Zudilin sequence

   \[
   b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{n}\binom{n+2k}{n}

The missing finite certificate is an exact Kummer twist and Ore intertwiner from this source module to P2.7, or a new two-variable telescoping kernel whose telescoper is \(L_{27}\) itself.

# 1. First correct the recurrence and adjoint indexing

The project recurrence is

\[
 u_{n+1}
 =\frac{B(n)}{A(n)}u_n
 -\frac{C(n-1)}{A(n-1)}u_{n-1}
 +\frac{D(n-2)}{A(n-2)}u_{n-2},
 \qquad n\ge2.
\tag{1.1}
\]

It is **not** equivalent to

\[
A(n)u_{n+3}=B(n)u_{n+2}-C(n-1)u_{n+1}+D(n-2)u_n.
\]

Multiplying (1.1) by one copy of \(A(n)\) cannot clear the three differently shifted denominators. The nonnegative-shift operator corresponding to the actual project recurrence is

\[
\boxed{
L
=S^3-\frac{B(n+2)}{A(n+2)}S^2
+\frac{C(n+1)}{A(n+1)}S
-\frac{D(n)}{A(n)}.
}
\tag{1.2}
\]

The formal backward adjoint acts by

\[
\boxed{
A(n)v_{n-3}-B(n)v_{n-2}+C(n)v_{n-1}-D(n)v_n=0.
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

For Miller backward iteration it is more convenient to shift to a forward-indexed adjoint \(w\):

\[
\boxed{
A(n+2)w_n-B(n+2)w_{n+1}
+C(n+2)w_{n+2}-D(n+2)w_{n+3}=0.
}
\tag{1.4}
\]

Equivalently, with

\[
\alpha_n=\frac{B(n)}{A(n)},\qquad
\beta_n=-\frac{C(n-1)}{A(n-1)},\qquad
\gamma_n=\frac{D(n-2)}{A(n-2)},
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
w_n=\alpha_{n+2}w_{n+1}
+\beta_{n+3}w_{n+2}
+\gamma_{n+4}w_{n+3}.}
\tag{1.5}
\]

The actual forward characteristic root is

\[
\rho_0=\frac{\nu_0}{64}=0.8588077359\ldots,
\]

not exactly \(55/64\). The number \(55/64\) is the limiting coefficient of \(u_n\) in the monic recurrence. The slow adjoint multiplier is

\[
\rho_0^{-1}=\frac{64}{\nu_0}=1.1644\ldots .
\]

It is the **recessive adjoint mode** relative to the other adjoint multipliers, whose moduli are about \(949\).

# 2. The exact Lagrange bracket

For the project normalization, the conserved concomitant is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
J_n(w,u)={}&\frac{D(n)}{A(n)}w_{n+1}u_n\\
&+\left(
-\frac{C(n+1)}{A(n+1)}w_{n+1}
+\frac{D(n+1)}{A(n+1)}w_{n+2}
\right)u_{n+1}
+w_nu_{n+2}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{2.1}
\]

A direct expansion gives the Green identity

\[
J_{n+1}(w,u)-J_n(w,u)
=w_{n+1}(Lu)_n-u_{n+2}(L^\vee w)_n.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Hence \(J_n\) is independent of \(n\) when both equations are homogeneous.

At \(n=0\),

\[
\boxed{
J_0(w,u)=
\frac{D(0)}{A(0)}w_1u_0
+\left(
-\frac{C(1)}{A(1)}w_1
+\frac{D(1)}{A(1)}w_2
\right)u_1
+w_0u_2.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Thus the recessive initial hyperplane is explicit **once** the projective triple

\[
(w_0^{(0)}:w_1^{(0)}:w_2^{(0)})
\]

is known exactly. Its covector is

\[
\ell_\infty=
\left(
\frac{D(0)}{A(0)}w_1^{(0)},
-\frac{C(1)}{A(1)}w_1^{(0)}
+\frac{D(1)}{A(1)}w_2^{(0)},
 w_0^{(0)}
\right).
\tag{2.4}
\]

For a Birkhoff basis \(y^{(0)},y^{(+)},y^{(-)}\), choose \(w^{(0)}\) so that

\[
J(w^{(0)},y^{(+)})=J(w^{(0)},y^{(-)})=0,
\qquad
J(w^{(0)},y^{(0)})=\kappa\ne0.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{J(w^{(0)},u)=\kappa c_0(u).}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
c_0(e)=0
\iff
J(w^{(0)},p)
=(\zeta(2)+\zeta(3))J(w^{(0)},q).}
\tag{2.6}
\]

This is already the sharpest scalar formulation. The missing theorem is an exact construction or evaluation of \(w^{(0)}\), not another manipulation of the bracket.

# 3. Why exponent \(0\) does not give a finite algebraic membership test

The equality of all three Birkhoff powers says that the canonical modes have the form

\[
y_n^{(i)}=\rho_i^n\left(1+\frac{a_{i,1}}n+\frac{a_{i,2}}{n^2}+\cdots\right).
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is local formal information at infinity. It does not give the connection matrix between the finite-index basis

\[
(u_0,u_1,u_2)
\]

and the asymptotic basis \(y^{(0)},y^{(+)},y^{(-)}\). The entries of that connection matrix are generally periods or other transcendental constants.

In particular, the assertion in the current proof draft that the dominant coefficient ratio is obtained from a \(3\times3\) system of algebraic numbers is not justified: the values of a normalized Birkhoff basis at \(n=0,1,2\) are not known to be algebraic.

## 3.1 No rational second-order equation for the recessive plane

Here is a precise no-go statement.

**Proposition.** Suppose \(Q\in\mathbb Q(n)\langle S\rangle\) has order \(2\) and its solution space is the two-dimensional recessive plane of \(L\). Then \(Q\) is a right divisor of \(L\).

**Proof.** Right-divide in the Ore Euclidean domain:

\[
L=RQ+T,\qquad \operatorname{ord}T<2.
\]

Every solution of \(Q\) is, by hypothesis, a solution of \(L\), so \(T\) annihilates two linearly independent solutions. A nonzero difference operator of order \(0\) or \(1\) cannot have a two-dimensional solution space. Hence \(T=0\), and \(Q\mid_{\rm right}L\). \(\square\)

The project proves \(L\) irreducible over \(\mathbb Q(n)\), so such a \(Q\) does not exist. The dual statement excludes a rational first-order adjoint equation selecting \(w^{(0)}\).

An extension-field factorization is a separate finite computation; the corrected exponent \(0\) does not rule it out by itself. But if an order-two factor over a number field \(K\) selected the recessive plane, then applying it to

\[
e=p-Lq,\qquad L=\zeta(2)+\zeta(3),
\]

would give

\[
Qp=L\,Qq.
\]

Since \(q\) has a nonzero dominant component, \(Qq\ne0\), and the right-hand ratio would make \(L\) algebraic over \(K\). That would imply the transcendence of \(\zeta(3)=L-\pi^2/6\). Thus such a finite algebraic factor would be an extraordinarily strong theorem, not a routine consequence of the recurrence.

## 3.2 Operator structure cannot determine the special constant

For every constant \(c\),

\[
p^{(c)}=p+cq
\]

satisfies the same recurrence, has the same Casoratian law, the same determinant gauge, and the same asymptotic roots and formal powers. Yet

\[
\frac{J(w^{(0)},p^{(c)})}{J(w^{(0)},q)}
=
\frac{J(w^{(0)},p)}{J(w^{(0)},q)}+c.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Therefore no property of the homogeneous recurrence alone can select the value \(\zeta(2)+\zeta(3)\). One needs an additional boundary condition, sum, integral, regulator, or period identity attached specifically to the marked numerator sequence.

# 4. The proposed product series

Let

\[
u_n=c_0(u)y_n^{(0)}+c_+(u)y_n^{(+)}+c_-(u)y_n^{(-)}
\]

and let \(w^{(0)}\) be the slow adjoint solution. Since all powers are \(0\),

\[
y_n^{(0)}w_n^{(0)}
=\kappa_0\left(1+O(n^{-1})\right),
\qquad \kappa_0\ne0,
\tag{4.1}
\]

whereas

\[
y_n^{(\pm)}w_n^{(0)}
=O\!\left(\left|\frac{\rho_\pm}{\rho_0}\right|^n\right).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
 u_nw_n^{(0)}
 =c_0(u)\kappa_0\left(1+O(n^{-1})\right)
 +O\!\left(\left|\frac{\rho_\pm}{\rho_0}\right|^n\right).
\tag{4.3}
\]

It follows that

\[
\sum_{n=0}^N u_nw_n^{(0)}
=c_0(u)\kappa_0N+O(\log N)+C_u+o(1).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
c_0(u)=0
\iff
\sum_{n\ge0}u_nw_n^{(0)}
\text{ converges}.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

But if the series converges, its limit \(C_u\) is generally not zero. The zero object is the conserved bracket \(J(w^{(0)},u)\), not the ordinary product sum.

An equivalent Abelian statement is that

\[
F_u(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}u_nw_n^{(0)}z^n
\]

has a simple pole at \(z=1\) whose residue is proportional to \(c_0(u)\). Thus \(c_0(u)=0\) is equivalent to removability of that pole.

## 4.1 Why high-precision summation cannot prove exact zero

Miller backward iteration gives exponentially accurate approximations to \(w^{(0)}\), and interval arithmetic can turn this into a rigorous bound

\[
|J(w^{(0)},e)|<10^{-N}.
\]

But no finite \(N\) implies equality to zero unless one also has a lower bound for every possible nonzero value. No such arithmetic lower bound is known for this connection coefficient. A nonzero value of size \(10^{-10^{10}}\) is not excluded by the recurrence.

This is exactly why the current 5200-digit computation is compelling evidence but remains conditional.

# 5. What the Casoratian factorization does and does not say

Let \(u^{(1)},u^{(2)},u^{(3)}\) be three independent forward solutions and define

\[
\mathcal C_n=
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
 u_n^{(1)}&u_n^{(2)}&u_n^{(3)}\\
 u_{n+1}^{(1)}&u_{n+1}^{(2)}&u_{n+1}^{(3)}\\
 u_{n+2}^{(1)}&u_{n+2}^{(2)}&u_{n+2}^{(3)}
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For the project companion state \((u_n,u_{n+1},u_{n+2})^T\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_{n+1}=\frac{D(n)}{A(n)}\mathcal C_n.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The ratio is \(D(n)/A(n)\), not \(D(n)/A(n+2)\), in this indexing.

Put

\[
R(n)=946n^2+4515n+5399.
\]

Since

\[
946n^2+6407n+10860=R(n+1),
\]

one has

\[
\frac{D(n)}{A(n)}
=\frac{R(n)}{R(n+1)}
\frac{(n+3)^4(n+4)^6}
{1024(2n+5)^4(2n+7)^3(2n+9)^3}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Define

\[
h_n=2^{-20n}
\frac{(3)_n^4(4)_n^6}
{(5/2)_n^4(7/2)_n^3(9/2)_n^3}.
\]

Then the second factor in (5.3) is \(h_{n+1}/h_n\), and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_N
=\mathcal C_0\frac{R(0)}{R(N)}h_N.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is valuable structural information: it explicitly trivializes the determinant module \(\bigwedge^3V\).

It does **not** identify the recessive plane. The slow adjoint line lives in

\[
V^\vee\simeq\bigwedge^2V\otimes(\bigwedge^3V)^{-1}.
\]

Knowing \(\bigwedge^3V\) gives the denominator in this expression, but one still needs the specific two-vector

\[
y^{(+)}\wedge y^{(-)}
\]

that represents the recessive plane. If the two exact recessive solutions were known, their cross product, divided by the Casoratian gauge, would give \(w^{(0)}\). Formula (5.4) alone supplies neither solution.

This is the order-three difference from the familiar order-two Apéry situation. A top Wronskian in rank two almost determines the complementary line once one solution is known. In rank three, the determinant line does not determine a distinguished two-plane.

Any Casoratian built only from \(p,q,e\) is also vacuous, because

\[
e=p-Lq
\]

already makes these three columns linearly dependent for every value of \(L\).

# 6. The existing WZ construction for the inner AESZ/Zudilin sequence

Define

\[
F(n,k)=\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{n}\binom{n+2k}{n},
\qquad
b_n=\sum_{k=0}^nF(n,k).
\tag{6.1}
\]

The term is proper hypergeometric. Its elementary shift quotients are

\[
\frac{F(n+1,k)}{F(n,k)}
=\frac{(n+k+1)(n+2k+1)}{(n+1-k)^2},
\tag{6.2}
\]

and

\[
\frac{F(n,k+1)}{F(n,k)}
=\frac{(n-k)^2(n+k+1)(n+2k+1)(n+2k+2)}
{(k+1)^3(2k+1)(2k+2)}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Zudilin's Gosper--Zeilberger calculation gives the recurrence

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
0={}&2(946n^2-731n+153)(2n+1)(n+1)^3b_{n+1}\\
&-2(104060n^6+127710n^5+12788n^4-34525n^3\\
&\qquad-8482n^2+3298n+1071)b_n\\
&+2n(3784n^5-1032n^4-1925n^3+853n^2+328n-184)b_{n-1}\\
&-(946n^2+1161n+368)n(n-1)^3b_{n-2}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{6.4}
\]

The initial values are

\[
b_0=1,\qquad b_1=7,\qquad b_2=163.
\]

Equivalently, there is a rational certificate \(R(n,k)\) such that

\[
L_Z^{(n)}F(n,k)
=\Delta_k\bigl(R(n,k)F(n,k)\bigr).
\tag{6.5}
\]

After division by \(F(n,k)\), the certificate is the rational solution of the Gosper equation

\[
\frac{F(n,k+1)}{F(n,k)}R(n,k+1)-R(n,k)
=\frac{L_Z^{(n)}F(n,k)}{F(n,k)}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

This is the exact WZ certificate problem; the source paper states that the Gosper--Zeilberger algorithm produces it, although it does not print the expanded rational function.

The primary source is Wadim Zudilin,
[*Approximations to -, di- and tri-logarithms*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0409023), Theorem 2.

## 6.1 The source already has the correct \(\zeta(2)\) and \(\zeta(3)\) companions

Zudilin starts from

\[
\mathcal R_n(t)=
\frac{((t-1)(t-2)\cdots(t-n))^3}
{(n!)^2t(t+1)\cdots(t+n)}.
\tag{6.7}
\]

The two linear forms are

\[
r_n^{(2)}=-\sum_{\nu\ge1}\mathcal R_n'(\nu)
=b_n\zeta(2)-p_n^{(2)},
\tag{6.8}
\]

\[
r_n^{(3)}=\frac12\sum_{\nu\ge1}\mathcal R_n''(\nu)
=b_n\zeta(3)-p_n^{(3)},
\tag{6.9}
\]

with the endpoint \(z=1\) understood by the Barnes/Abel continuation used in the paper. The sequences

\[
b_n,\quad p_n^{(2)},\quad p_n^{(3)},\quad r_n^{(2)},\quad r_n^{(3)}
\]

all satisfy (6.4). Thus

\[
E_n^Z=p_n^{(2)}+p_n^{(3)}
-(\zeta(2)+\zeta(3))b_n
=\sum_{\nu\ge1}
\left(\mathcal R_n'(\nu)-\frac12\mathcal R_n''(\nu)\right)
\tag{6.10}
\]

is an exact, manifestly recessive source error.

There is also the Whipple identity

\[
\boxed{
 b_n=(-1)^n\sum_{k=0}^n(-1)^k
 \binom nk\binom{n+k}{k}^3
 =\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{n}\binom{n+2k}{n}.}
\tag{6.11}
\]

So the inner AESZ #209 sum is already the denominator in a complete simultaneous-approximation construction.

## 6.2 Why this does not yet prove P2.7

The recurrence (6.4) and the printed P2.7 recurrence have the same limiting cubic but different finite coefficients and different marked initial values. Equality of Poincaré data is only an asymptotic statement.

The outer AESZ period

\[
\binom{2n}{n}b_n
\]

is another object again; its characteristic roots are multiplied by \(4\).

Therefore the existing WZ certificate proves recessiveness for \(E_n^Z\), not for the challenge error \(e_n\).

# 7. The minimal additional certificate that would finish P2.7

The source sequence \(b_n\) has formal power \(-3/2\). Define

\[
\widehat b_n
=64^{-n}\frac{(5/2)_n}{n!}b_n.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Then:

- \(64^{-n}\) changes the source roots \(
u_i\) to the P2.7 roots \(ho_i=
u_i/64\);
- \((5/2)_n/n!\sim n^{3/2}/\Gamma(5/2)\) changes the power \(-3/2\) to \(0\).

Thus the twisted source module has exactly the same formal exponential and power data as P2.7.

Let \(L_Z^{\rm tw}\) be the Ore-gauged version of (6.4). The decisive finite search is:

\[
\boxed{
L_{27}U=V L_Z^{\rm tw},
\qquad
U=u_0(n)+u_1(n)S+u_2(n)S^2,
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

with \(u_i(n)\in\mathbb Q(n)\), together with the exact marked identities

\[
q=U\widehat b,
\tag{7.3}
\]

\[
p=U\left(64^{-n}\frac{(5/2)_n}{n!}
(p_n^{(2)}+p_n^{(3)})\right).
\tag{7.4}
\]

If (7.2)--(7.4) hold, then applying \(U\) to (6.10) gives

\[
e=U\left(64^{-n}\frac{(5/2)_n}{n!}E^Z\right),
\]

and a finite Ore operator cannot introduce the dominant exponential root into a source error supported only on the two subdominant roots. This would prove \(c_0(e)=0\) exactly.

A scalar map cannot work: already

\[
\widehat b_0=1,
\qquad
\widehat b_1=\frac{5}{2}\frac7{64}=\frac{35}{128},
\]

while \(q_1/q_0=0.859569944\ldots\). Therefore a nontrivial cyclic-vector operator \(U\) is essential.

If (7.2) has no solution in reasonable degree bounds, the alternative target is a genuinely new multiple certificate

\[
\boxed{
L_{27}^{(n)}\Phi(n,k,\nu)
=\Delta_k U(n,k,\nu)+\Delta_\nu V(n,k,\nu),
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

with

\[
e_n=\sum_{k,\nu}\Phi(n,k,\nu),
\]

vanishing boundary terms, and a subdominant saddle estimate. Equation (7.5), not the common characteristic polynomial, is what a three-variable WZ proof would have to produce.

# 8. Reproducible Sage diagnostics

The following standalone code uses the recurrence exactly as it appears in the project. It:

- constructs \(p,q\);
- computes the slow adjoint line by Miller backward iteration;
- evaluates the exact bracket at \(n=0\);
- confirms numerically that the bracket ratio is \(\zeta(2)+\zeta(3)\);
- compares the ordinary product sums for \(q\) and \(e\);
- verifies the Casoratian coboundary.

It is a numerical diagnostic, not an exact proof of zero.

```sage

# Precision
# ============================================================

def A_Q(k):

    return (
        1024*(2*k+5)^4*(2*k+7)^3*(2*k+9)^3
        *(946*k^2+6407*k+10860)
    )


def B_Q(k):
    k = QQ(k)
    return (
        128*(2*k+7)^3*(2*k+9)^3
        *(104060*k^6+1745370*k^5+12145238*k^4
          +44886481*k^3+92943995*k^2
          +102256019*k+46709052)
    )


def C_Q(k):
    k = QQ(k)
    return (
        16*(k+3)^4*(2*k+9)^3
        *(3784*k^5+57792*k^4+351019*k^3
          +1059230*k^2+1587211*k+944620)
    )


def D_Q(k):
    k = QQ(k)
    return (k+3)^4*(k+4)^6*(946*k^2+4515*k+5399)


def alpha_Q(k):
    return B_Q(k)/A_Q(k)


def beta_Q(k):
    return -C_Q(k-1)/A_Q(k-1)

    return D_Q(k-2)/A_Q(k-2)

def beta(k):  return RF(beta_Q(k))
def gamma(k): return RF(gamma_Q(k))

p_init = [
    QQ(-612218384750),
    QQ(-9525021973931919)/18100,
    QQ(-29561828382772029)/65380,

    QQ(-215040420000),
    QQ(-167282265043404)/905,
    QQ(-964185327658080)/6071,

# ============================================================
# Forward solutions, indices 0,...,N
# ============================================================
def forward(init, N):
    u = [RF(0)]*(N+1)
    for j in range(3):
        u[j] = RF(init[j])

            alpha(k)*u[k]
```

# 9. Final assessment

The equal formal powers \(0\) are important: they make the slow-adjoint product criterion especially clean and remove an earlier false irrational-exponent obstruction. They do **not** make the recessive hyperplane algebraic.

The current recurrence already supplies an exact characterization,

\[
 c_0(e)=0\iff J(w^{(0)},e)=0,
\]

and Miller iteration computes the relevant adjoint direction extraordinarily accurately. To turn this into a proof, one still needs one genuinely new exact input:

1. a Barnes/Euler/modular integral for \(w^{(0)}\) and an exact evaluation of its bracket;
2. two exact recessive forward solutions;
3. the twisted Zudilin Ore intertwiner (7.2)--(7.4); or
4. a direct multiple creative-telescoping certificate (7.5) with a subdominant contour estimate.

Among these, the twisted Zudilin source is the most concrete algebraic target because the source already contains the exact \(\zeta(2)+\zeta(3)\) companion and the correct characteristic cubic. What is missing is not the WZ mechanism; it is the exact finite bridge from that mechanism to the printed P2.7 cyclic vector.
