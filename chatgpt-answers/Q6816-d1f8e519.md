# Verdict summary

The Christol angle is structurally correct, but the hoped-for implication is false in the form needed. The decisive point is:

A bounded-degree algebraic equation for a power series over F_p(t) does NOT imply that the first p coefficients have O(1), or even polylogarithmic, many zeros. Automaticity gives decidability and computability of densities, not a small zero count. Large zero sets occur for very small automata.

Therefore [AVG-ZERO] does not follow from Christol alone. It requires Apery-specific arithmetic beyond the algebraic/automatic package.

# (i) Verification of the Christol reduction

The diagonal statement is the right starting point. Apery numbers are diagonals of rational functions, so after reduction modulo p the generating function B(t)=sum b_n t^n is algebraic over F_p(t) by Furstenberg's diagonal theorem.

Christol's theorem then gives:

B(t) algebraic over F_p(t) <=> (b_n mod p) is p-automatic.

The digit formula

b_{mp+r}=b_m b_r (mod p)

is the stronger Lucas/Gessel property. Iterating gives

b_n = product_i b_{d_i} (mod p)

for n=sum d_i p^i.

Hence the zero set is exactly

{n : some base-p digit d_i lies in Z_p}.

This part is correct.

The identification of |Z_p| with the number of zero coefficients of the p-kernel seed B_0(t)=sum_{r<p} b_r t^r is also correct. The Mahler relation separates the first digit.

# Degree/height issue

The obstacle appears immediately here.

General diagonal bounds are effective but depend on the ambient rational function, not merely on the fact that the sequence is Apery. Furstenberg's construction produces an algebraic equation whose degree is controlled by the denominator geometry after reduction modulo p. It is not a fixed degree independent of p in the naive sense.

For a rational function in d variables with denominator degree h, the diagonal algebraicity degree bounds from the effective diagonal theory are polynomial/exponential in h and d (depending on the chosen construction), and the resulting equation height can grow with p after clearing denominators.

The Adamczewski-Bell framework gives effective algebraicity/automaticity bounds for diagonals, but it does not give a uniform bounded automaton size as p varies.

For the Apery diagonal, the ambient rational function is fixed over Z, which is favorable, but the reduction modulo p can still produce p-dependent Cartier orbits. The relevant quantity is the size of the Cartier kernel, not just the formal degree of the characteristic-zero diagonal representation.

# (ii) Zero coefficients of algebraic series

The hoped-for statement:

algebraic degree D and height H => number of zero coefficients among first p is O(poly(D,H))

is false.

Reason: automatic sequences with tiny automata can have arbitrarily large zero sets.

Example:

f(t)=sum_{n>=0} t^{p n}

over F_p has coefficient sequence 1 exactly on numbers whose base-p expansion has one nonzero digit in the lowest position and zeros elsewhere. Its automaton is finite and its generating function is rational. Variants give arbitrarily large structured zero sets.

Even simpler, the rational series

f(t)=1/(1-t^m)

has coefficients supported on one residue class modulo m. For m growing with p, the first p coefficients contain about p/m nonzero coefficients and hence many zeros, while the automaton remains simple.

Thus no bound independent of p can follow from algebraicity alone.

The Christol-Kamae-Mendes France-Rauzy theory gives:

- the zero set of an automatic sequence is automatic;
- densities are computable from transition matrices when the automaton is given.

It does not say the density is small.

Derksen's theorem for linear recurrences is analogous but does not transfer to arbitrary algebraic diagonals in a way that bounds zero counts. The algebraic analogue is an automatic-set structural theorem, not a sparsity theorem.

# (iii) What if the algebraic complexity grows with p?

This also does not save the argument.

To prove

sum_{p<=X}|Z_p|=o(X)

from a pointwise bound, one would need roughly |Z_p|=o(1) on average over primes, because pi(X)~X/log X.

A bound |Z_p|=p^alpha is useless unless alpha is extremely negative. Even |Z_p|=log p gives

sum_{p<=X} log p ~ X,

which is only the boundary, not o(X).

The known unconditional bound

|Z_p| <= 3 p^{2/3}

is far too weak.

Therefore the needed automatic bound is much stronger than generic algebraicity can supply.

# (iv) Adversarial examples

Yes, generic algebraic power series can have many zero coefficients.

The feature missing from these examples is exactly the Apery arithmetic:

- the recurrence has Picard-Fuchs origin;
- the orbit lives in a two-dimensional p-adic/finite-field representation;
- reflection b_{p-1-r}=b_r;
- the eta-product supercongruence controls special points;
- zeros empirically look Poisson/random rather than residue-class structured.

A generic automatic sequence can have a large forbidden digit set. The Apery automaton appears experimentally closer to a random automaton where the zero output state has probability about 1/p per digit, giving mean O(1) zeros in the first p block.

But that heuristic is not a theorem.

# (v) Reflection and pairing

The reflection identity is useful but insufficient.

It reduces the zero count to paired roots plus possibly the midpoint:

r <-> p-1-r.

This explains the observed parity law and the exceptional midpoint controlled by

b_{(p-1)/2}=a_p mod p.

However, pairing only divides the count by roughly two. It does not turn a p^{2/3} bound into o(log p).

The lower-degree object one would want is not the reflected polynomial alone, but a quotient encoding the digit automaton plus reflection symmetry. At present no such bounded-complexity object is known.

# Final assessment

The Christol route changes the formulation:

AVG-ZERO is a statement about the first-level Cartier orbit of the Apery algebraic series.

But the missing theorem is not a generic algebraic-zero theorem. It is an Apery-specific Cartier rigidity theorem.

A realistic target statement would be:

For the Apery diagonal modulo p, the Cartier orbit has a uniformly bounded number of zero-producing states on average over p.

Equivalently, the p-kernel of (b_n mod p) has controlled zero-state mass.

This is stronger than Christol and exactly tailored to [AVG-ZERO].

Least confident step: the discussion of effective degree/height growth from the diagonal representation. The qualitative conclusion is robust, but the sharpest published numerical dependence depends on the chosen Furstenberg/Cartier implementation.

Blind spot in the question: it treats algebraicity as if it implied pseudorandom sparsity of zeros. Automaticity only gives finite-state structure; finite-state structure can encode both random-looking sequences and highly zero-rich sequences. The missing ingredient is not algebraicity but a uniform arithmetic restriction on the Apery Cartier automaton.
