# Q6801 corrected sparse-slice audit

## 1. Correct prime decomposition

The previous reduction using the full zero set was too strong and false. The master sum only samples a sparse arithmetic slice.

Write

`M(n)=sum_{r<n/2} log rad_{>max(sqrt(n),r)} gcd(b_r,n-r)`.

Using the radical, a prime p contributes at most once. The contribution is

`M_p(n)=log p * #{r<n/2 : p|n-r and p|b_r and p>max(sqrt(n),r)}`.

Equivalently, define

`R_p(n)={r<n/2 : r == n mod p, r in [0,p-1], b_r=0 mod p}`.

Then

`M(n)=sum_{sqrt(n)<p<=n} log p * |R_p(n)|`.

This is the exact sparse slice. Since r is a residue representative,

`|R_p(n)| <= 1 + n/(2p)`

before imposing the zero condition.

For p>n/2 there is exactly one possible representative: r=n-p. Hence

`M_top(n)=sum_{n/2<p<=n, b_{n-p}=0 mod p} log p`.

For sqrt(n)<p<=n/2 there can be several representatives, approximately n/p many. The valuation cap

`v_p(G_n)<=6`

means even if the same prime appears through the gcd, the total contribution of p to log G_n is bounded by a constant multiple of log p; multiplicities in the radical slice are not multiplied by valuations.

## 2. Correct sufficient statements

The top window requires a density statement:

`#{p in (n/2,n] : b_{n-p}=0 mod p}=o(n/log n)`.

Equivalently,

`sum_{n/2<p<=n, b_{n-p}=0} log p=o(n)`.

This is the correct threshold because the full prime log mass in the interval is asymptotic to n.

The middle range requires the weighted sparse-slice estimate:

`sum_{sqrt(n)<p<=n/2} log p * #{r<n/2 : r==n mod p, b_r=0} = o(n)`.

A sufficient dyadic formulation is: for every dyadic P with sqrt(n)<P<=n/2,

`sum_{P<p<=2P} log p * |R_p(n)| = o(n)`

uniformly after summing dyadic intervals.

The threshold is not a bound on |Z_p|. It is a bound on sampled hits.

## 3. Average-over-p assessment

This is genuinely different from the fixed-p problem.

For fixed p, |Z_p| asks for all orbit visits to a line. The master sum asks for one prescribed orbit time (top window) or O(n/p) prescribed times (middle range).

A positive-proportion top-window failure would mean

`#{p in (n/2,n]: xi_{n-p} hits the zero line}=c n/log n`

for some c>0.

This is a family of single-point tests. It is much closer to a prime sieve problem than to the quenched orbit-energy problem.

Chebotarev alone does not immediately solve it: the tested point has index n-p, which changes with p. Standard Frobenius equidistribution fixes the prime and varies algebraic data; here the index and the prime are coupled.

A possible route is a bilinear formulation over pairs (p,r) with r=n-p (or r=n mod p in the middle range), using large sieve technology for the corresponding Galois representations. This is not currently proved in the available toolkit.

## 4. Supercongruence/Lucas possibility

The key question is whether

`b_{n-p} mod p`

can be rewritten purely from Frobenius data at p.

The known Lucas/Gessel relation

`b_{mp+r}=b_m b_r mod p`

reduces indices with a fixed base-p expansion, but n-p has a borrow in base p. In the top window:

`n=p+r`

gives

`b_n=b_{p+r}=b_1 b_r mod p = 5b_r mod p`.

This relates b_r to b_n, but it does not turn b_r into a local Frobenius invariant attached only to p.

The modular form attached to Apery numbers controls Frobenius traces such as a_p. It does not presently provide a formula for the individual value b_r mod p with r depending on p.

Therefore the promising conversion in (3c) is not yet available: the index dependence survives.

## 5. Correct reduction chain

The valid chain is:

`log G_n <= 6 M(n)+O(n^(2/3)log n)`

with

`M(n)=sum_{sqrt(n)<p<=n} log p |R_p(n)|`.

It is enough to prove:

Top:

`sum_{n/2<p<=n} log p * 1_{b_{n-p}=0 mod p}=o(n)`.

Middle:

`sum_{sqrt(n)<p<=n/2} log p |R_p(n)|=o(n)`.

The previous condition

`sum_p |Z_p|log p=o(n)`

is not only unnecessary but empirically false.

## Verdict

The sparse-slice correction changes the strategic picture. The obstacle is no longer an average theorem for full orbit zero counts. It is a coupled prime-index distribution theorem.

The route through (3c) would be decisive if a theorem expressing b_{n-p} mod p through local Frobenius data existed. The current Lucas and modularity facts do not provide that conversion.

Least confident step: the exact handling of the middle range after splitting into dyadic prime intervals; the sparse representative count is clear, but a sharp uniform large-sieve estimate would require new input.

Possible blind spot: the question may assume the modular form representation is the correct state variable. It may not be: the missing object could be a two-variable distribution theorem for the Apéry recurrence cocycle, not a one-prime Frobenius statistic.
