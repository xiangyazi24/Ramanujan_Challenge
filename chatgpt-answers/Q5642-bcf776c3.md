# 1. Exact pure-cross count and the final implication

For `p<ell=p+h` in the top-half target set at `n`, put

\[
s=n-\ell.
\]

The collision conditions are exactly PC.1:

\[
p\mid b_{s+h},
\qquad
\ell\mid b_s,
\qquad
0\le s<s+h<p<\ell.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The pair is degenerate when

\[
p\mid b_s
\quad\text{or}\quad
\ell\mid b_{s+h},
\tag{1.3}
\]

and otherwise pure cross. Let `M_h^x(n)` denote the number of unordered pure-cross pairs of exact prime gap `h`. PC.3--PC.6 give

\[
M_h^{\rm deg}(n)\ll h,
\qquad
\sum_{h\le H}M_h^{\rm deg}(n)\ll H^2,
\tag{1.4}
\]

and on the pure-cross locus the gap continuant is a unit in both target characteristics.

Because

\[
n-p\equiv n\pmod p,
\]

the all-pair count of exact gap `h` is

\[
M_h(n)
=
\sum_{\substack{n/2<p\le n-h\\p,\,p+h\ {m prime}}}
 f_p(n)f_{p+h}(n).
\tag{1.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{h\le H}M_h^x(n)
\le
\sum_{h\le H}M_h(n),
\tag{1.6}
\]

and, conversely, subtracting (1.4) changes the short-gap count by only `O(H^2)`.

## Theorem 1.1 -- two-characteristic dispersion implies top-half P3.2

Fix every `A>0`. Assume (TD). Then uniformly for `N<n<=2N`,

\[
\sum_{\substack{h\le A\log N\\2\mid h}}M_h^x(n)
=o_A(N/\log N).
\tag{1.7}
\]

Consequently

\[
|T_n|=o(N/\log N)
\]

uniformly on the shell, and hence

\[
\sum_{p\in T_n}\log p=o(N).
\]

### Proof

The mean term in (TD) is

\[
\mathcal M_A(n)
=
\sum_{\substack{h\le A\log N\\2\mid h}}
\sum_{\substack{n/2<p\le n-h\\p,\,p+h\ {m prime}}}
\frac{z_pz_{p+h}}{p(p+h)}.
\]

The proved row estimate gives `z_p\ll N^(2/3)` in this range. The Selberg upper-bound sieve and the mean estimate for the singular series give

\[
\#\{(p,h):h\le A\log N,\ p,p+h\ {m prime},\ p\asymp N\}
\ll_A \frac{N\log N}{\log^2N}
\ll_A\frac N{\log N}.
\]

Every summand is `O(N^(-2/3))`, hence

\[
\mathcal M_A(n)
\ll_A\frac{N^{1/3}}{\log N}
=o(N/\log N).
\tag{1.8}
\]

Combining (TD), (1.5), and (1.8) gives

\[
\sum_{h\le A\log N}M_h(n)=o_A(N/\log N).
\]

Equation (1.4) then gives (1.7).

Now suppose instead that, along a sequence of `n`,

\[
K=|T_n|\ge\varepsilon N/\log N.
\tag{1.9}
\]

Order the target primes. Their total span is less than `n/2<=N`. The number of adjacent gaps exceeding `A log N` is at most

\[
\frac{N}{A\log N}.
\]

Choose `A>4/epsilon`. Then all but at most `epsilon N/(4 log N)` of the `K-1` adjacent gaps are at most `A log N`. By (1.4), only `O_A(log^2N)` are degenerate. Hence

\[
\sum_{h\le A\log N}M_h^x(n)
\ge c_\varepsilon N/\log N,
\]

contradicting (1.7). This proves the theorem. `square`

The theorem is deliberately stronger than PC.21 because it counts every short pure-cross pair, not only adjacent pairs after order pruning. It is nonetheless the cleanest exact Fourier interface.

# 2. Exact Fourier expansion

For each prime `p`, extend `f_p` periodically to `Z`. Define

\[
\widehat f_p(a)
=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}f_p(r)e_p(-ar).
\]

Then

\[
f_p(n)
=\frac{z_p}{p}
 +\frac1p\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\widehat f_p(a)e_p(an).
\tag{2.1}
\]

For `ell=p+h`, multiplying the two expansions gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
f_p(n)f_\ell(n)-\frac{z_pz_\ell}{p\ell}
={}&
\frac{z_\ell}{p\ell}
 \sum_{a\ne0}\widehat f_p(a)e_p(an)\\
&+\frac{z_p}{p\ell}
 \sum_{b\ne0}\widehat f_\ell(b)e_\ell(bn)\\
&+\frac1{p\ell}
 \sum_{a\ne0}\sum_{b\ne0}
 \widehat f_p(a)\widehat f_\ell(b)
 e_p(an)e_\ell(bn).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
\]

This is an identity, with no probabilistic approximation and no replacement of the pointwise quantifier.

Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak D_A(N;n)
:=\sum_{\substack{2\le h\le A\log N\\2\mid h}}
\sum_{\substack{n/2<p\le n-h\\p,\,p+h\ {m prime}}}
\Bigg[&
\frac{z_{p+h}}{p(p+h)}
 \sum_{a\ne0}\widehat f_p(a)e_p(an)\\
&+\frac{z_p}{p(p+h)}
 \sum_{b\ne0}\widehat f_{p+h}(b)e_{p+h}(bn)\\
&+\frac1{p(p+h)}
 \sum_{a\ne0}\sum_{b\ne0}
 \widehat f_p(a)\widehat f_{p+h}(b)
 e_p(an)e_{p+h}(bn)
\Bigg].
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Then (TD) is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{N<n\le2N}|\mathfrak D_A(N;n)|
=o_A(N/\log N).
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

This is the precise trace/character sum requested in the question.

A weaker-looking bound on only the double-nonzero-frequency term is not automatically sufficient: the two mixed terms are also pointwise and can align. They must either be included in the theorem, or separately bounded using additional cancellation for the first moments of the actual Apéry masks.

# 3. A sharp obstruction for one-row Fourier and large-sieve input

The following result strengthens the Q5639 obstruction in the direction needed here. It is not merely a sparse-row/star construction: it satisfies optimal individual Fourier bounds and creates the exact summed short-gap failure.

## Theorem 3.1 -- Fourier-optimal pure-cross adversary

There are absolute constants `c>0` and `A_0>0` such that, for arbitrarily large `n`, one can choose a set `P_n` of primes in `(n/2,n]` and two-point reflected masks `f_p` for `p in P_n` with the following properties:

1. `|P_n|>=c n/log n`;
2. with `r_p=n-p`,
   \[
   \operatorname{supp}(f_p)=\{r_p,p-1-r_p\};
   \]
3. each mask has exact reflection and no consecutive support points;
4. every pair of selected primes is pure cross;
5. every saturated same-row continuant condition is vacuous: the only pair of support points is the reflected pair, and its unsaturated continuant contains the universal factor `p`;
6. for every `a mod p`,
   \[
   |\widehat f_p(a)|\le2;
   \tag{3.1}
   \]
7. for every fixed `A>A_0`, the number of adjacent selected-prime gaps at most `A log n` is
   \[
   \gg_A n/\log n.
   \tag{3.2}
   \]

Consequently no theorem based only on reflection, support size, continuant saturation, Parseval, individual Fourier bounds, or individual bounded-conductor trace estimates can prove (2.4).

### Proof

Begin with all primes in `(n/2,n]`, excluding the `O(1)` values for which the two reflected support points coincide or are consecutive. For two distinct primes `p<ell`, the corresponding reflected masks make the pair degenerate precisely when

\[
n-\ell=p-1-(n-p),
\]

or symmetrically. This is equivalent to

\[
\ell=2n+1-2p.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The map on the right is injective. Thus the graph whose vertices are candidate primes and whose edges are the degeneracy relations is a matching, apart from the excluded boundary values. Choose one endpoint from every edge. The resulting independent set `P_n` contains at least half the candidate primes, and hence

\[
|P_n|\ge c n/\log n
\]

for all sufficiently large `n` by the prime number theorem.

For `p in P_n`, define

\[
f_p(r)=\mathbf1_{\{r=r_p\}}+\mathbf1_{\{r=p-1-r_p\}}.
\]

The graph independence is exactly the pure-cross condition for every selected pair, as checked in Q5639. Reflection and same-row saturation are built into the definition.

The Fourier transform is explicit:

\[
\widehat f_p(a)
=e_p(-ar_p)+e_p(a(r_p+1)),
\]

so (3.1) holds, including the stronger bound `2` rather than `O(sqrt(p))`.

Order the selected primes. Their total span is less than `n/2`. Therefore the number of adjacent selected gaps exceeding `A log n` is at most

\[
\frac{n}{2A\log n}.
\]

Taking `A` larger than a fixed constant depending on `c`, and subtracting this from `|P_n|-1`, proves (3.2). `square`

## Corollary 3.2 -- exact scope of a viable dispersion theorem

A proof of (2.4) must use a relation which distinguishes the actual Apéry masks from the masks in Theorem 3.1. In particular, none of the following is sufficient by itself:

- `z_p=O(1)`, even with the optimal value `2`;
- square-root cancellation for every nontrivial Fourier coefficient;
- bounded moments of the Fourier coefficients;
- reflection and no-consecutive-zero constraints;
- all saturated same-prime continuant identities;
- the central-binomial support carrier;
- a large sieve whose only input is a norm such as
  \[
  \sum_a|\widehat f_p(a)|^2=pz_p.
  \]

The missing datum must compare actual quotient, jet, Mellin, or Frobenius coordinates in **different characteristics**.

# 4. Why the classical large sieve stops before the pointwise theorem

Write schematically

\[
F(n)=\sum_{p\asymp N}\frac1p
\sum_{a\ne0}c_{p,a}e_p(an).
\]

The additive large sieve gives, for an interval of external integers `n` of length `X`,

\[
\sum_{n\le X}|F(n)|^2
\ll (X+Q^2)\sum_{p\le Q}\sum_a|c_{p,a}|^2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Here `Q asy N` and the required pointwise interval has `X asy N`, so the `Q^2` term dominates. More importantly, (4.1) is an average over `n`. It cannot rule out one exceptional `n`, which is exactly the quantifier in P3.2.

Restricting to `ell-p<=A log N` does not remove this defect. It reduces the number of pairs but does not improve the separation of the fractions

\[
\frac ap+\frac b\ell.
\]

Near-collisions with distinct `(p,a,ell,b)` remain at the natural `N^(-2)` Farey scale, while the external interval has length only `N`. The short-gap relation provides no automatic extra phase in the fixed column `n`.

Theorem 3.1 is stronger than the usual `Q^2` diagnosis: even granting individual Fourier coefficients bounded by `2`, the pointwise sum can have critical size. Thus no optimization of standard large-sieve constants can close the gap.

# 5. The finite-field trace representation and the zero-mask obstruction

The repository contains fixed Laurent-polynomial and hypergeometric descriptions of the Apéry coefficients modulo `p`. Abstractly, one may write a marked Mellin coordinate in the form

\[
t_p(r)=b_r\pmod p,
\]

where `r` indexes a Kummer character or a coefficient of a fixed rational diagonal.

Suppose, optimistically, that `t_p(r)` is the trace of a compatible bounded-conductor object as `r` varies. Deligne's theorem can then bound complete sums of the form

\[
\sum_{r\in\mathbf F_p}K_p(r)e_p(ar)
\]

when `K_p` itself is a bounded-conductor trace function.

The required mask is instead

\[
f_p(r)=\mathbf1_{\{t_p(r)=0\}}
=\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}e_p(ut_p(r)).
\tag{5.1}
\]

There are two separate problems.

## 5.1 Additive character of a trace is not a bounded-conductor trace operation

The number `t_p(r)` is the Frobenius trace of a sheaf or cohomology group. It is not generally the value of a regular map `r -> A^1` to which one may pull back a fixed Artin--Schreier sheaf. The operation

\[
t\longmapsto e_p(ut)
\]

is nonlinear on Frobenius traces. Expressing it in the representation ring requires symmetric/exterior powers of unbounded degree, or interpolation of a function on `F_p` of degree up to `p-1`. The resulting rank and conductor grow with `p`.

Thus a Deligne bound for the original Apéry trace does not imply a uniform Deligne bound for the exact zero indicator.

## 5.2 The two factors are in different characteristics

Even if each `f_p` separately had a bounded-conductor realization, (2.3) couples

\[
f_p(n)\quad\text{and}\quad f_{p+h}(n)
\]

in the fields `F_p` and `F_(p+h)`. There is no common finite field over which their product is the trace of a tensor-product sheaf. Deligne's Riemann Hypothesis is a theorem for a fixed variety over one finite field, not a comparison theorem between two nearby residue characteristics.

A possible future route would require a compatible arithmetic object over `Z`, together with a theorem controlling the joint reductions at two varying primes and a moving Kummer index. No such theorem is supplied by standard geometric monodromy alone.

# 6. Why published trace-function-over-primes theorems do not apply

The closest standard framework is the work of Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel on algebraic trace functions over primes. Its basic setup fixes a prime modulus `q` and a bounded-conductor trace function `K mod q`, then estimates sums such as

\[
\sum_{m\le X}\Lambda(m)K(m).
\]

Type I/II decompositions and Deligne bounds produce power savings under suitable conductor and nonexceptionality hypotheses.

Our sum differs in every load-bearing quantifier:

1. the modulus is the prime `p` being summed;
2. the trace function changes with `p`;
3. there is a second modulus `p+h`;
4. the evaluated residue is tied to the fixed external column `n`;
5. the function is the nonlinear zero mask (5.1), not the original bounded-conductor Apéry trace;
6. the required result is a supremum over every `n`, not a mean over `n` or over primes in a fixed modulus.

Katz's Mellin-transform and hypergeometric-sheaf theorems provide monodromy and square-root cancellation for complete sums within one characteristic. They do not furnish a two-prime pointwise correlation theorem of the form (2.4).

Primary frameworks relevant to this diagnosis include:

- N. Katz, *Exponential Sums and Differential Equations*, Annals of Mathematics Studies 124;
- N. Katz, *Gauss Sums, Kloosterman Sums, and Monodromy Groups*, Annals of Mathematics Studies 116;
- E. Fouvry, E. Kowalski, P. Michel, *Algebraic trace functions over the primes*, Duke Math. J. 163 (2014), 1683--1736, arXiv:1211.6043.

These references justify square-root cancellation once a bounded-conductor sheaf in one finite field has been constructed. They do not construct the zero mask or bridge two varying characteristics.

# 7. Common-carrier formulation and its exact barrier

A summed-gap carrier strong enough for the desired theorem would be a nonzero integer `C_A(n)` satisfying

\[
\sum_{\substack{h\le A\log n\\2\mid h}}M_h^x(n)\log(n/2)
\le \log|C_A(n)|
\tag{7.1}
\]

and

\[
\log|C_A(n)|=o_A(n).
\tag{7.2}
\]

For degenerate pairs, the product of continuants supplies such factors with total logarithmic height `O_A(log^3 n)`. On a pure-cross pair, PC.6 says the ordinary gap continuant is a unit modulo both primes, so it contributes no valuation to (7.1).

The fixed-gap ambient carrier

\[
D^{\rm pp}_{n,h}
=\prod_{\substack{n/2<p\le n-h\\p,\,p+h\ {m prime}}}p(p+h)
\]

has

\[
\log D^{\rm pp}_{n,h}=o(n)
\]

for each exact `h`, but

\[
\sum_{h\le A\log n}\log D^{\rm pp}_{n,h}=O_A(n),
\]

which is exactly the critical loss.

Cross-weighting the Apéry Casoratian to force `p(p+h)` divisibility leaves the exponential term `b_sb_(s+h)`. Section 17's tensor saturation theorem shows that bounded-degree universal Pluecker/symmetric-power contractions cannot remove this final dominant slot without inserting `p(p+h)` as coefficient content.

Thus a successful carrier must be nonlocal in `h`, or exploit a relation among the actual opposite quotient residues

\[
\frac{b_{s+h}}p\pmod{p+h},
\qquad
\frac{b_s}{p+h}\pmod p,
\]

which is absent from the present recurrence-only algebra.

# 8. Exact new theorem interfaces

The summed-gap route can be reopened by any one of the following genuinely new statements.

## 8.1 Two-characteristic zero-mask dispersion

Prove (2.4) for every fixed `A>0`.

This is the cleanest sufficient theorem and directly closes the top-half branch by Theorem 1.1.

## 8.2 Nonlinear Mellin dispersion before zero detection

Construct a bounded-complexity marked Mellin object `T_p(r)` with

\[
T_p(r)=b_r\pmod p,
\]

and prove a theorem which controls, uniformly in `n`, the complete additive-output expansion

\[
\sum_{h,p}\frac1{p(p+h)}
\sum_{u\bmod p}\sum_{v\bmod p+h}
 e_p\!\bigl(uT_p(n)\bigr)
 e_{p+h}\!\bigl(vT_{p+h}(n)\bigr)
\tag{8.1}
\]

after subtracting the `(u,v)=(0,0)` term, with bound `o_A(N/log N)`.

This is equivalent to (TD), but exposes exactly where a geometric construction is needed: the output characters `u,v` and the two varying characteristics must be handled simultaneously.

## 8.3 A joint compressed carrier

Construct a nonzero `C_A(n)` satisfying (7.1)--(7.2). It need not factor gap by gap. Indeed, any successful construction must compress the whole logarithmic gap family jointly, because the individual Selberg carriers already attain the critical total height.

## 8.4 Opposite-quotient decorrelation

Prove that all but `o_A(N/log N)` prime pairs in the short-gap family fail at least one of the congruences forced by the actual opposite quotient residues. Such a theorem must use the distinguished Apéry initial state; the formal recurrence permits arbitrary residues after CRT.

# 9. Final assessment

The summed-gap loss is not an artifact of a crude summation. It is the exact point where all one-characteristic information has been exhausted.

The strongest theorem currently proved for each exact gap is

\[
M_h^x(n)\ll \mathfrak S(h)\frac n{\log^2n}.
\]

The mean singular-series estimate gives only

\[
\sum_{h\le A\log n}M_h^x(n)=O_A(n/\log n).
\]

No classical large sieve can turn this into a pointwise little-oh, and Theorem 3.1 shows that even perfect rowwise Fourier cancellation cannot do so. Deligne/Katz bounds remain one-characteristic and do not apply to the nonlinear zero mask across `p` and `p+h`.

The exact missing statement is the uniform two-characteristic dispersion estimate (2.4), or an equivalent joint carrier of height `o(n)`. Proving that estimate would close the top-half P3.2 branch. At present it is a new arithmetic theorem, not a consequence of existing trace-function technology.
