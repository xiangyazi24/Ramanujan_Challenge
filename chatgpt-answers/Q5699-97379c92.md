# P3.2 reflection/Kummer norm audit

## Executive verdict

Let

\[
b_r=\sum_{k=0}^r\binom rk^2\binom{r+k}{k}^2,
\qquad 0\le r<p,
\]

and suppose that \(p\) is an odd prime with \(p\mid b_r\). Put

\[
g=\gcd(r,p-1),\qquad d=\frac{p-1}{g},\qquad r=ga,\quad (a,d)=1.
\]

The exact answer is:

1. **Off the reflection center, the full cyclotomic norm really is divisible by \(p^2\).** In the explicit Kummer lift, the same algebraic integer is divisible by the two distinct split primes above \(p\) indexed by \(a\) and \(-a\). Equivalently, at one fixed \(p\)-adic embedding the two inverse-character conjugates both reduce to zero.

2. **Those are not two independent scalar equations.** At the fixed embedding their reductions are
   \[
   (-b_r,-b_{p-1-r})=(-b_r,-b_r).
   \]
   Reflection puts the pair on the diagonal. The second zero is the Galois/reflection image of the first, not a new transverse condition.

3. **The \(p^2\) is an artifact of using the full cyclotomic field.** In the minimal self-dual trace field, where inverse characters are already identified, the two cyclotomic primes contract to one prime and a simple reflected zero contributes only one factor \(p\) to the minimal norm. Norming back up to the full field squares both the divisibility and the archimedean height.

4. **There is no bounded-degree rational carrier supplied by the inverse pair.** The quadratic relative norm
   \[
   H_{p,d,a}H_{p,d,-a}
   \]
   lies in the maximal real field, not in \(\mathbf Q\). To obtain a rational integer one must still norm through a field of degree up to \(arphi(d)/2\). In the hard fixed-\(n\) range, \(d=p^{1-o(1)}\), so this degree is \(p^{1-o(1)}\).

5. **The norm-height ledger has no gain.** Unconditionally, the raw three-torus Kummer sum has
   \[
   |\sigma H_{p,d,a}|\le (p-1)^3,
   \]
   hence
   \[
   \log |N_{\mathbf Q(\zeta_d)/\mathbf Q}H_{p,d,a}|
   \le 3\varphi(d)\log p.
   \]
   Even granting the optimal rank-two, weight-three Weil normalization gives only
   \[
   \log |N(T_{p,d,a})|
   \le [L:\mathbf Q]\bigl(\tfrac32\log p+O(1)\bigr).
   \]
   The reflected factor \(p^2\) consumes only \(2\log p\), while the available norm height grows with the orbit degree. Near-primitivity makes this comparison worse, not better.

6. **Traces, a bounded number of conjugates, and bounded products of Gauss or Jacobi sums do not repair the problem.** The actual example \(p=17,r=3\) has primitive character order \(16\), but only \(r=3\) and \(-r=13\) vanish among the eight primitive indices. The full rational trace and the trace of the inverse-pair product are both nonzero modulo \(17\). Thus the first genuinely target-selective Galois-invariant operation is the whole orbit product, i.e. the norm.

7. **Bounded-order or bounded-orbit slices are already harmless by the project's divisor-sensitive order sieve.** They cannot address the residual P3.2 channel. The unresolved primes select characters of order \(p^{1-o(1)}\), where every standard orbit norm has growing degree and is a different integer for each rational prime.

Therefore the rigorous answer is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{YES: an off-center reflection pair forces }p^2\text{ in the full cyclotomic norm;}\quad
\text{NO: it gives no independent conjugate equation or useful low-height rational carrier.}
}
\]

This route cannot yield the pointwise estimate \(\log R_n=o(n)\). A successful argument must add an actual-Apéry, cross-prime relation not contained in reflection, cyclotomic conjugacy, Gauss/Jacobi identities, or ordinary norm descent.

## 1. The explicit Laurent/Kummer lift

Use the Gorodetsky Laurent polynomial

\[
\Phi(x,y,z)
 =\frac{(x+y)(z+1)(x+y+z)(y+z+1)}{xyz},
\qquad
b_m=\operatorname{CT}_{x,y,z}\Phi(x,y,z)^m.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Its Newton polytope has the origin as its unique interior lattice point. This is exactly the hypothesis needed for the first-block torus orthogonality calculation.

Let \(p-1=gd\), choose a generator \(u\) of \(\mathbf F_p^\times\), and let \(\zeta_d\) be a primitive \(d\)-th root of unity. For \(a\in(\mathbf Z/d\mathbf Z)^\times\), define

\[
\chi_a(u)=\zeta_d^a
\]

and extend every nontrivial multiplicative character by \(0\) at \(0\). Put

\[
H_{p,d,a}
 :=\sum_{(x,y,z)\in(\mathbf F_p^\times)^3}
   \chi_a\!\left(\Phi(x,y,z)\right)
 \in \mathbf Z[\zeta_d].
\tag{1.2}
\]

Because \(d\mid p-1\), the prime \(p\) splits completely in

\[
K_d=\mathbf Q(\zeta_d).
\]

The primes above \(p\) can be indexed by \(j\in(\mathbf Z/d\mathbf Z)^\times\):

\[
\mathfrak p_j=(p,\zeta_d-u^{gj}).
\tag{1.3}
\]

Every residue degree is one. If \([aj]_d\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}\) is the least positive residue, reduction at \(\mathfrak p_j\) sends

\[
\chi_a(v)\longmapsto v^{gaj}.
\]

Torus orthogonality and the unique-interior-point property then give the exact formula

\[
\boxed{
H_{p,d,a}\equiv -b_{g[aj]_d}\pmod{\mathfrak p_j}.
}
\tag{1.4}
\]

The sign is \((-1)^3=-1\), one factor from each torus coordinate. Since \(0<g[aj]_d<p-1\), no endpoint ambiguity occurs.

For \(c\in(\mathbf Z/d\mathbf Z)^\times\), let

\[
\sigma_c(\zeta_d)=\zeta_d^c.
\]

Directly from (1.2)--(1.3),

\[
\sigma_c(H_{p,d,a})=H_{p,d,ca},
\qquad
\sigma_c(\mathfrak p_j)=\mathfrak p_{jc^{-1}}.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Thus Galois conjugation moves the character and the prime above \(p\) simultaneously.

## 2. Exact reflection-square theorem

The Apéry reflection law is

\[
b_m\equiv b_{p-1-m}\pmod p,
\qquad 0\le m<p.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For \(r=ga\), its reflected index is

\[
p-1-r=g(d-a)=g[-a]_d.
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 — two cyclotomic primes, one scalar equation

Assume \(p\mid b_r\) and \(r\ne(p-1)/2\). Then \(d>2\), the primes

\[
\mathfrak p_1\ne\mathfrak p_{-1}
\]

are distinct, and

\[
H_{p,d,a}\in\mathfrak p_1\cap\mathfrak p_{-1}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Consequently, if \(H_{p,d,a}\ne0\),

\[
\boxed{
p^2\mid N_{K_d/\mathbf Q}(H_{p,d,a}).
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

#### Proof

From (1.4) at \(j=1\),

\[
H_{p,d,a}\equiv-b_r\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p_1}.
\]

At \(j=-1\), equations (1.4), (2.1), and (2.2) give

\[
H_{p,d,a}
 \equiv-b_{p-1-r}
 \equiv-b_r
 \equiv0
 \pmod{\mathfrak p_{-1}}.
\]

If \(\mathfrak p_1=\mathfrak p_{-1}\), then the primitive \(d\)-th roots \(u^g\) and \(u^{-g}\) would coincide, so \(d\le2\). Since \(r\ne(p-1)/2\) and \(r\ne0\) for a zero, this is excluded. Each prime has residue degree one, so the two local factors contribute at least \(p^2\) to the rational norm. ∎

### The equivalent inverse-character statement

At the one fixed prime \(\mathfrak p_1\),

\[
H_{p,d,a}\equiv-b_r,
\qquad
H_{p,d,-a}\equiv-b_{p-1-r}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Hence on the reflection locus

\[
(H_{p,d,a},H_{p,d,-a})\bmod\mathfrak p_1
 =(-b_r,-b_r).
\tag{2.6}
\]

The two inverse-character values lie on the diagonal. Their simultaneous vanishing has local rank one, not rank two.

The two descriptions are equivalent. Since

\[
H_{p,d,-a}=\sigma_{-1}(H_{p,d,a}),
\]

having both \(H_{p,d,a}\) and \(H_{p,d,-a}\) in \(\mathfrak p_1\) is the same as having \(H_{p,d,a}\) in both \(\mathfrak p_1\) and \(\mathfrak p_{-1}\).

### The central exception

If

\[
r=\frac{p-1}{2},
\]

then \(d=2\), \(a=-a\pmod d\), and reflection has a fixed point. There is only one relevant prime and no forced square:

\[
p\mid N(H)
\]

is the most reflection alone supplies. The endpoint \(r=0\) has \(b_0=1\), so it is never a target zero.

### Nonzero-carrier caveat

The ideal statement (2.3) remains true if \(H_{p,d,a}=0\), but then its norm is the zero integer and is useless as a height carrier. In the genuinely near-primitive regime this pathology is excluded for all sufficiently large \(p\): if \(H_{p,d,a}=0\) algebraically, all \(arphi(d)\) conjugates vanish, and (1.4) forces every primitive index \(gc\) to lie in \(Z_p\). The project's unconditional bound \(|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}\) contradicts this once \(arphi(d)>Cp^{2/3}\), in particular in any residual range \(d>p^{2/3+\delta}\).

## 3. Why \(p^2\) is not an amplification

There are two possible meanings of “two conjugates.” Both occur, and neither gives independence.

### 3.1 One algebraic integer, two distinct primes

Equation (2.3) says that the single integer \(H_{p,d,a}\) is divisible by two distinct primes above \(p\). This is a genuine ideal-theoretic statement. It is also exactly what complete splitting permits: different reductions of one algebraic integer can vanish at an arbitrary subset of the split primes.

The Chinese remainder theorem makes the logical limitation explicit. If

\[
p\mathcal O_K=\prod_{i=1}^e\mathfrak P_i
\]

splits completely, then for every subset \(S\subset\{1,\ldots,e\}\) there is an \(A\in\mathcal O_K\) with

\[
v_{\mathfrak P_i}(A)=1\ (i\in S),
\qquad
v_{\mathfrak P_i}(A)=0\ (i\notin S).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus complete splitting plus integrality never propagates divisibility beyond the primes already forced by a separate identity. Reflection supplies the subset \(\{1,-1\}\), and nothing larger.

### 3.2 Two conjugate integers, one fixed prime

Equation (2.6) says that \(H_a\) and \(H_{-a}\) both vanish at \(\mathfrak p_1\). But their residues are literally the same scalar \(b_r\). The second equation is obtained by applying the reflection identity to the first. There is no determinant, Jacobian, or codimension-two gain.

This is exactly the same phenomenon as the forced reflection factor in the project's continuant analysis: before saturation, every reflected pair contributes a universal factor. Removing that factor leaves a derivative/reflection-defect condition, not another ordinary zero.

## 4. Full cyclotomic field versus minimal self-dual trace field

The distinction between the full character field and the minimal trace field is load-bearing.

Suppose a normalized Mellin trace \(T_{p,d,a}\) satisfies the exact self-duality

\[
T_{p,d,a}=T_{p,d,-a}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then it lies in a subfield \(L\subseteq K_d^+\), where

\[
K_d^+=\mathbf Q(\zeta_d+\zeta_d^{-1}).
\]

Complex conjugation interchanges \(\mathfrak p_1\) and \(\mathfrak p_{-1}\), and those two primes contract to one prime \(\mathfrak q\) of \(K_d^+\), hence also to one prime of \(L\). The reflected zero is then just

\[
T_{p,d,a}\in\mathfrak q.
\tag{4.2}
\]

A simple zero gives

\[
v_p N_{L/\mathbf Q}(T_{p,d,a})=1,
\tag{4.3}
\]

not \(2\). If one unnecessarily norms from the full cyclotomic field,

\[
N_{K_d/\mathbf Q}(T)
 =N_{L/\mathbf Q}(T)^{[K_d:L]},
\tag{4.4}
\]

so the exponent is multiplied by the extension degree. The archimedean logarithmic height is multiplied by exactly the same degree.

For the raw \(H_{p,d,a}\), which need not itself be fixed by inversion, the relative norm

\[
U_{p,d,a}
 :=N_{K_d/K_d^+}(H_{p,d,a})
 =H_{p,d,a}H_{p,d,-a}
\tag{4.5}
\]

is an algebraic integer in \(K_d^+\). The reflected zero gives

\[
v_{\mathfrak q}(U_{p,d,a})\ge2.
\tag{4.6}
\]

But \(U_{p,d,a}\) is not rational. Obtaining a rational carrier still requires

\[
N_{K_d^+/\mathbf Q}(U_{p,d,a}),
\tag{4.7}
\]

whose degree is \(arphi(d)/2\). Calling (4.5) a “quadratic norm” therefore does not produce a bounded-degree rational integer; it merely performs the first degree-two step of a growing-degree descent.

## 5. Exact norm-height ledger

### 5.1 Unconditional raw bound

The sum (1.2) has at most \((p-1)^3\) terms, each of complex absolute value at most one. Therefore, for every complex embedding \(\sigma:K_d\hookrightarrow\mathbf C\),

\[
|\sigma(H_{p,d,a})|\le(p-1)^3.
\tag{5.1}
\]

If \(H_{p,d,a}\ne0\),

\[
\boxed{
\log\left|N_{K_d/\mathbf Q}(H_{p,d,a})\right|
 \le3\varphi(d)\log p.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

For the real relative norm \(U=H_aH_{-a}\), every conjugate has size at most \(p^6\), while the degree is \(arphi(d)/2\). Thus

\[
\log\left|N_{K_d^+/\mathbf Q}(U)\right|
 \le3\varphi(d)\log p,
\tag{5.3}
\]

the same ledger. The reflected \(p^2\) has not changed the divisibility-to-height ratio.

### 5.2 Best possible pure-trace bound still fails

Grant the strongest favorable normalization: a rank-two pure trace of weight three with

\[
|\sigma(T_{p,d,a})|\le2p^{3/2}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

If \(L\) is its minimal trace field of degree \(e\), then

\[
\log|N_{L/\mathbf Q}(T_{p,d,a})|
 \le e\bigl(\tfrac32\log p+\log2\bigr).
\tag{5.5}
\]

One simple zero contributes only \(\log p\). Squaring the trace or norming through the inversion pair contributes \(2\log p\) but doubles the right-hand side. There is again no gain.

If \(z\) distinct primes above \(p\) divide \(T\), (5.5) yields at best

\[
z\le\frac32e+O\!\left(\frac e{\log p}\right),
\tag{5.6}
\]

which is of the same order as the total number of available conjugates. The norm merely counts zeros already known to exist.

### 5.3 Near-primitive order is an archimedean liability

For a fixed integer \(n\), write

\[
n=qp+r,
\qquad q=\left\lfloor\frac np\right\rfloor.
\]

The selected character has exact order

\[
\boxed{
d_{p,n}
 =\frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,r)}
 =\frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,n-q)}.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

The project's divisor-sensitive order sieve removes, with total logarithmic weight \(o(n)\), all primes for which \(d_{p,n}\) is bounded or moderately growing. The residual primes have

\[
d_{p,n}=p^{1-o(1)}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Consequently

\[
\varphi(d_{p,n})=p^{1-o(1)}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

For such a prime, even the optimal pure norm has certified logarithmic height

\[
p^{1-o(1)}\log p
\]

against only one or two factors of \(p\). The growing Galois degree is not an amplifier; it is the dominant cost.

## 6. A universal orbit-carrier no-go theorem

The preceding norm calculation can be upgraded to an exact algebraic minimality statement.

### Lemma 6.1 — the orbit product is the first universal selective invariant

Let \(X_1,\ldots,X_e\) be the coordinates of a folded Galois orbit, so inverse characters have already been identified. Let

\[
F\in\mathbf Z[X_1,\ldots,X_e]
\]

be a Galois-invariant polynomial with the following universal selectivity property:

> for every commutative ring and every tuple \((x_1,\ldots,x_e)\), if any one \(x_i=0\), then \(F(x_1,\ldots,x_e)=0\).

Then

\[
F\in\bigcap_{i=1}^e(X_i)
 =(X_1X_2\cdots X_e).
\tag{6.1}
\]

In particular,

\[
\deg F\ge e.
\tag{6.2}
\]

#### Proof

Vanishing whenever \(X_i=0\) is exactly membership in the principal ideal \((X_i)\). The variables are pairwise coprime prime elements in the polynomial ring, so the intersection of their principal ideals is their product. ∎

For the inverse-folded primitive-character orbit,

\[
e\le\frac{\varphi(d)}2,
\]

with equality in the absence of extra stabilizers. The norm is precisely the orbit product in Lemma 6.1. Therefore:

* reflection reduces the orbit size by a factor two;
* it does not reduce it to bounded size;
* no bounded-degree invariant built only from the conjugate trace values can be universally target-selective.

A lower-degree carrier could exist only if the **actual Apéry orbit values satisfy an additional algebraic relation** that makes the universal polynomial-ring argument inapplicable. Reflection is already the one such relation being folded out. No further bounded-degree relation is supplied by the Kummer description, and the next section gives an actual Apéry counterexample to the obvious candidates.

## 7. Exact primitive-order counterexample: \(p=17,r=3\)

The recurrence gives

\[
(b_0,\ldots,b_{16})
\equiv
(1,5,5,0,4,13,8,8,16,8,8,13,4,0,5,5,1)
\pmod{17}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For \(r=3\),

\[
g=1,\qquad d=16,
\]

so the selected character has primitive order \(16\). The primitive indices are

\[
1,3,5,7,9,11,13,15.
\]

Their Apéry values are

\[
5,0,13,8,8,13,0,5.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Only \(3\) and \(-3=13\) vanish. Thus one primitive zero forces exactly its reflection mate and no other primitive-character conjugate.

### 7.1 Rational trace fails

Let \(H=H_{17,16,3}\). As \(c\) runs through \((\mathbf Z/16\mathbf Z)^\times\), the indices \(3c\) run through all primitive indices. By (1.4), at \(\mathfrak p_1\),

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{K_{16}/\mathbf Q}(H)
 \equiv
 -\sum_{c\in(\mathbf Z/16\mathbf Z)^\times}b_{[3c]_{16}}
 \equiv-52
 \equiv16
 \not\equiv0
 \pmod{17}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

So the simplest degree-one rational carrier, the trace, does not detect the target.

### 7.2 Trace of the inverse-pair product also fails

Put

\[
U=H_{17,16,3}H_{17,16,-3}\in K_{16}^+.
\]

The four real conjugates reduce to the squares of one representative from each reflection pair. Using representatives \(1,3,5,7\),

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{K_{16}^+/\mathbf Q}(U)
 \equiv
 b_1^2+b_3^2+b_5^2+b_7^2
 \equiv 5^2+0^2+13^2+8^2
 \equiv3
 \not\equiv0
 \pmod{17}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Thus even after explicitly pairing inverse characters, taking a rational trace does not give a carrier. The rational norm does, because it multiplies all four real conjugates and one of them is zero. This is exactly Lemma 6.1 in a live Apéry row.

The same example can be placed directly in the fixed-\(n\) top-half problem by taking

\[
n=20,\qquad p=17,\qquad q=1,\qquad r=3.
\]

Then

\[
\gcd(p-1,n-q)=\gcd(16,19)=1,
\]

so it lies in the smallest-kernel, largest-order residual range rather than in a harmless bounded-order slice.

## 8. Several small Galois orbits do not help

There are four natural variants. None supplies the requested carrier.

### 8.1 A bounded set of conjugates

Multiplying \(k=O(1)\) selected conjugates produces a bounded-degree expression, but it detects only zeros occurring in those selected coordinates. A rational construction cannot privilege one moving primitive coordinate: Galois invariance rotates the target through the whole orbit. Universal target selectivity therefore forces the full orbit product by Lemma 6.1.

### 8.2 Traces to intermediate subfields

A field trace is a sum, and a zero in one summand does not force the sum to vanish. Equations (7.3)--(7.4) refute the two most immediate trace constructions in an actual primitive-order Apéry fiber.

A subfield norm is selective, but its degree is the size of the corresponding orbit. Folding inversion changes \(arphi(d)\) to at best \(arphi(d)/2\), not to \(O(1)\).

### 8.3 Fixed small character orders

If \(d\le D\), then with \(m=n-q\), every candidate prime has

\[
p=1+dg,
\qquad g\mid m.
\tag{8.1}
\]

For fixed \(q\), there are at most \(D\tau(n-q)\) such candidates. Summed over bounded \(q\), their logarithmic weight is

\[
QD\,n^{o(1)}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Choosing \(D=n^{1-\varepsilon}/Q\) makes this \(n^{1-\varepsilon+o(1)}=o(n)\). Thus bounded-order or slowly growing-order orbits are already removed unconditionally. They are not the P3.2 obstruction.

### 8.4 Fixed rational slopes and special modular slices

A fixed rational slope \(r/(p-1)\) has bounded character order and may descend to a fixed hypergeometric motive or modular form. For fixed \(n\), such slices are arithmetically sparse and are covered by the same divisor parametrization. Pairing finitely many of them cannot cover the moving near-primitive residue \(r=n\bmod p\).

## 9. Gauss sums, Jacobi sums, and Hasse--Davenport

The Kummer trace can be embedded in a Gauss-sum expression. For a nontrivial additive character \(\psi\), define

\[
\mathcal S_{p,d,a}
 =\sum_{w\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
   \sum_{x\in(\mathbf F_p^\times)^3}
   \chi_a^{-1}(w)\psi(w\Phi(x)).
\tag{9.1}
\]

Summing first over \(w\) gives the exact factorization

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal S_{p,d,a}
 =g_p(\chi_a^{-1})H_{p,d,a},
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

where \(g_p(\chi)\) is a nonzero Gauss sum.

### 9.1 Gross--Koblitz removes the predictable factor

Gross--Koblitz writes \(g_p(\chi)\) as an explicit power of a Dwork uniformizer times a \(p\)-adic gamma unit. Dividing (9.2) by that nonzero factor leaves exactly \(H_{p,d,a}\). The Apéry condition \(p\mid b_r\) is cancellation in the unit-level coefficient, not a forced positive Stickelberger slope.

In the project's explicit balanced calculation, the zero-valuation leading coefficient is

\[
\sum_{k=0}^{\min(r,p-1-r)}
 \binom rk^2\binom{p-1-r}{k}^2
 \equiv b_r\pmod p.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Thus the target is an additive cancellation among many \(p\)-adic units.

### 9.2 Hasse--Davenport controls products, not cancellation of sums

Hasse--Davenport relates products such as

\[
\prod_{\rho^m=1}g_p(\chi\rho)
\]

to another Gauss sum and explicit nonzero factors. Finite hypergeometric values, however, are sums of products of Gauss or Jacobi sums. A multiplicative identity for the terms does not imply that cancellation of the whole sum propagates to the conjugate sums.

The \(p=17,r=3\) row is again a concrete refutation: the primitive-character hypergeometric trace vanishes at \(3\) and \(13\), but not at the other six conjugate primitive characters.

### 9.3 Bounded products of Jacobi sums

Stickelberger's ideal factorization gives bounded piecewise-linear valuations for each Jacobi factor; in the standard nontrivial case these local valuations are \(0\) or \(1\). A bounded product therefore has \(O(1)\) predictable valuation independent of \(arphi(d)\). After balanced normalization, those predictable blocks cancel. What remains is the same additive trace \(H_{p,d,a}\).

Accordingly, a bounded Jacobi product is either:

* nonzero and target-independent at the selected place;
* already carrying a predictable \(p\)-factor unrelated to \(b_r=0\); or
* one term inside an additive expression whose total cancellation is not multiplicatively detectable.

Norming the full additive expression returns to the growing-degree orbit norm already audited.

## 10. Why there is no fixed-\(n\) rational carrier

For the top half,

\[
T_n=\{p:n/2<p\le n,\ p\mid b_{n-p}\}.
\]

For each \(p\in T_n\), the reflection/Kummer construction produces a different field

\[
K_{d_{p,n}},
\]

a different algebraic integer \(H_{p,d_{p,n},a_{p,n}}\), and a different rational norm. The implication

\[
p\in T_n
 \Longrightarrow
 p^2\mid N_{K_{d_{p,n}}/\mathbf Q}(H_{p,d_{p,n},a_{p,n}})
\tag{10.1}
\]

is a correct per-prime statement, but it is not a common carrier.

There are only two ways to combine it:

1. **Multiply norms only over target primes.** This is circular because the product is defined using the unknown set \(T_n\).

2. **Multiply norms over all candidate primes.** This is target-blind, but the certified height contains the sum of the growing orbit degrees. In the near-primitive range it is vastly larger than \(n\), not \(o(n)\).

Even for the target-only product, the best norm ledger has the form

\[
2\sum_{p\in T_n}\log p
 \le
 \sum_{p\in T_n}\log|N_p|
 \le
 C\sum_{p\in T_n}\varphi(d_{p,n})\log p,
\tag{10.2}
\]

which is weaker than the desired estimate by the factors \(arphi(d_{p,n})=p^{1-o(1)}\). No cancellation between distinct rational norms is available: they live in different character fields and arise from different finite-field trace objects.

The elementary common carrier \(b_n\) remains available through Lucas congruences, but

\[
\log b_n
 =n\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log n),
\]

which is linear. The reflection norm does not lower this height scale.

## 11. Exact scope of the no-go result

The following statements are proved here.

| Statement | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Off-center \(p\mid b_r\) forces two distinct primes above \(p\) to divide the raw Kummer integer | **Yes** |
| The full cyclotomic norm is divisible by \(p^2\), when nonzero | **Yes** |
| The two inverse-character reductions are independent scalar equations | **No**; they are both \(b_r=0\) |
| The center \(r=(p-1)/2\) forces \(p^2\) | **No** |
| Minimal self-dual trace norm receives \(p^2\) from one reflected zero | **No**; a simple orbit contributes \(p\) |
| The quadratic inverse-pair norm is rational | **No**; it lies in \(K_d^+\) |
| A field trace supplies a target-selective rational carrier | **No**; \(p=17,r=3\) is an exact counterexample |
| A bounded number of conjugates supplies a universal rational carrier | **No**; Lemma 6.1 forces the orbit product |
| Full orbit norm has bounded degree in the residual range | **No**; \(d=p^{1-o(1)}\) |
| Gauss/Jacobi/Hasse--Davenport identities propagate the Apéry zero | **No** |
| The norm route yields \(\log R_n=o(n)\) | **No** |

What is **not** proved is an impossibility theorem against every conceivable future Apéry-specific identity. A lower-degree carrier could only arise from an additional algebraic relation among the actual conjugate trace values, or from a new relation coupling different rational primes. Such an identity is not a consequence of reflection or of the standard Kummer/Gauss/Jacobi formalism; it would be genuinely new arithmetic input.

---

## 12. Final conclusion for P3.2

Reflection has exactly one algebraic effect in the Kummer description:

\[
\text{one selected zero}
\quad\leadsto\quad
\text{the inverse-character copy of the same zero}.
\]

In the full split cyclotomic field this appears as two primes above \(p\) and therefore \(p^2\) in the norm. After passing to the natural self-dual quotient, it is one prime and one scalar condition. The apparent square is exactly balanced by the doubled field/height contribution.

The first universal rational operation that is genuinely target-selective is the norm over the entire inverse-folded Galois orbit. Its degree is \(arphi(d)/2\), and the fixed-\(n\) order sieve shows that the unresolved characters have \(d=p^{1-o(1)}\). Hence the rational norm carrier has the wrong height by a factor of essentially \(p\) per target prime and varies with \(p\).

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{minipage}{0.88\linewidth}
The reflected pair is a genuine \(p^2\)-divisibility statement only in the full cyclotomic norm, but it is not a pair of independent conjugate conditions. No bounded-degree or subexponential-height rational carrier follows from the pair, from finitely many Galois orbits, from traces, or from bounded Gauss/Jacobi constructions. The reflection/Kummer norm route cannot prove the pointwise P3.2 radical estimate. The remaining theorem must be horizontal and actual-Apéry-specific.
\end{minipage}
}
\]
