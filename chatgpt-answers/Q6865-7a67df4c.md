## 1. Correct cell coordinate and the mistake in Q6844

Use the exact two-block identity at the pole `X=-j`:

```text
r=j-1,
s=h-j,
r+s=h-1,

f_h(-j+z)
 = z^-3 F_r(-z)F_s(z)
   + z^3 G_r(-z)G_s(z).                              (1.1)
```

The canonical open cell to the right of `-j` is `0<Re z<1`. Reflection of the full height-`h` rational map sends this cell to its mirror cell. It does not send the two conjugate roots inside this cell to each other.

For odd `h=2m+1`, choosing `j=m+1` gives

```text
(r,s)=(m,m).
```

For even `h=2m`, the nearest nonmirror canonical cell has

```text
(r,s)=(m-1,m).
```

The limiting critical roots in that cell are

```text
xi_+=1/2+i y_*,
xi_-=conj(xi_+)=1/2-i y_*.
```

The old Q6844 pairing `xi <-> -xi` is a different symmetry. It pairs points in opposite cells and accounts for the exact mirror equality of squared critical values. It is not the small nonmirror separation measured by the Arb audit.

The corrected limiting symmetry is naturally stated for the squared cell map. If

```text
Phi(z)
 = z^-3 phi(-z)phi(z)+z^3 gamma(-z)gamma(z),
S(z)=Phi(z)^2,
```

then the two-sided cell problem has the midpoint symmetry

```text
S(1-z)=S(z),                                           (1.2)
```

and hence

```text
S(xi_+)=S(xi_-)=:W_* !=0,
S'(xi_+)=S'(xi_-)=0.                                  (1.3)
```

For the final proof, (1.2) should be checked from the exact two-sided Jost connection identities, not inferred from decimal roots. The local exact audit is consistent with it and uses precisely this branch geometry.

## 3. Exact central-pair calculation

Put

```text
M_(r,s)(z)=R_r(-z)R_s(z).                              (3.1)
```

The finite cell is, up to the solution-dependent analytic limiting cell function and exponentially small recessive contamination,

```text
Phi_(r,s)(z)=M_(r,s)(z)Phi(z).                         (3.2)
```

Because `Phi'(xi_±)=0`, the displacement of the finite critical point does not affect its critical value at first perturbative order. A perturbation of size `m^-2` moves the root by `O(m^-2)`, but the resulting change in `Phi` is quadratic, hence `O(m^-4)`.

For `h=2m+1`, `r=s=m`. Direct multiplication of (2.2)-(2.3) gives

## 4. The near-cluster coefficient lattice

The corrected geometry gives a much cleaner global-minimum mechanism.

Set

```text
H=h-1,
k=s-r>=0,
r=(H-k)/2,
s=(H+k)/2,
q=k+1.                                                 (4.1)
```

For odd `h`, admissible `q` are

```text
q=1,3,5,...,
```

while for even `h`, admissible `q` are

```text
q=2,4,6,....                                           (4.2)
```

After factoring out the common height scale, the common `H^-1` correction, and the common limiting value `W_*`, the two conjugate branches have the coefficient lattice

```text
A_(q,+)=A_common+3(q+2iy_*)^2,
A_(q,-)=A_common+3(q-2iy_*)^2.                         (4.3)
```

This follows from two contributions:

1. the fixed-height scale

```text
(b_r b_s)^2
 ~ const_H [r s]^-3,
```

which contributes `3k^2/H^2` relative to the central scale;

2. the cell multiplier, which contributes

```text
12(k xi_±+xi_±^2)/H^2.
```

Their sum is

```text
3k^2+12(kxi_±+xi_±^2)
 =3(k+1+-2iy_*)^2.                                    (4.4)
```

### 4.1 Exact minimum of the coefficient lattice

For a fixed `q`, the conjugate separation is

```text
|A_(q,+)-A_(q,-)|=24q y_*.                             (4.5)
```

It is minimized at the smallest admissible `q`:

```text
q=1 for odd h,
q=2 for even h.                                        (4.6)
```

For distinct `q,q'` with the same sign,

```text
|A_(q,sigma)-A_(q',sigma)|
 =3|q-q'| sqrt((q+q')^2+16y_*^2).                     (4.7)
```

For opposite signs,

```text
|A_(q,+)-A_(q',-)|
 =3(q+q') sqrt((q-q')^2+16y_*^2).                     (4.8)
```

The smallest competitors are therefore

```text
odd:
```

Thus the central conjugate pair has a very large coefficient-level safety margin. The global-minimum problem is no longer a delicate contest between several coefficients near `2.65` or `5.30`; the next near-cluster coefficient is already above `24` or `36`.

## 5. Explicit analytic remainder: a Riccati majorant

This is the part that must replace the phrase “standard Birkhoff”.

Write the shifted recurrence as

```text
Y_(n+1)=a_n(z)Y_n-b_n(z)Y_(n-1),                      (5.1)

a_n(z)=P(n+z)/(n+z+1)^3,
b_n(z)=(n+z)^3/(n+z+1)^3,
P(t)=34t^3+51t^2+27t+5.                               (5.2)
```

Let `S_n(z)=Y_n^[4](z)` be the explicit fourth-order approximant (2.7), and put

```text
p_n(z)=S_n(z)/S_(n-1)(z).                              (5.3)
```

Its exact Riccati defect is

```text
delta_n(z)
 =a_n(z)-b_n(z)/p_n(z)-p_(n+1)(z).                    (5.4)
```

For an exact solution with `Y_(n-1)` nonzero on the working domain, define

```text
q_n(z)=Y_n(z)/Y_(n-1)(z).                              (5.5)
```

Then

```text
q_(n+1)=a_n-b_n/q_n.                                   (5.6)
```

Let, on a compact analytic domain `Omega`,

```text
L_n=inf_Omega |p_n|,
B_n=sup_Omega |b_n|,
D_n=sup_Omega |delta_n|.                               (5.7)
```

If

```text
sup_Omega |q_n-p_n| <= E_n < L_n,                     (5.8)
```

then the exact error recurrence is

```text
E_(n+1)
 <= D_n+B_n E_n/[L_n(L_n-E_n)].                       (5.9)
```

This is the requested computable majorant recurrence. It is a one-line consequence of

```text
1/p_n-1/q_n=(q_n-p_n)/(p_n q_n).                      (5.10)
```

It has three advantages here:

1. the contraction factor is approximately `lambda^-2`, numerically about `8.7*10^-4`;
2. every quantity in (5.7) is an exact rational-algebraic interval evaluation;
3. no convergence of the full formal Birkhoff series is assumed.

## 6. Critical-root trapping and value perturbation

Use disjoint rational disks

```text
D_+=D(xi_+,rho),
```

with `rho` chosen by interval Newton for the limiting derivative `Phi'`.

```text
m_2=inf_(D_+ union D_-) |Phi''| >0,
M_0=sup |Phi|,
M_1=sup |Phi'|,
W_min=inf |Phi|^2 >0,                                 (6.2)
```

and a boundary Rouché margin

```text
tau_D=inf_(partial D_+ union partial D_-) |Phi'|>0.   (6.3)
```

If the finite cell perturbation `E_(r,s)` satisfies

```text
||E_(r,s)'|| < tau_D,                                 (6.4)
```

then each disk contains exactly one finite critical point. Moreover the root displacement is bounded by

```text
|z_(r,s,±)-xi_±|
 <= 2||E_(r,s)'||/m_2,                                (6.5)
```

provided the right side is smaller than `rho`.

For the critical value,

```text
Phi_(r,s)(z_(r,s,±))
 =Phi_(r,s)(xi_±)
  +O(||E_(r,s)'||^2/m_2).                             (6.6)
```

The absence of a first-order root-shift contribution is the key reason the explicit multiplier computation survives unchanged.

Combining (5.22)-(5.25) for the four factors

## 7. `NEAR / FAR / EDGE` global-minimum partition

The global theorem should be organized by the type of limiting comparison, not merely by adjacency of cells.

### 7.1 NEAR: the coalescing conjugate cluster

`NEAR` consists of comparisons among the two critical branches converging to the common squared value `W_*`, in cells with both blocks long:

```text
r,s>=N, N=30.                                         (7.1)
```

The coefficient lattice (4.3) handles all central and near-central comparisons. The exact second-order formula (4.11)-(4.14) handles the same cluster uniformly when `k` is not fixed.

The proof has two steps:

1. use (4.14) and the remainder (6.8) to prove that the same-cell conjugate gap is increasing with `k`;
2. use (4.7)-(4.8), with interval remainder disks, to prove that every cross-cell cluster difference exceeds the central same-cell difference.

Because the next coefficient is above `24` or `36`, while the central coefficient is `2.65` or `5.30`, the interval remainder may be quite coarse and still preserve the ordering.

### 7.2 FAR: distinct limiting squared values or separated scales

Let `w_1,w_2,w_3` be the limiting squared critical values in one cell, with

```text
w_2=w_3=W_*
```

for the conjugate pair and `w_1` the remaining branch value. The limiting interval certificate must give

```text
delta_branch
 =min(|w_1-W_*|, |w_1|, |W_*|)>0.                    (7.2)
```

All comparisons involving `w_1` are then `FAR`: their leading separation is order one after the natural cell scale is removed. They are stable under an `O(1/N)` perturbation.

For cells whose scale ratio is not close to one, use the exact positive scale

```text
S_(r,s)=(b_r b_s)^2.                                  (7.3)
```

The Apéry ratios are positive, and the canonical cell scales increase away from the center. It is enough to enclose, for each limiting branch pair, the one-dimensional ray distance

```text
inf_(q in I) |q w_a-w_b|,                             (7.4)
```

where `I` is the certified interval of adjacent or nonadjacent scale ratios. This is a finite complex interval problem. Nonadjacent cells need no separate argument once adjacent scale bands are disjoint in the ordered envelope.

For the coalescing cluster with scale ratio close to one, return to `NEAR`; that is exactly what the lattice (4.3) was designed to handle.

### 7.3 EDGE: one block short

If

```text
min(r,s)<N,                                            (7.5)
```

the two-sided expansion must not be used. Keep the short block exact and expand only the long block.

For fixed `r<N` and `s->infinity`, the one-sided limiting cell is

```text
Phi_r^edge(z)
 =z^-3 [F_r(-z)/b_r] phi(z)
  +z^3 [G_r(-z)/b_r] gamma(z).                        (7.6)
```

There are only `N=30` such functions, together with their reflected versions. For each `0<=r<30`, interval Newton must certify:

```text
- all relevant critical roots of Phi_r^edge;
- critical-root simplicity;
- nonzero squared critical values;
- internal squared-value separation;
- separation from the two-sided bulk branch bands.    (7.7)
```

The long-block error is then controlled by the same Riccati majorant. The edge cell scale is polynomially larger than the central scale, so the edge is not expected to be the minimum, but that fact should be proved by the finite interval family (7.6), not asserted from asymptotic intuition.
