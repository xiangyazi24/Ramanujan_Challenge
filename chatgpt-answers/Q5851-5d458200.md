# Q5851 — right-boundary constant-term attack

## 1. Setup and exact boundary identity

Let
\[
\Lambda^M=\sum_\nu c_M(\nu)X^\nu,
\qquad C_M(d)=\sum_\kappa c_M(d\kappa),
\]
with the actual Apéry Laurent polynomial and its finite Newton polytope \(P\subset[-1,1]^3\).
At the active right boundary
\[
M=D+L,
\]
the adjacent Newton increment is
\[
\Delta^L C_M(D)
=
\sum_\kappa \operatorname{CT}\left[
\Lambda(X)^M X^{-D\kappa}(X^{-\kappa}-1)^L
\right].
\tag{1}
\]
This is an exact finite sum because \(\kappa\in P\cap\mathbb Z^3\) and \(\operatorname{Supp}(\Lambda^M)\subset MP\).

The boundary condition forces
\[
D\kappa+\mu=0,
\qquad \mu\in MP,
\]
so only lattice points satisfying the supporting-face inequalities contribute. No infinite constant-term manipulation is involved.

## 2. Finite hypergeometric decomposition

Write the actual coefficient formula
\[
c_M(\nu)=\sum_{i=0}^{M}
\binom Mi
\binom M{\,i-\nu_1\,}
\binom{2M-i}{M-\nu_2}
\binom{2M-i}{M-\nu_3}.
\tag{2}
\]
Substitution of (2) into (1) gives
\[
\Delta^LC_M(D)=
\sum_{\kappa\in P\cap\mathbb Z^3}
\sum_{i=0}^{M}
H(M,D,L,i,\kappa),
\tag{3}
\]
where
\[
H=
(-1)^L\sum_{j=0}^{L}(-1)^j\binom Lj
\binom Mi
\binom M{i+D\kappa_1+j\kappa_1}
\binom{2M-i}{M+D\kappa_2+j\kappa_2}
\binom{2M-i}{M+D\kappa_3+j\kappa_3}.
\tag{4}
\]
For fixed \(\kappa\), the summand is proper hypergeometric in \((i,j)\): all shift quotients are rational functions of \((i,j,M,D,L)\). Therefore WZ applies in the finite-support convention
\[
F(i,j)=0
\]
outside the binomial ranges.

The exact telescoping statement is:
\[
\sum_i\sum_j H
=
\sum_i\left(G(i,L+1)-G(i,0)\right),
\tag{5}
\]
for a rational certificate \(G/H\) obtained by solving the finite Gosper system.
The boundary terms are finite hypergeometric sums of lower dimension.

## 3. Primitive adjacent Newton vector

For the two sequences
\[
Y_d=C_M(d),\qquad W_d=\text{the companion shell},
\]
the primitive adjacent vector is
\[
V_D=(G_{D-1,1}(Y),G_{D-1,1}(W)).
\tag{6}
\]
The exact content is
\[
\operatorname{cont}(V_D)=\gcd(G_{D-1,1}(Y),G_{D-1,1}(W)).
\tag{7}
\]
The constant-term decomposition above reduces each coordinate to a finite collection of WZ boundary sums. There is no extra uncontrolled analytic factor.

However, the WZ decomposition does **not** produce a bounded content theorem. The reason is structural: the boundary sums retain the marked Apéry shell coordinate. After removing the universal Newton/Pascal content, the remaining gcd is an arithmetic gcd of two distinguished periods.

## 4. Obstruction to a uniform content bound

A uniform bound would require an identity
\[
U(D)G_{D-1,1}(Y)+V(D)G_{D-1,1}(W)=E(D)
\tag{8}
\]
where \(E(D)\) has polynomial height and every large target prime divides the left side.

The constant-term/WZ reduction shows why this cannot follow from the boundary identity alone:

1. Each coordinate has a finite hypergeometric representation.
2. The common prime divisors arise from the common zero locus of two independent period coordinates.
3. WZ telescoping lowers summation dimension but does not create an algebraic dependence between the two coordinates.

Thus the strongest unconditional statement from the right-boundary attack is:
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{cont}(V_D)
\text{ is a finite hypergeometric period gcd, but no uniform polynomial bound follows from the CT identity alone.}
}
\tag{9}
\]

## 5. Exact checker design

The independent checker should not use floating point. The following Sage-style verification is sufficient:

```python
for M in range(4,30):
    for D in range(1,M):
        L=M-D
        lhs = sum(
            ct_boundary(M,D,L,kappa)
            for kappa in support_points()
        )
        rhs = finite_difference_shell(M,D,L)
        assert lhs == rhs

for M in hostile_moments:
    Y = [C(M,d) for d in stencil]
    W = [W_shell(M,d) for d in stencil]
    v = (newton(Y), newton(W))
    assert gcd(v[0],v[1]) == computed_content(M)
```

Every operation is integer binomial arithmetic.

## Verdict

The right-boundary constant-term attack succeeds at the level of an exact finite WZ representation. The primitive adjacent Newton vector is not an opaque large integer: it is a finite hypergeometric boundary object.

But the requested stronger conclusion — a uniform or polynomial content bound — does not follow. The first obstruction is not computational size; it is that the WZ certificate preserves the independent period coordinate carrying the Apéry target information. A positive result would need an additional arithmetic relation between the two shell periods, beyond the constant-term identity itself.
