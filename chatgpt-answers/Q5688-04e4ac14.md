# 8. Scoped no-go theorem

## Theorem 8.1 -- finite recurrence-module saturation

Fix n,a,h and a direct or reflected affine branch. Consider the class of scalar constructions obtained by:

1. adjoining finitely many recurrence states on the interval between the marked indices;

1. adjoining the Apéry companion and its exact Casoratian;

1. adjoining finitely many spectral/index jets;

1. using continuant transfer, addition, Dodgson, Pluecker, or exterior-power identities;

1. eliminating state variables by determinants, Fitting ideals, Bezout identities, resultants, or subresultants;

1. dividing every factor declared universal or invertible on the regular pure-cross locus.

Then the primitive saturated scalar elimination ideal is zero, while any artificial same-characteristic endpoint elimination has unit ideal. In particular no nonzero C_{n,a,h} in this class is forced to be divisible by q, \ell, or q\ell by the pure-cross hypotheses.

### Proof

Sections 3--4 show that the endpoint block is invertible in both characteristics and that each incidence chart is a Laurent polynomial ring. Section 5 shows that every permitted enlargement is affine over those rings or has a block-triangular transfer with the same invertible diagonal block. Elimination from a Laurent polynomial ring contracts to zero. If the two endpoint equations are placed in one characteristic, their determinant is the saturated unit N_d. All listed determinant and subresultant operations are functorial consequences of these two statements. \square
