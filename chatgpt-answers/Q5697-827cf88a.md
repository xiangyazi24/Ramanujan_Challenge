# Q5697 — nonlinear distinguished-state audit for the Apéry Cartier packet

## Executive verdict

The distinguished state \(H=\Lambda^a\) does give an exact nonlinear refinement of the Cartier packet, but it does **not** produce a short cross-prime carrier.

Let

\[
 \mathcal C_p\!\left(\sum_v f_vX^v\right)
   =\sum_\mu f_{p\mu}X^\mu,
 \qquad
 P=\Lambda,
 \qquad
 n=ap+r,\quad 0\le r<p,
\]

and put

\[
 A_{p,n}:=\mathcal C_p(P^n)
          =\sum_\mu c_n(p\mu)X^\mu.
\]

The exact nonlinear expansion is

\[
 \boxed{
 A_{p,n}
 =\sum_{j=0}^{a}\binom ajp^jP^{a-j}J^{(j)}_{p,r},
 \qquad
 J^{(j)}_{p,r}
 =\mathcal C_p(\Psi_p^jP^r),
 }
\tag{1}
\]

where

\[
 \Psi_p=\frac{P^p-P(X^p)}p\in\mathbf Z[X_1^{\pm1},X_2^{\pm1},X_3^{\pm1}].
\]

Here \(J^{(0)}_{p,r}=b_r\), and \(J^{(j)}_{p,r}\) is supported in the box \([-j,j]^3\). Thus

\[
 A_{p,n}\equiv b_rP^a\pmod p
\]

is only the zeroth layer of a full osculating expansion of the power state.

At a target \(p\mid b_r\), writing \(b_r=pq_{p,r}\), the first divided packet is

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{A_{p,n}}p
 \equiv
 P^{a-1}\bigl(q_{p,r}P+aJ^{(1)}_{p,r}\bigr)
 \pmod p.}
\tag{2}
\]

This is the strongest exact positive statement found in this route. It says that the divided packet lies in the tangent space to the power map at \(P^a\).

The decisive negative fact is that the tangent and higher-osculating congruences are **universal**, not target-selective. Modulo \(p^2\), the whole packet lies in

\[
 P^{a-1}\,\mathbf Z[X^{\pm1}]_{[-1,1]^3}
\]

for every residue \(r\), whether or not \(p\mid b_r\). After these universal factors are saturated, the only selective grade is still the scalar \(b_r\). The quotient \(q_{p,r}P+aJ^{(1)}_{p,r}\) is a free first Frobenius state; targetness does not force it to vanish or to satisfy a second scalar equation.

This yields a precise scoped no-go:

> Any bounded-degree identity obtained functorially from the Cartier relation, the power law \(H=P^a\), convolution, Hadamard products, Gram/Parseval forms, projective minors, or resultants either retains a power of the one scalar \(b_r\), or cancels that scalar and becomes a universal Frobenius/osculating congruence. It cannot both cancel the free state and retain a new target-selective factor.

There is a trivial degree-two cross-prime carrier. For any packet coordinates \(\mu,\nu\),

\[
 D_{\mu,\nu}(p,\ell)
 =c_n(p\mu)c_n(\ell\nu)-c_n(p\nu)c_n(\ell\mu)
\tag{3}
\]

satisfies

\[
 p\ell\mid D_{\mu,\nu}(p,\ell)
\]

whenever \(p\mid b_{n-ap}\) and \(\ell\mid b_{n-a\ell}\). But it can vanish, and the only unconditional height bound is

\[
 \log|D_{\mu,\nu}(p,\ell)|
 \le 2n\log40+O(1),
\tag{4}
\]

which is vastly larger than \(H=n^{1/3}\). Adding tangent columns cancels the common state, but then the resulting determinant is divisible by the same prime powers **for every prime**, so all selectivity is lost.

The exposed-face scalar makes the height obstruction concrete. The Laurent polynomial \(P\) has coefficient \(1\) at the unique exposed vertex

\[
 v=(1,1,1)
\]

and coefficient \(5\) at the zero step. Therefore

\[
 S_{p,n}:=c_n(pa\,v)=c_n((n-r)v)
 \ge \binom nr5^r.
\tag{5}
\]

It satisfies \(S_{p,n}\equiv b_r\pmod p\), so it is the primitive exposed selective packet coordinate. In the unresolved interior, where \(r\ge H\),

\[
 \log S_{p,n}\ge r\log5\ge H\log5.
\tag{6}
\]

Thus exposed-face products, squares, positive Parseval norms, and subtraction-free convolution carriers already have an \(\Omega(H)\) height floor, not \(o(H)\). The small-\(r\) cases are exactly the boundary region already handled by the existing folded-boundary carriers.

No integer \(D_{n,a,h}\) with \(p(p+h)\mid D_{n,a,h}\) for every pure-cross pair and \(\log|D_{n,a,h}|=o(H)\) is obtained. The route can reopen only through a genuinely special arithmetic identity involving the actual first Frobenius defects

\[
 J^{(1)}_{p,n-ap},
 \qquad
 J^{(1)}_{p+h,n-a(p+h)}
\]

across two residue characteristics. Such an identity is not a consequence of the power state itself.

## 2. Cartier notation and the exact packet identity

Write

\[
 P(X)=\Lambda(X)
 =\sum_{s\in\mathbf Z^3}m_sX^s,
 \qquad
 c_m(v)=[X^v]P^m.
\tag{7}
\]

The support of \(P\) is contained in \([-1,1]^3\), and

\[
 [X^0]P^m=b_m.
\tag{8}
\]

For a prime \(p\), define the centered Cartier operator

\[
 \mathcal C_p(F)(X)
 =\sum_{\mu\in\mathbf Z^3}[X^{p\mu}]F(X)\,X^\mu.
\tag{9}
\]

It satisfies the exact projection rule

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal C_p\bigl(G(X^p)F(X)\bigr)
 =G(X)\mathcal C_p(F(X)).}
\tag{10}
\]

If \(n=ap+r\), \(0\le r<p\), then the freshman's-dream congruence gives

\[
 P^n
 \equiv P(X^p)^aP^r\pmod p.
\tag{11}
\]

Since the support of \(P^r\) is contained in \([-r,r]^3\), and \(r<p\), the only exponent of \(P^r\) in \(p\mathbf Z^3\) is zero. Hence

\[
 \mathcal C_p(P^r)=b_r
\tag{12}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 A_{p,n}:=\mathcal C_p(P^n)
 \equiv b_rP^a\pmod p.}
\tag{13}
\]

Coefficientwise, (13) is exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 c_n(p\mu)\equiv b_r c_a(\mu)\pmod p.}
\tag{14}
\]

This formula is valid also at \(r=0\) and \(r=p-1\); no division by \(P(u)\) or exclusion of zero values is used. For the target problem, the boundary residues are harmless because \(b_0=1\) and \(b_{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p\).

## 3. Exact nonlinear expansion around the power state

The mod-\(p\) packet suppresses the first Frobenius defect. Keep it integrally by defining

\[
 \Psi_p(X)
 =\frac{P(X)^p-P(X^p)}p.
\tag{15}
\]

The numerator has coefficients divisible by \(p\), so \(\Psi_p\) is an integral Laurent polynomial. The identity

\[
 P^p=P(X^p)+p\Psi_p
\tag{16}
\]

is exact, not merely a congruence.

Now expand

\[
 P^{ap+r}
 =\bigl(P(X^p)+p\Psi_p\bigr)^aP^r.
\tag{17}
\]

Applying (10) term by term gives the following theorem.

### Theorem 3.1 — exact nonlinear Cartier packet expansion

For every prime \(p\), every \(a\ge0\), and every \(0\le r<p\),

\[
 \boxed{
 A_{p,ap+r}
 =\sum_{j=0}^{a}
   \binom ajp^jP^{a-j}J^{(j)}_{p,r},}
\tag{18}
\]

where

\[
 \boxed{
 J^{(j)}_{p,r}
 =\mathcal C_p(\Psi_p^jP^r).}
\tag{19}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 J^{(0)}_{p,r}=b_r,
\tag{20}
\]

and

\[
 \operatorname{supp}J^{(j)}_{p,r}
 \subseteq[-j,j]^3.
\tag{21}
\]

#### Proof

Equation (18) is the binomial expansion of (17) followed by (10). For (21), \(\Psi_p\) is supported in \(p[-1,1]^3\), so \(\Psi_p^jP^r\) is supported in

\[
 [-(jp+r),jp+r]^3.
\]

After Cartier extraction and division of exponents by \(p\), only integral coordinates between \(-j\) and \(j\) occur because \(0\le r<p\). ∎

The first two grades are

\[
 A_{p,n}
 =b_rP^a
  +apP^{a-1}J^{(1)}_{p,r}
  +p^2\binom a2P^{a-2}J^{(2)}_{p,r}
  +\cdots.
\tag{22}
\]

This is the exact nonlinear information special to \(H=P^a\).

## 4. The first target jet is tangent, but remains free

Assume \(p\mid b_r\) and write

\[
 b_r=pq_{p,r}.
\tag{23}
\]

Dividing (22) by \(p\) and reducing modulo \(p\) gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{A_{p,n}}p
 \equiv
 P^{a-1}Q_{p,r}\pmod p,}
\tag{24}
\]

with

\[
 \boxed{
 Q_{p,r}=q_{p,r}P+aJ^{(1)}_{p,r},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{supp}Q_{p,r}\subseteq[-1,1]^3.}
\tag{25}
\]

Let

\[
 V_1
 =\{Q:\operatorname{supp}Q\subseteq[-1,1]^3\}.
\tag{26}
\]

Then

\[
 T_a:=P^{a-1}V_1
\tag{27}
\]

is exactly the tangent image of the power map

\[
 \nu_a:V_1\longrightarrow V_a,
 \qquad F\longmapsto F^a,
\tag{28}
\]

at \(P\), up to the unit factor \(a\):

\[
 d\nu_a|_P(Q)=aP^{a-1}Q.
\tag{29}
\]

Thus the nonlinear state has reduced the first divided packet to a fixed \(27\)-dimensional tangent space.

That sounds like extra codimension, but it is not a second target equation.

### Proposition 4.1 — universal tangent saturation

Modulo \(p^2\), one has

\[
 \boxed{A_{p,n}\in T_a+p^2V_a}
\tag{30}
\]

for **every** \(r\), not only for target residues.

#### Proof

Both terms

\[
 b_rP^a=P^{a-1}(b_rP)
\]

and

\[
 apP^{a-1}J^{(1)}_{p,r}
\]

in (22) lie in \(T_a\). ∎

Consequently, every linear functional annihilating \(T_a\) gives a congruence modulo \(p^2\) for every prime and every residue. Such a congruence is a universal Frobenius congruence; it is not target-selective.

At a target, the normalized packet (24) ranges over the same tangent space, with the quotient \(Q_{p,r}\) left unconstrained by targetness. The scalar \(q_{p,r}=b_r/p\) has not disappeared; it has merely joined the free first Frobenius defect \(J^{(1)}_{p,r}\).

This is the nonlinear packet analogue of the repository's rank-one first Dwork jet.

## 5. The tangent lattice is already primitively saturated

The preceding statement is not hiding a useful integral index.

The support of \(P\) has the unique exposed vertex

\[
 v=(1,1,1),
 \qquad [X^v]P=1.
\tag{31}
\]

Consider the integral multiplication map

\[
 m_{a-1}:V_1(\mathbf Z)\longrightarrow V_a(\mathbf Z),
 \qquad Q\longmapsto P^{a-1}Q.
\tag{32}
\]

### Lemma 5.1 — unit minor for the tangent lattice

The coefficient matrix of \(m_{a-1}\) has a \(27\times27\) minor equal to \(1\). Hence \(T_a(\mathbf Z)\) is a primitive sublattice of its rational span.

#### Proof

Index the columns by the \(27\) monomials \(X^s\), \(s\in[-1,1]^3\). Choose an integral linear functional \(w\) which uniquely exposes \(v\) on the support of \(P\) and separates all \(s\)'s; for example, after a harmless lexicographic refinement one may use a sufficiently rapidly weighted positive functional.

Take rows indexed by

\[
 (a-1)v+s.
\]

The entry in row \((a-1)v+s\), column \(t\), is

\[
 [X^{(a-1)v+s-t}]P^{a-1}.
\]

If it is nonzero, exposedness gives \(w(s)\le w(t)\). Ordered by \(w\), the submatrix is triangular. Its diagonal entries are

\[
 [X^{(a-1)v}]P^{a-1}=1.
\]

Thus its determinant is \(1\). ∎

Therefore Smith saturation of the tangent packet removes no hidden target factor. The transverse quotient is torsion-free, and Proposition 4.1 says that it carries a universal \(p^2\), not a selective one.

There is an even sharper obstruction in the top quotient branch.

### Corollary 5.2 — no nonlinear gain when \(a=1\)

For \(a=1\),

\[
 T_1=V_1.
\tag{33}
\]

The first divided packet condition (24) imposes no equation at all: every packet supported in \([-1,1]^3\) is tangent. Thus the top-half branch, already a necessary hard core of P3.2, receives no nonlinear power-state improvement from this construction.

This alone prevents the route from proving the full theorem without an independent top-half argument.

## 6. Higher osculating filtration

For \(j\ge0\), let

\[
 V_j=\{Q:\operatorname{supp}Q\subseteq[-j,j]^3\}
\]

and define

\[
 \mathcal O_{a,k}
 =\sum_{j=0}^{k}P^{a-j}V_j.
\tag{34}
\]

Equation (18) gives the exact filtration

\[
 A_{p,n}
 \in
 \sum_{j=0}^{k}p^jP^{a-j}V_j
 +p^{k+1}V_a.
\tag{35}
\]

The extra congruences obtained by projecting away \(\mathcal O_{a,k}\) are again universal. At grade \(j\), the new coordinate is \(J^{(j)}_{p,r}\); targetness only affects the zeroth scalar \(J^{(0)}_{p,r}=b_r\).

The associated-graded picture is therefore:

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{grade}&\text{state}&\text{target effect}\\ \hline
 0&b_rP^a&p\mid b_r\\
 1&aP^{a-1}J^{(1)}_{p,r}&\text{none}\\
 2&\binom a2P^{a-2}J^{(2)}_{p,r}&\text{none}\\
 \vdots&\vdots&\vdots
 \end{array}
\tag{36}
\]

A finite-order osculating identity can increase a universal \(p\)-adic valuation, but after universal saturation it does not create a new equation on \(b_r/p\).

This statement is deliberately scoped. It proves saturation for identities generated by the exact Cartier expansion and the power-state osculating flag. It does **not** rule out a special arithmetic identity among the actual Laurent polynomials \(J^{(j)}_{p,r}\) for the specific Apéry \(P\). Such an identity would have to use more than the fact that \(H\) is a power.

## 7. Bounded-degree polynomial identities: exact dichotomy

Let the packet coefficient vector be denoted by \(A\), and let

\[
 h=(c_a(\mu))_\mu
\]

be the coefficient vector of \(P^a\). Modulo \(p\),

\[
 A=b_rh.
\tag{37}
\]

Consider any polynomial expression \(F(A,h)\), and decompose it into homogeneous pieces in the packet variables \(A\).

### Proposition 7.1 — scalar-or-universal dichotomy

For every homogeneous component of packet degree \(d\),

\[
 F_d(A,h)
 \equiv b_r^dF_d(h,h)\pmod p
\tag{38}
\]

whenever all packet slots are filled by the same packet vector.

Consequently:

1. If \(F_d(h,h)\ne0\), targetness contributes only the scalar power \(b_r^d\).
2. If \(F_d(h,h)=0\), the mod-\(p\) vanishing is universal for every residue, because it is a projective or power-state syzygy.
3. After dividing the universal factor, the first surviving term is a derivative of \(F\) in the tangent direction \(P^{a-1}Q_{p,r}\), and \(Q_{p,r}\) is not constrained by targetness.

Thus a bounded-degree identity can retain the scalar target factor or cancel the state projectively, but not both in an independent way.

This proposition covers the natural nonlinear operations in the question.

### 7.1 Convolution

For every \(d\ge1\),

\[
 A^{*d}\equiv b_r^d(P^a)^{*d}\pmod p.
\tag{39}
\]

Any convolution coefficient gives a power of \(b_r\). Eliminating \((P^a)^{*d}\) by comparing two convolution covariants produces a universal syzygy.

### 7.2 Hadamard products and Parseval forms

For example,

\[
 \sum_\mu A_\mu^2
 \equiv
 b_r^2\sum_\mu h_\mu^2\pmod p.
\tag{40}
\]

This is a repeated target factor, not a second equation. The centered Gram determinant

\[
 \left(\sum A_\mu^2\right)
 \left(\sum h_\mu^2\right)
 -\left(\sum A_\mu h_\mu\right)^2
\tag{41}
\]

cancels the scalar line, but then it vanishes modulo \(p\) for every residue, not only targets.

### 7.3 Minors and total positivity

Every \(2\times2\) minor formed solely from copies of \(A\) is homogeneous of degree two and carries \(b_r^2\). A minor of \([h,A]\) cancels the scalar line and is universally divisible by \(p\). Its first divided value is controlled by the free tangent vector, not by targetness.

Even a proof of strict total positivity or log-concavity would give signs and nonvanishing, not a short modular carrier. It would not change the scalar-or-universal dichotomy.

### 7.4 Resultants and discriminants

Resultants and discriminants are homogeneous in the input coefficients. If they retain the packet content, they carry a power of \(b_r\). If one forms a resultant specifically to remove the common factor \(P^{a-1}\), the resulting divisibility is part of the universal osculating filtration. The normalized resultant depends on the free quotient \(Q_{p,r}\).

## 8. Products and squares do not produce a second target coordinate

At a target, every packet coefficient is divisible by \(p\). Therefore any degree-\(d\) polynomial with no constant term is automatically divisible by at least one power of \(p\), and a homogeneous degree-\(d\) packet polynomial is divisible by \(p^d\).

This valuation amplification is tautological. Dividing by the forced power gives a polynomial in the divided packet

\[
 U_{p,r}:=A_{p,n}/p.
\]

Formula (24) gives

\[
 U_{p,r}\equiv P^{a-1}Q_{p,r}\pmod p.
\tag{42}
\]

A square or product of packet coefficients becomes a square or product of the coordinates of \(Q_{p,r}\). There is no target equation on those coordinates.

If one instead uses polynomial equations defining the tangent or osculating image, those equations vanish for every Frobenius packet after the corresponding universal \(p\)-power is removed. Hence they no longer detect targetness.

This is the exact point at which “cancel the free Frobenius state” collapses:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{retaining targetness leaves }Q_{p,r};
 \quad
 \text{eliminating }Q_{p,r}\text{ removes target selectivity}.}
\tag{43}
\]

## 9. A minimal cross-prime carrier and why it is insufficient

Let \(\ell=p+h\) lie in the same quotient block, so

\[
 n=ap+r=a\ell+s,
 \qquad
 s=r-ah.
\tag{44}
\]

Assume the pure-cross target conditions

\[
 p\mid b_r,
 \qquad
 \ell\mid b_s.
\tag{45}
\]

For any packet coordinates \(\mu,\nu\), define

\[
 D_{\mu,\nu}(p,\ell)
 =c_n(p\mu)c_n(\ell\nu)
  -c_n(p\nu)c_n(\ell\mu).
\tag{46}
\]

### Proposition 9.1 — exact degree-two cross carrier

Under (45),

\[
 \boxed{p\ell\mid D_{\mu,\nu}(p,\ell).}
\tag{47}
\]

#### Proof

Modulo \(p\), both coefficients sampled on the \(p\)-packet are zero, so both terms in (46) vanish. Modulo \(\ell\), both coefficients sampled on the \(\ell\)-packet are zero. Since \(p\) and \(\ell\) are distinct, their product divides the determinant. ∎

This is the minimal bidegree-\((1,1)\) construction. It proves that a cross-product identity exists, but not one of the required height.

First, it may vanish identically for the selected coordinates. Summing squares of all such minors gives a nonnegative expression, but it vanishes whenever the two packet vectors are rationally proportional, and no theorem in the current repository excludes that proportionality uniformly.

Second, since

\[
 0\le c_n(v)\le40^n,
\tag{48}
\]

one has

\[
 |D_{\mu,\nu}(p,\ell)|\le2\cdot40^{2n}
\]

and hence (4). This is a linear-\(n\) height, whereas the block budget is \(o(n^{1/3})\).

Third, the apparent common state does not cancel in the relevant residue field. Modulo \(p\), the \(p\)-packet is a scalar multiple of \(P^a\), but the \(\ell\)-packet is an uncontrolled sampling of the coefficient array. Modulo \(\ell\), the roles reverse. There is no field in which both packet proportionalities hold simultaneously.

This is the precise cross-characteristic obstruction.

## 10. Adding tangent columns cancels the state but also the target

Choose an integral basis \(B_1,\ldots,B_{27}\) of the tangent lattice

\[
 T_a=P^{a-1}V_1.
\]

Form a determinant with these \(27\) fixed columns and packet columns \(A_{p,n}\), \(A_{\ell,n}\):

\[
 \Delta_{p,\ell}
 =\det(B_1,\ldots,B_{27},A_{p,n},A_{\ell,n})
\tag{49}
\]

using any \(29\) coefficient rows.

Proposition 4.1 implies

\[
 p^2\mid\Delta_{p,\ell}
\]

for every prime \(p\), whether or not \(p\mid b_r\), because \(A_{p,n}\in T_a+p^2V_a\). Likewise

\[
 \ell^2\mid\Delta_{p,\ell}
\]

universally.

Thus

\[
 p^2\ell^2\mid\Delta_{p,\ell}
\tag{50}
\]

contains **no target information at all**. The determinant has successfully canceled the free power state, but precisely for that reason its divisibility is universal.

Its height is also too large:

\[
 \log|\Delta_{p,\ell}|
 \le
 2n\log40+27(a-1)\log40+O(1)
 =\Theta(n).
\tag{51}
\]

This is the cleanest exact illustration of the no-go requested in the question.

## 11. The exposed selective coordinate and its height floor

The scalar line \(\mathbf ZP^a\) is primitive because

\[
 [X^{av}]P^a=1.
\tag{52}
\]

Therefore the exposed coefficient

\[
 S_{p,n}
 =[X^{av}]A_{p,n}
 =c_n(pa\,v)
\tag{53}
\]

extracts the selective scalar modulo \(p\):

\[
 \boxed{S_{p,n}\equiv b_r\pmod p.}
\tag{54}
\]

The exact first-jet expansion at the exposed vertex is

\[
 S_{p,n}
 \equiv
 b_r+ap\,[X^v]J^{(1)}_{p,r}
 \pmod{p^2},
\tag{55}
\]

because the exposed coefficient in the product \(P^{a-1}J^{(1)}\) can arise only from the exposed terms \((a-1)v\) and \(v\). On a target,

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{S_{p,n}}p
 \equiv
 q_{p,r}+a[X^v]J^{(1)}_{p,r}\pmod p.}
\tag{56}
\]

The right-hand side is the explicit free first Frobenius scalar in this packet model. Targetness does not force it to be zero.

Now use positivity. The coefficient of the zero step of \(P\) is

\[
 [X^0]P=5.
\tag{57}
\]

Among the words counted by \(c_n((n-r)v)\) are those with \(n-r\) exposed steps \(v\) and \(r\) zero steps. Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 S_{p,n}\ge\binom nr5^r.}
\tag{58}
\]

This proves the height floor (6).

For two targets \(p,\ell\) in the same direct branch, with residues \(r,s\), the positive product

\[
 S_{p,n}S_{\ell,n}
\]

is divisible by \(p\ell\), but

\[
 \log(S_{p,n}S_{\ell,n})
 \ge(r+s)\log5.
\tag{59}
\]

In the unresolved core, \(r,s\ge H\), so this is at least \(2H\log5\), not \(o(H)\).

This also disposes of every subtraction-free expression containing the exposed selective coordinate, including positive convolution products, squares, and packet \(L^2\) norms.

## 12. Assessment of the proposed nonlinear mechanisms

### 12.1 Coefficient convolution

The exact expansion (18) is already the complete convolution law obtained from the power state. Its zeroth grade is scalar; its higher grades are universal Frobenius defects. Convolving packets raises the scalar power and enlarges the osculating defect spaces. It does not create a second selective equation.

### 12.2 Hadamard products and Parseval

Hadamard squares give \(p^2\) at a target for the tautological reason that every packet coefficient is divisible by \(p\). Parseval rewrites the same positive integer as a root-of-unity average but does not lower its height. Any centered Gram form which removes the scalar line is universally divisible.

### 12.3 Total positivity and log-concavity

No proof that the relevant three-dimensional packet arrays form a uniformly totally positive or Lorentzian family is present in the repository. More importantly, even such a theorem would supply a sign/nonvanishing statement for determinants, not an \(o(H)\) upper bound. The entries themselves have exponential height, and the modular factors still arise from the scalar-or-universal dichotomy.

### 12.4 Exposed-face products

The exposed vertex gives an exact nonzero carrier, but (58) shows that it has the wrong height in the interior. Near the boundary, where \(r=o(H)\), this route merely recovers the already controlled boundary channel.

### 12.5 Resultants

A resultant of the packet with \(P\), or of two packet covariants, is homogeneous in the packet coefficients. Keeping packet content retains only a power of \(b_r\). Removing the common power-state factor produces a universal osculating resultant. Multivariate resultants also increase degree with the packet support, worsening the height ledger.

### 12.6 Squares intended to remove the free state

Quadratic equations of the power variety vanish at \(P^a\), but their first derivative vanishes precisely on the tangent space. Evaluating them on a target packet therefore gives a higher universal \(p\)-adic order. The next divided value is the second fundamental form in the free tangent vector plus a new second Frobenius defect. It is not forced to vanish.

## 13. Scoped bounded-degree no-go theorem

The following is the exact scope in which a no-go can be proved without claiming algebraic independence of the actual Apéry defects.

### Theorem 13.1 — functorial bounded-degree packet saturation

Fix a degree bound \(D\) and a finite Cartier precision \(k\). Consider identities obtained by the following operations:

1. coefficient extraction from the packets \(A_{p,n}\);
2. addition and multiplication of packet coordinates;
3. convolution or Hadamard products of bounded total degree;
4. contraction against fixed coefficient tensors formed from \(P^a\);
5. minors, Gram forms, discriminants, or resultants of bounded total packet degree;
6. elimination using only the exact expansion (18) through precision \(p^{k+1}\) and the power identities of \(P^a\).

After dividing all prime powers forced for every residue by the osculating filtration (35), every target-selective polynomial consequence lies in the ideal generated by the zeroth scalar \(b_r\). At a target, its first normalized value is a polynomial in

\[
 q_{p,r}=b_r/p
\]

and the Frobenius-defect coordinates

\[
 J^{(1)}_{p,r},\ldots,J^{(k)}_{p,r}.
\]

No nonzero equation on those normalized coordinates follows from the listed operations alone.

For two primes \(p\) and \(\ell\), the separately selective ideals are supported in disjoint packet variable sets. Their intersection is their product. Hence a cross-prime polynomial guaranteed to contain both target primes must retain at least one selective packet factor from each characteristic. Projective elimination of the common power state replaces those factors by universal osculating divisibility.

#### Meaning

This theorem does not say that the concrete Apéry defects are algebraically independent. It says exactly what must be added to beat the no-go:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{a special arithmetic relation among the actual }
 J^{(j)}_{p,r}\text{ as }p\text{ varies}.}
\tag{60}
\]

A relation following only from “the state is a power” is already included in the saturated osculating flag.

## 14. Why close primes are not coupled by the common state

For \(\ell=p+h\), the two target equations are

\[
 \mathcal C_p(P^n)\equiv0\pmod p,
 \qquad
 \mathcal C_\ell(P^n)\equiv0\pmod\ell.
\tag{61}
\]

Each reduction identifies its own packet with a scalar multiple of \(P^a\), but these statements occur in different residue fields. Reducing the \(\ell\)-packet modulo \(p\) gives no proportionality to \(P^a\), and reducing the \(p\)-packet modulo \(\ell\) gives none either.

Thus the common characteristic-zero array \(c_n(v)\) supplies a common source, but not a common rank drop. The closest exact coupling is the cross determinant (46), whose height is linear in \(n\).

A fixed gap \(h\) does not repair this. The sequence

\[
 d\longmapsto c_n(d\mu)
\tag{62}
\]

is a sampled coefficient sequence of the degree-\(n\) Laurent polynomial \(P^n\). No bounded-order recurrence in \(d\), bounded-degree resultant, or sub-\(H\)-height annihilator is proved. The fixed-moment shell of Section 48 gives a common coefficient array, but Q5695 shows that its natural cyclic covers and finite \(p\)-adic lifts likewise leave a free Witt coordinate.

## 15. Consequence for the adjacent-collision criterion

Let \(I\) be a quotient block of length \(H=n^{1/3}\), and let

\[
 C_A(n,I)
\]

be the number of adjacent target pairs with gap at most \(A\log n\), as in Section 46 of the repository.

The constructions above do not yield a common integer of height \(o(H)\) for those pairs:

- the exposed scalar has an \(\Omega(H)\) floor in the interior;
- positive norms and products are larger;
- pairwise cross minors have \(\Theta(n)\) available height and are not common across the block;
- tangent/osculating determinants are universally divisible and therefore do not count targets;
- multiplying pair-specific carriers has height proportional to the number of pairs times \(n\), far beyond the LAC budget.

Thus no averaged version sufficient for LAC follows from these nonlinear packet operations.

## 16. The weakest plausible reopening theorem

A genuinely useful theorem must survive osculating saturation and act on the actual Frobenius defects.

One precise sufficient statement is the following.

### Cross-Frobenius packet theorem — sufficient but open

For every fixed \(A>0\) and every \(\varepsilon>0\), there is \(N_0(A,\varepsilon)\) such that, whenever

\[
 n>N_0,
 \qquad H=n^{1/3},
 \qquad a\le H,
 \qquad 1\le h\le A\log n,
\]

there exists a nonzero integer \(D_{n,a,h}\), defined without knowing the target primes, satisfying

\[
 \log|D_{n,a,h}|\le\varepsilon H,
\tag{63}
\]

and such that for every pair of primes \(p,\ell=p+h\) in one quotient branch with

\[
 p\mid b_{n-ap},
 \qquad
 \ell\mid b_{n-a\ell},
\]

one has

\[
 p\ell\mid D_{n,a,h}.
\tag{64}
\]

To be structurally new, \(D_{n,a,h}\) must depend on a cross-characteristic relation among

\[
 Q_{p,n-ap}
 =\frac{b_{n-ap}}pP+aJ^{(1)}_{p,n-ap}\pmod p
\]

and

\[
 Q_{\ell,n-a\ell}
 =\frac{b_{n-a\ell}}\ell P
  +aJ^{(1)}_{\ell,n-a\ell}\pmod\ell,
\]

or on a higher defect after all universal osculating factors have been removed.

A weaker averaged theorem would suffice if the sum of logarithmic heights of a family of such carriers over \(h\le A\log n\) were \(o(H)\) and every short adjacent pair were covered with bounded multiplicity.

No identity of this type is currently proved.

## 17. Final route-selection statement

The exact nonlinear power-state calculation produces real mathematics:

\[
 \mathcal C_p(P^{ap+r})
 =\sum_j\binom ajp^jP^{a-j}J^{(j)}_{p,r}.
\]

It also identifies the obstruction sharply.

- The scalar grade is \(b_r\), the known target condition.
- The tangent and higher grades are universal Frobenius/osculating data.
- Saturating those universal grades leaves the divided scalar \(b_r/p\) mixed with a free first defect.
- Products and squares retain powers of the scalar.
- Projective minors and resultants cancel the state but become universal.
- Cross-prime determinants retain both primes but have linear-\(n\) height and no uniform nonvanishing.
- The top-half branch \(a=1\) has no nonlinear tangent codimension at all.

Therefore the requested \(o(H)\) carrier is not obtained. The strongest rigorous outcome is the scoped bounded-degree no-go above: **the fact that the distinguished state is \(\Lambda^a\) is fully consumed by its osculating filtration. Any successful nonlinear attack must use an arithmetic relation special to the actual Frobenius defects of the Apéry Laurent polynomial across distinct characteristics, not merely polynomial identities of the power state.**
