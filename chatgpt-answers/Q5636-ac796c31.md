## 8. Published-theorem audit

### 8.1 Prime-moduli large sieve

Wolke/Iwaniec prime-moduli large-sieve estimates improve the generic all-moduli Q^2 constant by the density of prime moduli in suitable ranges. In the present complete-residue setup, the elementary orthogonality argument (10) already gives the favorable N^2/\log N operator norm.

That is still only critical under |Z_p|=O(1). The theorem has no mechanism to distinguish an Apéry row from an adversarial bounded row. It cannot produce the required little-oh.

### 8.2 Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel and Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel--Sawin

The trace-function bilinear theorems concern a fixed finite field \mathbf F_q and a fixed bounded-conductor sheaf, with kernels such as K(m^bn^c) or fixed-modulus trace functions sampled at prime arguments. Here there is no single field: the two local factors live over \mathbf F_p and \mathbf F_\ell, while p,\ell themselves are summed.

Moreover the exact zero indicator is not a bounded-conductor trace function. Therefore these theorems do not apply after a formal replacement of K by \mathbf1_{b_r=0}.

### 8.3 Large sieve for Frobenius

The large sieve for Frobenius requires one algebraic family of bounded complexity and compares its reductions through fixed auxiliary finite quotients. Here the auxiliary modulus is the same prime as the residue characteristic, and the Kummer order grows like that prime. There is no fixed quotient or bounded-rank compatible system on which to invoke independence.

### 8.4 Bettin--Chandee and Kloosterman-fraction bilinear forms

After CRT completion, one encounters phases involving inverses such as \bar\ell\bmod p and \bar p\bmod\ell. This superficially resembles bilinear or trilinear Kloosterman-fraction sums. But the coefficients are the modulus-dependent root Fourier sums

and the affine root relation r-s=q(\ell-p) entangles the numerator data with both moduli. The published Kloosterman-fraction estimates require separable coefficient sequences and a fixed algebraic phase. They do not cover these unknown, characteristic-dependent root sets.

### 8.5 Classical BDH

Classical BDH concerns a common arithmetic sequence distributed among residue classes. It does not cover self-divisibility of a holonomic sequence value by the varying modulus. A theorem that does cover (2) would be new even if described as “BDH for Apéry zero sections.”

## 10. Final hostile assessment

The positive fixed-q reduction is real: it converts the pointwise P3.2 obstruction into a single pair-energy statement with no growing-q uniformity. But it does not place the problem inside the range of an existing standard large sieve.

The exact failures are:

- additive double large sieve: optimal generic L^2 constant, but only critical even for bounded rows;

- multiplicative/Mellin inversion: bounded geometry for the trace, unbounded geometry for the exact-zero indicator;

- BDH: no common modulus-independent coefficient sequence;

- compatible-family geometry: character order, coefficient field, and residue characteristic all move;

- dyadic gaps: ambient Selberg sieve gives the right prime-pair density but no Apéry saving; pure-cross continuants are units.

Therefore the honest status is:

The replacement sufficient statement is (\mathrm{MCAD})_q, or equivalently (\mathrm{FD})_q. Any claimed proof from Katz equidistribution, bounded |Z_p|, ordinary BDH, or a standard trace-function large sieve is missing the same essential horizontal local-limit input.
