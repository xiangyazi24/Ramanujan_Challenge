# 4. Primitive divisors do not give the needed upper bound

A primitive-divisor theorem would say that a term B_r possesses a prime factor not dividing earlier terms. That kind of result is naturally a lower bound on prime-factor novelty. The present problem requires an upper bound on the number of specially positioned divisors

that can occur simultaneously as r varies.

There are two basic obstructions.

First, the Apéry numbers are not a classical divisibility sequence. Already

so 1\mid2 does not imply B_1\mid B_2. The Zsigmondy--Carmichael machinery for Lucas or elliptic divisibility sequences therefore does not apply directly.

Second, even an ideal primitive-divisor theorem would not prevent a diagonal pile-up. Different indices r could have different primitive divisors p_r=n-r, all aligned at the same n. Primitivity only says that p_r did not occur at an earlier index; it does not relate p_r+r for different r.

Reflection makes this even clearer. A target prime divides a reflected pair of Apéry terms. If the reflected partner is earlier, then the prime is not primitive at the later index; after folding to the earlier member, it may still be primitive there. In neither orientation does primitivity constrain the common sum p+r=n across different primes.

A targeted literature search did not surface an Apéry primitive-divisor theorem applicable to this problem. The relevant published prime-factor result is Florian Luca and Igor Shparlinski, Arithmetic properties of Apéry numbers, Journal of the London Mathematical Society 78 (2008), 545--562, DOI 10.1112/jlms/jdn031. It proves, for a density-one set of indices, lower bounds for the number of distinct prime factors and the largest prime factor of B_r. Those density-one lower bounds are compatible with, and do not control, the pointwise top-half diagonal.

## 10.2 Larger sieve

The larger sieve can show that a set of many integers occupying few residue classes modulo many primes is small. It cannot rule out one exceptional integer. A singleton occupies one residue class modulo every prime and is perfectly compatible with all congruence conditions. The present theorem is pointwise: even one heavy column is forbidden asymptotically.

Upper-bound sieves estimate averages over n after local densities are supplied. Here the local densities are small, and this explains the near-Poisson average. The route audit is:

- integer divisibility: exact reduction to B_n, but exponential height;

- universal product/factorial carrier: at least (1/2+o(1))n logarithmic height by the top-half primorial;

- primitive divisors: wrong direction and no cross-prime sum constraint;

- reflection: same mod-p equation twice; continuant has the forced factor p;

- Casoratian/Ore identities: adjacent values are not simultaneously divisible; fixed-window certificates collapse to the original coordinate;

- ordinary resultants: forget the moving root and record any prefix zero;

- marked subresultants/Padé-family gcds: exact support, but no independent height bound;

- large sieve: Farey spacing produces the n^2 barrier and same-prime Parseval does not control p\ne q;

- row sparsity: controls average incidence, not the maximum column.

A genuine advance must therefore do one of two things:

1. prove cross-characteristic cancellation for the marked Fourier coefficients U_p(h) at the phases dictated by one fixed n; or

1. construct a recurrence-specific characteristic-zero integer whose nonzero value has height \exp(o(n)) and which is divisible by every target prime, without being divisible by all candidate primes.

No current construction in the repository meets the second criterion, and no standard sieve theorem supplies the first.
