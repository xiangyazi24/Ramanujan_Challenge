# Q6692 audit: AP cascade from consecutive zeros of N_d

## 1. Consecutive zeros of the same N_d

Assume a k-run of gap d:

N_d(r+jd)=0 for j=0,...,k-1.

Equivalently,

pi(r)=pi(r+d)=...=pi(r+kd).

The key point is that the cascade is not merely a polynomial divisibility statement: it is a sequence of projective equalities. Therefore every composed transfer block has zero determinant direction.

For two consecutive zeros of N_d at r and r+d, we get

pi(r)=pi(r+d)=pi(r+2d).

Hence

N_{2d}(r)=0.

Using the addition law

N_{2d}(r) = N_d(r+d)N_{d+1}(r) - (r+d+1)^6 N_{d-1}(r+d+1)N_d(r),

both displayed terms vanish because both factors containing N_d vanish. Therefore the addition identity itself gives no new contradiction. The useful information is instead obtained from adjacent coprimality.

Since

gcd(N_{d-1},N_d)=gcd(N_d,N_{d+1})=1,

a root of N_d cannot be a root of N_{d-1} or N_{d+1} over the algebraic closure. Thus, at a point r with

N_d(r)=N_d(r+d)=0,

we necessarily have

N_{d-1}(r), N_{d+1}(r) != 0,

unless r lies in the excluded pole/degenerate locus. Consequently the cascade does not create a local contradiction; it creates a rigid tower of common roots of the dilated family.

## 2. The common-root tower

A k-run gives

N_d(r)=N_{2d}(r)=...=N_{kd}(r)=0.

Thus r is a common root of the family

{N_{jd}:1<=j<=k}.

The relevant object is not the degree bound deg(N_{jd})=3(jd-1), but the gcd geometry of the family.

The adjacent-resultant law only controls

Res(N_a,N_{a+1}).

It does not immediately control

Res(N_d,N_{2d}), Res(N_d,N_{3d}), ...

because the indices are dilated. Therefore the strongest unconditional conclusion from the banked facts is:

K_max(d) <= 3(d-1),

coming from the degree of N_d.

A stronger bound such as O(1) or O(log d) would require a new dilated-resultant theorem proving that

has uniformly bounded degree. This is currently OPEN.

## 3. What the h=2 splitting law suggests

For d=2,

N_2(r)=P(r+1)

has root count

1+2*[(-51/p)=1].

The quadratic splitting field Q(sqrt(-51)) also appears in the two-step return probability. This indicates that the common-root phenomenon is governed by the return monodromy field rather than accidental polynomial degree.

However, this does not imply bounded AP runs: a single return field can support isolated higher coincidences.

## 4. Counting AP triangles

Let Q_D denote the number of triangles with gaps <= D.

The AP triangles are the diagonal case where the two gaps coincide. They require

pi(r)=pi(r+e)=pi(r+2e),

hence

N_e(r)=0 and N_{2e}(r)=0.

Therefore the AP contribution satisfies

Q_AP(D) <= sum_{e<=D} #{r:N_e(r)=N_{2e}(r)=0}.

A Bezout bound gives only

#{r:N_e(r)=N_{2e}(r)=0} << e^2,

so

Q_AP(D) << D^3.

This is not sufficient by itself. A nontrivial improvement requires a dilated-family gcd theorem.

## 5. Does triangle-free amplification work?

The desired mechanism would be:

Q_D << S_1(D)+Q_AP(D).

Then the cascade would turn generic triangles into AP-type events and capacity estimates could force the desired energy exponent.

This fails with the current information.

Reason: a generic triangle

pi(r)=pi(r+e)=pi(r+d)

only gives

N_e(r)=0,

N_d(r)=0.

The two gaps are unrelated. There is no forced relation

N_{d-e}(r+e)=0

unless the middle point is already known to sit on the orbit segment between the two endpoints. The addition law generates identities, but not a third collision with a distinct gap.

Hence generic triangles do not collapse into AP triangles.

## 6. Final verdict

Established:

1. Consecutive zeros of N_d imply a cascade

N_d,N_{2d},...,N_{kd}

at the same base point.

1. Adjacent coprimality prevents the cascade from extending through neighboring gaps; it gives nonvanishing of N_{d-1},N_{d+1} at the run point.

1. The unconditional AP-run bound remains

K_max(d)<=3(d-1).

1. The AP triangle contribution has only the current Bezout-type bound

Q_AP(D)<<D^3.

Not proved:

- a uniform bound on AP run length;

- a dilated resultant theorem for N_d,N_{2d},...;

- triangle-free amplification Q_D<<S_1(D)+AP.

The bottleneck is exactly the missing family-averaged cancellation/common-root theorem for the growing dilated family. The cascade identifies the right algebraic object, but the current resultant toolkit controls adjacent gaps and cannot yet control dilations.
