\begin{remark}[N/K offset dictionary]\label{rem:offset-dictionary}
The index conversion between the N and K gauges is
\[
N_h(r)=K_{h-1}(r+1).
\]
The renewal vanishing implication is a polynomial identity at the shifted offset $a+1$ only. More precisely, the continuant identity
\[
K_{a+g}=K_a K_g(X+a)-(X+a)^6K_{a-1}K_{g-1}(X+a+1)
\]
gives
\[
N_a(x)=0,\quad N_g(x+a+1)=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N_{a+g+1}(x)=0.
\]
This statement is an identity of polynomials and therefore also governs extension-field roots and cut-edge roots.

By contrast, the orbit-transitivity statement
\[
N_a(r)=0,\quad N_g(r+a)=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N_{a+g}(r)=0
\]
holds only at regular $\mathbb F_p$ points of the projective orbit. Extension-field roots and cut-edge roots are governed by the polynomial renewal identity above, not by the regular-point orbit statement.

\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Gauge freeze warning]
An $h$-dependent nonzero rescaling of the determinant $\Delta$ preserves every zero-fiber statement, but it changes all-level and coincidence statistics completely. Likewise, a one-unit offset slip between the $N$ and $K$ gauges silently changes the gcd family being counted.

Therefore every gcd, resultant, and valuation count in this document states its gauge and offset convention explicitly before counting.
