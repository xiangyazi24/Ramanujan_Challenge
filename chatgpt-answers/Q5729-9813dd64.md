## 8. Why known prime-divisor theorems do not apply

The available arithmetic results on Apéry and holonomic sequences do not imply (0.1):

- density-one results on the number or largest prime divisor of b_n are lower bounds on the total prime support of b_n, not upper bounds for its support in the short interval (M/2-H,M/2];

- prime-divisor theorems for binary holonomic sequences likewise force many prime divisors in products and run in the opposite direction;

- residue-class results for Apéry numbers control long orbits or additive coverage, not the number of zeros in a moving interval of only p^{1/3} residue positions;

- the exact fixed-order first-cell telescopers govern characteristic-zero propagation but supply no uniform finite-field trace estimate for the distinguished state.

Thus applying any of these statements here would require precisely the missing short-zero incidence estimate.

def forward_differences(values):
    out = []
    level = values[:]
    while level:
        out.append(level[0])
        level = [level[i + 1] - level[i] for i in range(len(level) - 1)]
    return out


def parity_newton_carrier(P, values):
    diffs = forward_differences(values)
    return sum(comb(P, k) * diffs[k] for k in range(len(diffs))), diffs
