# Q70 Furstenberg/Christol audit

## Verdict

The diagonal algebraization route does not currently produce a sub-sqrt(p) complexity collision variety. The correct outcome is (C): a precise open lemma remains. The diagonal structure helps with the first Apéry solution b, but the collision indicator needs the second solution c and the two-variable zero set; the available algebraicity does not survive with sufficiently small complexity.

## (i) Algebraicity degree of B(t)

For a rational diagonal over F_p, Furstenberg gives algebraicity because diagonals are stable under Cartier operators. For a rational function in k variables, the general Cartier-state construction gives degree bounded by a p-power whose exponent depends on the number of variables/state rank. It does not give a bounded degree independent of p.

For the Apéry diagonal representation, the relevant consequence is only that B(t) is algebraic over F_p(t) with a p-dependent degree bound. The generic bound is of the form p^k for the Cartier orbit size (k depending on the chosen rational presentation), not p^theta with theta < 1/2.

The Gessel-Lucas property gives the functional equation

B(t) = B_0(t) B(t^p)

where B_0(t)=sum_{0<=r<p} b_r t^r in F_p[t]. Iterating gives the formal product

B(t)=product_{j>=0} B_0(t^{p^j}).

However, on the single window n<p this says only that the coefficient sequence is the polynomial B_0(t) truncated at degree p-1. It gives no nontrivial compression of the collision problem. The higher Cartier factors are invisible in the window.

## (ii) Collision kernel and the second solution

The determinant kernel

K(x,y)=B(x)C(y)-C(x)B(y)

is algebraically useful only if C(t)=sum c_n t^n has comparable algebraicity.

The second solution is a logarithmic/jet solution of the underlying differential equation. A parameter derivative construction explains the appearance of harmonic corrections, but differentiation of an algebraic family generally introduces logarithmic periods. Mod p these are controlled by Dwork-logarithm type objects rather than by the same finite Cartier orbit as B.

Therefore the natural statement "C(t) is algebraic with the same degree scale as B(t)" is not established. The obstruction is not the first solution; it is the second independent solution.

## (iii) Two-variable collision avatar

Even granting algebraicity of B and C separately, an algebraic zero-locus argument needs a complexity bound for

b_r c_{r+d}-b_{r+d}c_r.

A degree bound p^theta with theta<1/2 would be strong enough because a Lang-Weil style estimate on a curve/surface of controlled degree could improve the D^2 incidence budget.

The present Furstenberg/Christol machinery gives a degree scale comparable to a positive power of p coming from Cartier states. It does not provide theta<1/2.

## Death certificate status

A full death certificate "every possible avatar has degree >= p^(1/2)" is not proved. The correct terminal statement is:

OPEN LEMMA: construct a two-variable algebraic avatar of the collision relation with complexity o(p^(1/2)), or prove that every avatar containing the second Apéry solution necessarily has Cartier complexity at least p^(1/2).

The known blockers remain valid: characteristic-zero bounded-degree H-algebraization fails, single-window automatic methods have p-growing state spaces, and fixed-height sheaf estimates have growing conductor.

## Least confident step

The least confident step is the exact lower-bound intuition on the Cartier complexity of the second solution. The logarithmic nature is clear, but a sharp impossibility theorem for all possible avatars is not available.

## Blind spot

The possible blind spot is assuming the collision indicator itself must be represented by the full pair (b,c). A hidden quotient, Frobenius-invariant determinant, or formal-group coordinate could have lower complexity even when C(t) alone is transcendental-looking. Such a quotient would be the genuinely new arithmetic input needed.
