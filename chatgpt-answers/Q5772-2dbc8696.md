## Result

The correct algorithmic test is a finite linear-algebra search after fixing an a priori degree bound. The audit does not prove existence of a rational Ore intertwiner globally merely from gcrd(L,Aop)=1 or from the factorization P=L*R.

For an order-2 candidate

U=u_0(n)+u_1(n)S,

write

L U=V A_{op},

where V has unknown rational coefficients. Clearing denominators gives a polynomial linear system in the coefficients of u_0,u_1,V. Solving this system is the exact certificate search.

# Mathematical obstruction

The degree-bounded search only proves:

\exists U,V \text{ with } \deg(U),\deg(V)\le D.

It does not imply existence in the rational Ore ring because a hypothetical intertwiner can require arbitrarily high degree after clearing denominators.

The valid global route would require a theorem bounding the degree of a Darboux/intertwiner transformation for this specific pair of Ore operators.

# Why gcrd is insufficient

gcrd(L,Aop)=1 only excludes a common right divisor. An intertwiner satisfies

L U=V A_{op},

which is an equivalence/gauge relation, not a common-factor relation. Therefore:

- gcrd=1 does not imply no intertwiner;

- absence of small-degree solutions does not imply no rational intertwiner.

# Final verdict

The mathematically correct conclusion is a bounded-degree nonexistence/existence test, not a global theorem. A finite Sage search can certify a missing low-degree gauge, but only an additional degree-growth theorem can upgrade it to a proof that no rational Ore intertwiner exists.
