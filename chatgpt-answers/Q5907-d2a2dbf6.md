# Literature audit: moving Newton/Ore hybrid carrier

## Scope

The question concerns whether existing arithmetic theorems for diagonals, G-functions, Picard-Fuchs periods, and holonomic sequences imply

[

log operatorname{rad}{p: pmid b_{n-p}text{ and }pmidtext{moving carrier}}=o(n)

]

for a family where both the Newton margin and finite-difference order grow linearly with (n).

The decisive distinction is between fixed-object congruence theorems and this moving family. The known theorems below control a fixed diagonal/period sequence (a_m) as (mtoinfty); they do not directly control a finite-difference operator whose coefficients, order, and evaluation point all depend on (n).

## Candidate theorems and applicability

### 1. Christol: diagonals are G-functions

Reference: G. Christol, "Diagonales de fonctions rationnelles", Séminaire de Théorie des Nombres de Bordeaux (1985).

Statement used: the diagonal of a rational power series over a number field is a G-function and therefore satisfies a linear differential equation with arithmetic growth bounds.

Mapping:

- Apéry numbers fit the diagonal framework.

- The Laurent constant term representation gives a fixed rational diagonal after standard conversion.

- The theorem controls the fixed sequence (b_m).

Failure:

The carrier contains

[

Delta^{L_n}C_{M_n}(D_n)

]

with (L_nasymp n). Christol does not bound primitive parts of these moving high-order finite differences.

### 2. André/Chudnovsky G-function arithmetic

Reference: Y. André, G-functions and Geometry, Aspects of Mathematics E13, Vieweg (1989).

Relevant content: denominators and p-adic growth of coefficients of G-functions are controlled by the underlying differential equation.

Mapping:

Applicable to the fixed Picard-Fuchs module of the Apéry period.

Failure:

The theorem gives denominator bounds for Taylor coefficients, not gcd separation between a Lucas residue condition (pmid b_{n-p}) and a Kummer binomial mask generated by a moving Newton stencil.

### 3. Dwork/Delaygue p-Lucas type congruences

References:

- B. Dwork, "On the rationality of the zeta function of an algebraic variety", Amer. J. Math. 82 (1960).

- E. Delaygue, "Arithmetic properties of Apéry-like numbers", J. Théorie des Nombres de Bordeaux 20 (2008).

Relevant theorem shape: diagonal coefficients satisfy congruences of the form

[

a_{mp+r}equiv a_m a_r pmod p

]

under suitable p-integrality hypotheses.

Mapping:

This is the correct source of the already-used reduction

[

b_{p+r}equiv b_1b_rpmod p.

]

Failure:

It separates the target residue (r=n-p), but does not bound the set of primes for which the residue vanishes. It gives no subexponential radical estimate.

### 4. Krattenthaler--Rivoal style binomial transforms

Relevant literature: C. Krattenthaler and T. Rivoal, works on Apéry-like sequences and irrationality measures.

Applicability:

Their determinant/binomial-transform identities explain many fixed-order Apéry recurrences and denominator estimates.

Failure:

The present carrier uses Newton transforms with order (L=N+O(1)), not a fixed transform. Existing results do not provide primitive-content estimates uniform in transform order.

## Precise obstruction

The missing theorem would need the following form.

## Needed theorem-shaped lemma

Let

[

F(X)=sum_{mge0}a_mX^m

]

be a rational diagonal with integral coefficients and bounded Picard-Fuchs complexity. Define

[

N_{n,D,L}=sum_{i=0}^{L}(-1)^ibinom{L}{i}a_{D+i}.

]

A sufficient theorem would be:

For every admissible moving window with (D,Lasymp n),

[

logoperatorname{rad}{p>sqrt n:pmid a_{p+r}text{ and }pmid N_{n,D,L}}=o(n).

]

No known Christol/Dwork/André/Delaygue theorem has this conclusion.

The missing ingredient is not holonomicity; it is a theorem on primitive parts of moving binomial/Newton transforms of diagonals.

## Conclusion

The literature gives:

1. fixed diagonal/G-function arithmetic;

1. p-Lucas congruences explaining the target residue;

1. denominator and recurrence control.

It does not give the required separation between:

- the Apéry zero condition (pmid b_{n-p});

- the ambient Kummer/Pascal divisibility coming from the carrier.

The closest realistic closure statement is the missing primitive moving-transform theorem above. Proving it for the Apéry diagonal would directly close the current carrier route.
