# 2. Why the proposed rank argument does not work

The image of P_infinity in GL(V) is finite of exponent p. It is an elementary abelian p-group, not a positive-dimensional torus.

Therefore

```text
number of distinct P_infinity-characters
```

is not a lower bound for the rank of H.

Even if a finite abelian subgroup lies in a torus of H, a rank-one group can have arbitrarily many distinct weights in one irreducible representation. For example,

```text
SL_2 on Sym^m(std)
```

has m+1 distinct torus weights.

Thus the implication

```text
4h-4 distinct characters
  => rank(H) >= 2h-2 or 2h-1
```

is invalid.

The correct use of the wild characters is not rank counting. It is weight-multiplicity control after the identity component has been proved simple.

# 3. Uniform primitivity

We first exclude disconnected imprimitive monodromy.

Assume

```text
V = V_1 direct-sum ... direct-sum V_k,       (3.1)
```

is a nontrivial system of imprimitivity, with equal block dimension d > 0 and kd=r. The group H permutes the blocks transitively.

For the uniform argument below assume

```text
p > r.                                      (3.2)
```

Then p>k. The wild inertia P_infinity is a pro-p group, so its permutation image in S_k is trivial. Hence every block is P_infinity-stable.

Because the nontrivial P_infinity-character spaces L_a are one-dimensional and pairwise distinct, a P_infinity-stable block is a direct sum of:

```text
- a subset of the L_a lines;
- possibly a subspace of T_h.
```

The full inertia I_infinity preserves every L_a line and preserves T_h. Therefore any block containing an L_a is fixed by I_infinity.

A block containing no L_a lies inside T_h, whose dimension is 2.

If d>1, at most one such block exists, hence it is also fixed.

If d=1, H is monomial in a block basis. But every invertible monomial matrix in characteristic zero is semisimple: on a permutation cycle its characteristic polynomial is

```text
X^m-c,
```

with c != 0, hence separable. A monomial group therefore contains no nontrivial unipotent element. This contradicts nu != 1.

Thus

```text
I_infinity fixes every block.                (3.3)
```

The geometric fundamental group of G_m is generated by the two local inertia groups. Hence the block action is generated by the image sigma of T_0.

Every unipotent element of an algebraic group lies in the identity component. Since nu=T_0^3 is unipotent,

```text
sigma^3=1.                                  (3.4)
```

A transitive permutation generated by one element of order dividing 3 has either one point or three points. Nontrivial imprimitivity therefore forces

```text
k=3.                                        (3.5)
```

Then 3 divides r=4h-2, so

```text
h = 2 mod 3.                                (3.6)
```

But T_0 cyclically permutes the three blocks, so the three restrictions of T_0^3 to them are conjugate. Consequently

```text
rank(nu-1)
```

is divisible by 3. By (1.3), this says

```text
3 divides h-1,
```

or h=1 mod 3, contradicting (3.6).

Therefore:

```text
[PRIM_h]
V_h is primitive for every h>=2 and p>4h-2. (3.7)
```

This completely generalizes the block argument used in Q6663 for h=2,3.

# 4. The identity component acts irreducibly

Let H^0 be the identity component.

By Clifford theory and primitivity, the restriction to H^0 is homogeneous:

```text
V|H^0 = W^(direct-sum e).                   (4.1)
```

Equivalently,

```text
V = W tensor E,
dim E=e,
```

with H^0 acting trivially on E.

Since nu belongs to H^0,

```text
nu = nu_W tensor I_E,
rank(nu-1)=e rank(nu_W-1).                  (4.2)
```

Therefore e divides h-1. It also divides dim V=4h-2. Hence

```text
e divides gcd(h-1,4h-2)=gcd(h-1,2),
```

so

```text
e=1,
```

or

```text
e=2 and h is odd.                           (4.3)
```

We now exclude e=2 using the full tame type, not merely the cube nu.

Assume e=2. The normalizer of the H^0-isotypic tensor structure acts as

```text
T_0=A tensor B                              (4.4)
```

on W tensor E, after choosing the two tensor factors. Since T_0^3 belongs to H^0,

```text
B^3 is scalar.                              (4.5)
```

Thus B is semisimple.

Every nontrivial J_2 block of A with eigenvalue lambda produces two J_2 blocks of A tensor B, with eigenvalues

```text
lambda beta_1,
lambda beta_2,
```

where beta_1,beta_2 are the eigenvalues of B.

In the actual local type (1.1), every nontrivial J_2 block has eigenvalue 1. Therefore

```text
lambda beta_1=lambda beta_2=1,
```

so beta_1=beta_2 and B is scalar.

It follows that every eigenvalue multiplicity of T_0 on V is even. But the chi and chi^(-1) eigenspaces in (1.1) both have dimension h, and h is odd in the only possible e=2 case.

Contradiction.

Hence

```text
[H0-IRRED_h]
H^0 acts irreducibly on V for every h>=2.    (4.6)
```

# 5. Tensor products and multiple simple factors

Since H^0 is connected reductive and acts irreducibly inside Sp(V), its connected center acts by scalars and is finite in Sp(V). Thus H^0 is semisimple.

Suppose H^0 has at least two simple factors. Then

```text
V = V_1 tensor ... tensor V_s,

dim V_i>1.                                 (5.1)
```

Write

```text
nu=nu_1 tensor ... tensor nu_s.
```

Because

```text
(nu-1)^2=0,                                 (5.2)
```

at most one nu_i can be nontrivial. Indeed, if nu_i and nu_j are both nontrivial, with nilpotent parts N_i,N_j, then on a suitable pure tensor

```text
(N_i tensor 1 + 1 tensor N_j)^2
 = 2 N_i tensor N_j != 0
```

in characteristic zero.

Let D be the product of the dimensions of all factors on which nu is trivial. Then

```text
D divides rank(nu-1)=h-1,
D divides dim V=4h-2.                       (5.3)
```

Therefore

```text
D divides gcd(h-1,4h-2) <= 2.               (5.4)
```

Since D>1, necessarily

```text
D=2,
h is odd.                                   (5.5)
```

Thus the only possible tensor-product shape is

```text
V = W_(2h-1) tensor E_2,                    (5.6)
```

with nu nontrivial only on the odd-dimensional factor W.

The two factors have different dimensions, so T_0 normalizes each factor and can be written

```text
T_0=A tensor B,
```

with B acting on E_2 and B^3 scalar. The same J_2-eigenvalue argument from Section 4 forces B to be scalar. Hence every T_0 eigenvalue multiplicity is even, again contradicting the odd multiplicity h of chi and chi^(-1).

Therefore:

```text
[H0-SIMPLE_h]
H^0 is simple for every h>=2.                (5.7)
```

This gives the requested general tensor-decomposition exclusion. Notice that multiplicity-one wild characters alone are not enough; the decisive input is the combination of

```text

rank(nu-1)=h-1,
and the full chi/chi^(-1) tame multiplicities.
```

# 6. The wild p-group is toral in H^0

Let E be the finite image of wild inertia P_infinity in H.

It is an elementary abelian p-group. We now know that H^0 is simple and acts irreducibly on V.

By Schur's lemma, the centralizer of H^0 in Sp(V) is contained in {+1,-1}. Therefore

```text
H/H^0
```

embeds, up to this central group of order 2, in the outer automorphism group of H^0.

For a simple algebraic group in characteristic zero, the diagram automorphism group has order at most 6. Since p>=7,

```text
E subset H^0.                                (6.1)
```

A standard torsion-prime theorem says that an elementary abelian p-subgroup of a connected simple algebraic group in characteristic zero is toral whenever p is not a torsion prime. The only torsion primes for simple groups are among

```text
2,3,5.
```

Hence for p>=7 there is a maximal torus T of H^0 with

```text
E subset T.                                  (6.2)
```

This is the correct torus extracted from the wild part. It does not have to have large rank.

# 7. Wild multiplicities imply the Omega_2 condition

Decompose V into T-weight spaces:

```text
V = direct-sum_lambda V_lambda,
mult(lambda)=dim V_lambda.                  (7.1)
```

Restriction of a T-weight lambda to E is an E-character. Therefore the multiplicity of an E-character is the sum of mult(lambda) over all weights restricting to that character.

From the exact local type (1.6), the E-character multiplicities are:

```text
trivial character: multiplicity 2;
all other occurring characters: multiplicity 1. (7.2)
```

Consequently, every T-weight whose restriction to E is nontrivial has multiplicity 1.

The total multiplicity of all T-weights restricting trivially to E is 2.

Because V is symplectically self-dual,

```text
mult(lambda)=mult(-lambda).                 (7.3)
```

If a nonzero T-weight lambda restricting trivially to E had multiplicity at least 2, then -lambda would be a distinct nonzero weight with the same multiplicity, contributing at least 4 dimensions to the trivial E-character space. This contradicts (7.2).

Thus:

```text
all nonzero T-weights of V have multiplicity 1,
zero-weight multiplicity is at most 2.       (7.4)
```

In the notation of Testerman-Zalesski,

```text
V belongs to Omega_2(H^0).                  (7.5)
```

This is the scalable replacement for the failed rank argument.
