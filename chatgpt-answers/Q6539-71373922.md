## (1) RY automaton status for Apery b_n and c_n

The relevant framework is Rowland-Yassawi, "Automatic congruences for diagonals of rational functions" and related papers on p-automata for sequences satisfying Lucas-type congruences. The key theorem is that if a sequence is the diagonal of a rational function over Z, then its reduction mod p is p-automatic. The automaton state set is generated by Cartier operators Lambda_i applied to the diagonal series.

For Apery numbers b_n:

b_n = Sum_k binom(n,k)^2 binom(n+k,k)^2

b_n is a diagonal of a rational function (Beukers/Delaygue type representation), so the RY theorem applies. However:

- RY do NOT give an explicit minimal automaton for the Apery zeta(3) sequence.

- There is no published theorem giving S(p)=O(1) states for the fixed-prime mod-p automaton.

- The generic Cartier construction gives a finite automaton for each p, but the number of states can grow with p.

Therefore the previous obstruction remains: a digit automaton with state count S(p) approximately p cannot give pointwise Weyl cancellation.

For c_n:

c_n = Apery numerator / 6.

The recurrence relation couples a_n and b_n:

(n+1)^3 a_{n+1}=P(n)a_n-n^3 a_{n-1}

with the same Apéry differential equation. RY applies to the generating diagonal if a diagonal representation is available, but the specific numerator sequence c_n is not covered by an explicit RY Apery automaton theorem. The known p-adic/block congruence treatment is stronger than the generic automaton statement for this purpose.

Conclusion: b_n yes in principle, c_n only indirectly; no explicit Apery automaton spectrum exists in literature.

## (2) Can automaton transfer spectra prove fiber tails?

Define

y^(v)_n = betab_n - alphac_n mod p.

The zero language is

L_v = { n : y^(v)_n = 0 }.

The fiber multiplicity is

m_v = |L_v|.

A p-automaton gives a deterministic labeled graph. For fixed v, counting m_v is an output-counting problem. The desired quantity

N(T)=#{v:m_v>T}

is a distribution over the family of linear output twists.

The correct operator is not the usual transition operator A. One needs a twisted family

A_v = sum_d M_d * chi_v(output_d)

or equivalently a transfer operator on the product graph

(state, v).

A spectral gap for this operator could imply concentration:

sum_v m_v^2 = p^{-1} ||transfer||^2

and then

N(T) <= E[m_v^2]/T^2.

So the required mechanism is genuinely second-moment, not pointwise.

However the large state space problem remains:

S(p) ~ p

means the transfer matrix dimension is already O(p). A spectral gap independent of p is not supplied by the RY theorem. Thus the failure mode is similar but not identical:

- Pointwise Weyl: needs operator norm on a large graph.

- Fiber tail: only needs second moment, but still needs a uniform spectral estimate on the same growing graph.

A possible escape is that the family over v has extra algebraic rank collapse: the product graph may quotient by projective orbit symmetries. This is exactly the direction worth testing.

## (4) Random benchmark for quotient fiber energy

Without quotienting, for a random map

F_p -> F_p

fiber sizes have

E(sum_v m_v^2)=p + (p-1) = 2p.

The average is therefore

E[m_v^2]=2.

After removing the forced reflection collision

b_{p-1-r}=b_r,

there are about p/2 independent positions. Each orbit contributes a forced pair collision.

Hence the quotient random model has:

number of samples q=(p+1)/2,

birthday contribution:

q(q-1)/p,

plus the diagonal q.

Therefore

sum_v m_v^2 approximately q + q(q-1)/p.

Dividing by p gives

E[m_v^2] approximately

1/2 + 1/4 = 3/4

in quotient-normalized coordinates.

Restoring the original p fibers gives

sum_v m_v^2 approximately p*(3/2)

because every quotient hit represents two original orbit points except fixed points.

This is the exact numerical baseline to test against the p=13,17 FFT data.

## Final verdict

RY automata are applicable structurally but do not currently provide the missing fiber tail theorem. The right target is not automaton state count reduction but a twisted transfer operator with a p-uniform spectral gap after quotienting the Apéry reflection symmetry.

The decisive experiment is p=13,17: build the Cartier graph, compute its spectrum, and compare the second moment against the quotient random constant. A stable gap would be evidence for the missing FIBER-TAIL mechanism; absence of a gap confirms the state-growth obstruction.
