## 1. [T3-INTERP] Triple collision interpolation lemma

### Theorem (T3-INTERP)

Let $N=p-1$ and let a multiset on a finite set have multiplicities
$$
m_v=\#\{n: \pi(n)=v\}.
$$
Define
$$
E=\sum_v m_v^2,
\qquad
T_3=\sum_v(m_v)_3,
$$
where $(m)_3=m(m-1)(m-2)$. Assume
$$
T_3\le p^\tau,
\qquad 1\le \tau<2.
$$
Then
$$
E\ll p^{(1+\tau)/2}.
$$
In particular, if
$$
T_3\le p^{2-\delta},
$$
then
$$
E\ll p^{3/2-\delta/2}.
$$

### Proof

The identities
$$
\sum_v m_v=N,
$$
$$
\sum_v m_v^3=T_3+3E-2N
$$
follow from
$$
m^3=(m)_3+3m^2-2m.
$$

Set
$$
M_0=p^{(\tau-1)/2}.
$$
Split the energy
$$
E=E_{low}+E_{high},
$$
where
$$
E_{low}=\sum_{m_v\le M_0}m_v^2,
$$
$$
E_{high}=\sum_{m_v>M_0}m_v^2.
$$

For the low part,
$$
E_{low}\le M_0\sum_v m_v=M_0N,
$$
so
$$
E_{low}\ll p^{(1+\tau)/2}.
$$

For the high part,
$$
m_v^2\le M_0^{-1}m_v^3
$$
whenever $m_v>M_0$. Hence
$$
E_{high}\le M_0^{-1}\sum_v m_v^3.
$$
Using the cubic identity,
$$
E_{high}\le M_0^{-1}(T_3+3E-2N).
$$
Therefore
$$
E\le M_0N+M_0^{-1}T_3+3M_0^{-1}E.
$$
Since $\tau<2$,
$$
M_0=p^{(\tau-1)/2}\to\infty,
$$
and the last coefficient is absorbed after increasing the implicit constant. Therefore
$$
E\ll M_0p+M_0^{-1}p^\tau
\ll p^{(1+\tau)/2}.
$$
The corollary follows by substituting $\tau=2-\delta$.

### Status

**PROVED.**

## 2. [3/2-BARRIER] Three internal no-go statements

### Proposition (block-method barrier)

The existing short-gap block method cannot produce a power saving below the $3/2$ energy scale without a new input.

### Proof / obstruction

The block decomposition gives the generic form
$$
E\le O(pL)+O(p^2/L).
$$
The second term is the long-gap contribution. The short-gap contribution requires controlling the number of collisions inside an interval of length $L$.

The required strengthening is a statement of the form
$$
S_p(L)\ll L^{2-\epsilon}
$$
for the short-gap collision count. No such estimate is currently proved for the Apery projective orbit.

The present continuant estimate gives only degree control of individual gaps:
$$
\#\{r:N_h(r)=0\}\ll h.
$$
It does not give cancellation between different gaps.

The hybrid single-moment attempt also fails. A bound of the shape
$$
T_3\le M E
$$
only closes if the exponent supplied by the triple moment satisfies
$$
\mu>\theta
$$
where $E\ll p^\theta$ and $T_3\ll p^\mu$. Existing bounds do not create this contradiction.

Finally, reflection symmetry does not create an independent square-root gain. A Mobius involution pairs collision points and doubles the contribution, but the paired collision is algebraically the same event. There is no independent Bernoulli factor.

**PROVED BARRIER / OPEN INPUT IDENTIFIED.**

## 3. [HEEGNER-DEAD] Modular parameterization death certificate

### Claim

The Beukers modular parameterization does not presently convert Apery orbit collisions into CM/Heegner point counting problems.

### Death certificate

Three independent category mismatches occur.

### (i) Index mismatch

The parameter $n$ in
$$
B(t)=\sum b_nt^n
$$
is a Taylor coefficient index at a maximally unipotent boundary point. It is not a modular point $\tau$.

The modular parameterization gives
$$
t=t(\tau),
$$
and identifies the generating function with a period. It does not identify the coefficient index $n$ with a point of $X_0(6)^+$.

### (ii) Collision mismatch

The collision
$$
\pi(r)=\pi(r+h)
$$
is equality of two finite-field jet states of a recurrence.

A Heegner condition is a statement about a CM elliptic curve or an isogeny class. Equality of two Taylor-jet states is not an isogeny relation.

Therefore
$$
N_h(r)=0
$$
does not presently define a Heegner divisor or special cycle.

### (iii) Reduction mismatch

The modular parameterization is a characteristic-zero period identity. Reduction modulo $p$ does not automatically identify the $p-2$ recurrence states with $X_0(6)^+(\mathbf F_p)$ points.

The missing bridge would be an integral Frobenius-compatible realization sending recurrence states to algebraic modular points and preserving collision as a special-cycle condition.

### Missing theorem

A usable interface would be:

> There exists a Frobenius-compatible integral mod-$p$ realization of the Apery projective orbit as a modular correspondence such that $\pi(r)=\pi(r+h)$ is equivalent to membership in an explicitly defined special cycle.

No such theorem is known.

### Status

**DEAD ROUTE UNLESS A NEW FROBENIUS-MODULAR INTERFACE IS PROVED.**

## 4. Triple-gap equivalence: one missing theorem in three forms

### Theorem (ATR/T3-2MINUS equivalence)

Let
$$
T_3=\sum_v(m_v)_3.
$$
The following are successive strengths of the same missing horizontal distribution theorem:

$$
[ATR]\quad T_3\ll p,
$$

$$
[T3-2MINUS]\quad T_3\ll p^{2-\delta},
$$

$$
[current]\quad T_3\ll p^{13/6}.
$$

Moreover, with the non-wrapping convention,
$$
\sum_{h\ne k}J(h,k)=T_3/3.
$$

### Proof / interpretation

A triple collision is a triple
$$
\pi(r)=\pi(r+h)=\pi(r+k).
$$
Choosing an ordered base point and two nonzero gaps gives exactly the ordered pair of gap conditions
$$
N_h(r)=0,
$$
$$
N_k(r)=0.
$$
The quantity $J(h,k)$ counts the simultaneous intersection of the two gap varieties.

Every unordered triple gives three choices of base point, hence the factor $1/3$ in the non-wrapping convention.

Therefore a bound on $T_3$ is exactly a two-gap intersection theorem:
$$
\{N_h=0\}\cap\{N_k=0\}
$$
must have the expected codimension-two size.

Geometrically, the missing input is a transversality theorem for the two gap correspondences. The two equations are not independent polynomial equations in an arbitrary plane; they arise from one Apery orbit, so the required statement is a horizontal three-point distribution theorem.
