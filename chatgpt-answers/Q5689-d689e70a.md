### 3.2 Evertse’s S-unit estimates

These theorems concern solutions of additive equations in a fixed finitely generated group of S-units, or constant-coefficient recurrence terms expressible as bounded sums of exponential terms. They are fundamental inputs behind later fixed-S recurrence results.

## 9. Why no cited theorem bridges the final quantifier gap

The available conclusions can be organized as follows.

- Fixed S, every large n: Bugeaud–Evertse and classical S-unit methods, but only C-finite recurrences and only at the o(n) scale.

- Moving smooth cutoff, most n: Balaji–Luca, but for Lucas sequences and an initial prime segment.

- Apéry/P-recursive, most n: Luca–Shparlinski and the partial bounds above.

- Fixed p, all n: Lucas congruences, diagonal automaticity, and p-adic valuation theorems.

- Every n, existence/lower bounds: primitive-divisor and largest-prime-factor results, which point in the opposite direction.

- G-function/diagonal structure: denominator and congruence control, not horizontal numerator-prime anti-concentration.

The missing theorem is exactly horizontal and uniform:

Neither fixed-S Subspace-Theorem technology nor current diagonal/G-function arithmetic supplies this supremum.

## 10. A sufficient new relation

A useful future theorem must couple different characteristics or prevent interval concentration. Any one of the following would suffice:

1. Uniform local zero-density: for every n and interval I of length H=n^{1/3},

1. Weighted local large sieve with a pointwise remainder: an inequality whose right side is o(H) after specializing to one column n, not only after averaging columns.

1. Cross-prime carrier: a nonzero integer C_{n,I} of height o(H) divisible by every target prime in I.

1. Spike transport/coherence: a theorem forcing a positive fraction of a local spike to reappear at enough nearby indices, so the repository codegree theorem rules it out.

1. A genuine moving-S theorem for the Apéry differential module: uniform in |S|\asymp H/\log n, with an error o(H) and no exceptional n. This would be far stronger than current Subspace-Theorem estimates.

Without one of these new inputs, the best rigorous output remains O(H) pointwise and o(H) only for density-one indices for each preselected band.

## 11. Final conclusion

No existing theorem located in the primary literature implies the requested every-n, every-band o(n^{1/3}) bound for Apéry numbers. The direct Apéry literature is density-one and lower-bound oriented; fixed-S recurrence theorems are C-finite and nonuniform in S; Subspace-Theorem gcd results require fixed torus/S-unit structure and operate at height scale n; arithmetic G-function results control denominators; diagonal/automatic methods fix one prime at a time.

The strongest clean, genuinely applicable partial result is:

For any one preselected interval of length H=n^{1/3} in a fixed quotient branch, the target radical has logarithm o(H) for a density-one set of coefficient indices; quantitatively, the set where it exceeds H/\log n has size O_a(n^{2/3}\log n) in a dyadic branch window. Pointwise for every index, only the uniform O(H) bound is currently justified.

The central-binomial counterexample shows that no theorem based only on being P-recursive, a G-function coefficient sequence, or a rational diagonal can improve this to o(H). The missing ingredient must be specifically Apéry and horizontally cross-prime.
