# Strategic verdict

The corrected second-jet calculation is structurally meaningful. The cancellation of the free first-jet coordinate (eta) is exactly the event one would need before a scalar obstruction can appear. However, that cancellation alone does not decide whether the scalar is new.

There is no general theorem for inhomogeneous second-order difference equations forcing every higher-jet compatibility obstruction to be the original value equation (F_p(r)=0) times a unit. The reason is that the recurrence defect

[

mathcal D_p(Z)=-16(2Z+1)(Z^p-Z)^2

]

is supported to second order on the finite-field seam. When one transports jets around the reflected cycle, this defect defines an inhomogeneous cohomology class. Its second-order holonomy may survive after the homogeneous jet coordinate cancels. Such a class can produce a genuine independent subresultant.

The correct conclusion is therefore:

The route is open in principle, but only after a saturated elimination test. One must prove that the resulting scalar is nonzero modulo the value equation and after removing all Möbius denominators, recurrence singular factors, reflection factors, and powers of (Z^p-Z).

Without that test, the scalar may still be merely a disguised reconstruction of (F_p(r)=0).

# 1. The finite polynomial and the seam

Let (F_pinmathbf F_p[Z]) be the unique polynomial of degree at most (p-1) with

[

F_p(r)=b_rqquad(rinmathbf F_p).

]

The finite-Racah symmetry gives

[

F_p(-1-Z)=F_p(Z)

]

as a polynomial function on (mathbf F_p), and the corrected recurrence identity has the form

[

L_ZF_p=-16(2Z+1)(Z^p-Z)^2,

]

where (L_Z) is the second-order Apéry difference operator written in the finite-Racah coordinate.

Two features matter.

1. On every field point (rinmathbf F_p), the defect vanishes to order at least two because (r^p-r=0).

1. In the polynomial ring it is not zero. Its first nontrivial information lives in the second infinitesimal neighborhood of the reduced finite-field divisor

[

V(Z^p-Z).

]

Thus the second jet is precisely the first order at which the global interpolation polynomial can distinguish itself from an exact recurrence solution while still agreeing with the Apéry orbit at all field points.

# 2. Jet torsors for a second-order difference equation

Write a local state as

[

Y_Z=binom{F_p(Z)}{F_p(Z+1)}.

]

The homogeneous recurrence transports this by a matrix (M(Z)). Differentiating with respect to the interpolation variable gives a triangular jet system. Schematically,

[

begin{aligned}

Y_{Z+1}&=M(Z)Y_Z+E_0(Z),\

Y'_{Z+1}&=M(Z)Y'_Z+M'(Z)Y_Z+E_1(Z),\

Y''_{Z+1}&=M(Z)Y''_Z+2M'(Z)Y'_Z+M''(Z)Y_Z+E_2(Z).

end{aligned}

]

At a zero (F_p(r)=0), the zeroth-order state lies on a one-dimensional line. Reflection identifies the state at

[

r^*=p-1-r=-1-r.

]

At first order, the reflected transport normally leaves one affine coordinate (eta). This is a torsor under the one-dimensional homogeneous derivative solution.

At second order, one obtains two affine formulas for the reflected second jet:

[

J_2^{rm tr}=A(r,p)+B(r,p)eta,

qquad

J_2^{rm ref}=C(r,p)+D(r,p)eta.

]

The corrected calculation says

[

B(r,p)=D(r,p).

]

Hence compatibility becomes the scalar equation

[

S_p(r):=A(r,p)-C(r,p)=0.

]

This is the correct structural mechanism. The free torsor coordinate disappears, leaving the holonomy of the inhomogeneous second-jet extension.

# 3. Why no general rank-one theorem kills the scalar

For a homogeneous second-order recurrence, all jets are obtained by differentiating a two-dimensional solution module. In that setting, a closed transport cycle can indeed make higher compatibility equations dependent on the original state equation.

Here the system is not homogeneous. The defect contributes a cocycle. Let (T) denote transport from (r) to (r^*), and (R) the reflection identification. On second jets, the closed operator has the form

[

mathcal H_2=R^{-1}T=H_2^{rm hom}+c_2,

]

where (c_2) is the accumulated defect class. First-order compatibility says the affine fixed-point equation has a one-dimensional solution torsor. At second order, the coefficient of that torsor can cancel while the translation part remains nonzero.

Algebraically, this is the distinction between:

- the homogeneous kernel of (H_2^{rm hom}-1), and

- the image of the defect class in the cokernel of (H_2^{rm hom}-1).

There is no formal reason for the latter class to vanish or to be divisible by (F_p(r)). It vanishes automatically only under an additional exactness statement: the seam cocycle must be a coboundary in the reflected jet complex.

Therefore a theorem of the form

[

S_p(Z)=U_p(Z)F_p(Z)

]

cannot be deduced merely from second order, reflection, or rank two. It must be proved by an explicit identity.

# 5. Concrete decision tests

## Test A: reduction modulo the value equation

Compute the normal form

[

overline S_p=S_pbmod F_p.

]

- If (overline S_p=0), the scalar lies in ((F_p)) and is value-tautological before finite-field saturation.

- If (overline S_pne0), it is algebraically independent of the value polynomial in (mathbf F_p[Z]).

This is the fastest symbolic test, but it is not sufficient by itself because (S_p) could differ from a multiple of (F_p) by a multiple of (Z^p-Z).

## Test B: saturated finite-field remainder

Compute

[

S_p^{rm red}=S_pbmod (Z^p-Z),

]

then compare

[

G_p=gcd(S_p^{rm red},F_p,Z^p-Z).

]

There are three outcomes.

1. Tautology:

[

operatorname{rad}G_p=operatorname{rad}gcd(F_p,Z^p-Z)

]

and (S_p^{rm red}) vanishes on every field zero for the same ideal-theoretic reason as (F_p).

1. Independent necessary condition:

[

Z(F_p)subseteq Z(S_p^{rm red})

]

but (S_p^{rm red}notinsqrt{(F_p,Z^p-Z)}) before inserting the zero assumption. This can happen only if the elimination construction genuinely uses reflected transport and the defect class. In a reduced finite field algebra, vanishing on all zeros is equivalent to membership in the radical ideal, so the independence must be visible before specializing to the zero scheme, for example through a lower-degree generator of the elimination ideal or a nontrivial subresultant relation.

1. Failure: some actual zero (r) has (S_p(r)ne0). Then a denominator, seam term, or transport coefficient remains incorrect.

## Test C: quotient by the universal seam

The raw formulas may contain powers of (Z^p-Z). Write

[

S_p(Z)=A_p(Z)+(Z^p-Z)B_p(Z).

]

Only (A_p=S_pbmod(Z^p-Z)) has pointwise content. If (A_p=0), the apparent scalar is entirely a seam identity and gives no zero restriction. If (A_p=U_pF_pbmod(Z^p-Z)), it reconstructs the original equation. The route is genuinely reopened only when (A_p) is nonzero and its derivation gives an elimination generator not generated by the value equation.

## Test D: finite-field zero-set comparison

For a substantial prime range, record

[

Z_p(F)={r:F_p(r)=0},

qquad

Z_p(S)={r:S_p(r)=0}.

]

The minimum passing condition is

[

Z_p(F)subseteq Z_p(S)

]

for every tested prime and every zero, including central zeros. The useful diagnostic is the excess

[

]

- If (Z_p(S)=mathbf F_p), the scalar is a seam multiple.

- If (Z_p(S)=Z_p(F)) almost always and (deg S_pasymp p), it is likely another interpolation of the same value equation.

- If (deg S_p=O(1)) or (p^{o(1)}) and it contains all Apéry zeros, that is a major new theorem: it immediately bounds the zero fiber by the degree, outside identically zero exceptional primes.

# 6. Subresultant interpretation

The right symbolic object is not the raw resultant of the two endpoint equations, because reflection already builds in a universal factor. Form the two affine second-jet equations after first-order elimination:

[

L_1(r,p,eta)=0,qquad L_2(r,p,eta)=0.

]

Their resultant in (eta) is

[

operatorname{Res}_eta(L_1,L_2).

]

When the (eta)-coefficients coincide, the ordinary resultant degenerates to the scalar difference times a predictable leading coefficient. One must divide by:

- the common (eta)-coefficient;

- all Möbius denominators;

- all recurrence singular factors;

- the universal reflection factor;

- every power of (Z^p-Z) forced by the seam.

Call the remaining primitive polynomial (S_p^sharp(Z)). This is the candidate independent subresultant.

The definitive symbolic tests are

[

gcdbigl(S_p^sharp,F_pbigr),

qquad

S_p^sharpbmod(F_p,Z^p-Z),

]

and the saturation identity

[

bigl(I_p^{rm sat}capmathbf F_p[Z]bigr)

stackrel{?}= (F_p)

]

inside (mathbf F_p[Z]/(Z^p-Z)).

If equality holds, second order reconstructs the value equation. If the elimination ideal is strictly larger, the double seam has created a genuine new compatibility condition.

# 7. What degree behavior would be genuinely useful

A polynomial (S_p) of degree (O(p)) is not enough. The original interpolant (F_p) already has degree at most (p-1). Replacing it by another degree-(cp) equation gives no vertical or horizontal saving.

The first meaningful thresholds are:

1. Bounded degree: (deg S_ple D) uniformly. Then every nonexceptional prime has at most (D) Apéry zeros. This would be a strong vertical theorem, though by itself bounded rows do not prove the fixed-column all-(n) result.

1. Subpower degree: (deg S_p=p^{o(1)}). This gives (|Z_p|=p^{o(1)}), still only vertical unless the coefficients globalize.

1. Structured fixed family: (S_p(Z)) is the reduction of one characteristic-zero polynomial or rational function (S(P,Z)) of bounded degree and controlled height. This is the relevant horizontal possibility.

The universal factor (Z^p-Z) is dangerous because its degree is (p). Any formula that retains it, its derivative, or a quotient represented by a degree-(p) polynomial has not compressed the problem.

# 8. From (r=n-p) to an all-prime carrier

Suppose every top-half target satisfies

[

pmid b_{n-p}

quadLongrightarrowquad

S_p(n-p)=0pmod p.

]

This is not yet a global carrier. To prove

[

sum_{substack{n/2<ple n\pmid b_{n-p}}}log p=o(n),

]

one needs a nonzero integer (C_n), defined without knowing the target primes, such that every target (pmid C_n) and

[

log|C_n|=o(n).

]

A sufficient globalization pattern is:

[

S_p(r)equiv mathcal S(n,r)pmod p,

qquad p=n-r,

]

where (mathcal Sinmathbf Z[n,r]) has bounded degree and coefficients of subexponential height, followed by elimination of (r) using a second fixed relation of bounded degree. Merely evaluating (mathcal S(n,n-p)) still depends on (p).

Three viable shapes would be:

- Fixed evaluation: (S_p(n-p)equiv C_npmod p) for one integer (C_n) with (log|C_n|=o(n)).

- Bounded list: every target divides one of (n^{o(1)}) integers whose total logarithmic height is (o(n)).

- Resultant with selective multiplicity: a characteristic-zero resultant (R_nne0) of height (e^{o(n)}) in which each target contributes at least one factor (p).

If (S_p) has bounded degree but coefficients of size (p^{O(p)}), or if one multiplies a separate value over all candidate primes, the height is at least linear and usually quadratic in (n). The coefficient dependence on (p) must therefore be extremely rigid.

# 9. Monodromy decision in cohomological language

Let (V) be the two-dimensional homogeneous state module. The first derivative extension is a torsor under (V); the second derivative extension is a torsor under another copy of (V), twisted by the first jet. Reflection closes the path and gives a cyclic group action generated by (H).

The second-order obstruction is the image of the seam cocycle in

[

operatorname{coker}(H-1).

]

- If this class is zero, the cocycle is a coboundary and the scalar must reduce to a consequence of lower-order compatibility.

- If it is nonzero, the scalar is a genuine holonomy invariant.

The corrected equality of the (eta)-coefficients says only that the obstruction lands in the cokernel; it does not say whether its class is zero.

A clean theorem to prove would therefore be:

Second-seam nonexactness lemma. After saturation by the regularity divisor and reduction modulo (Z^p-Z), the accumulated second-order defect cocycle has nonzero class in (operatorname{coker}(H-1)), and its scalar representative is not in the ideal generated by (F_p).

That is genuinely new and sufficient to establish algebraic independence of the second-jet condition. It still needs the degree/global-height analysis above to affect P3.2.

# 10. Recommended exact audit sequence

1. Produce (S_p^{rm raw}(Z)) symbolically over (mathbf Z[p,Z]), retaining every denominator.

1. Clear denominators once, then take primitive content.

1. Saturate by the product of the denominators and recurrence singular factors.

1. Divide the maximal power of (Z^p-Z) and all forced reflection factors.

1. Reduce the result modulo (Z^p-Z).

1. Verify (S_p(r)=0) at every actual Apéry zero for a substantial prime range.

1. Compute (S_pbmod F_p) and the saturated elimination ideal.

1. Record (deg_ZS_p), coefficient height before reduction, and whether the formula descends from a fixed polynomial in ((p,Z)).

1. Test central zeros separately, since (2r+1=0) may be a saturated factor rather than a valid unit.

1. Only after these pass, attempt the substitution (r=n-p) and construct a prime-independent integer carrier.

# Final assessment

The corrected second-jet coincidence is a legitimate reopening, not automatically a reconstruction of the rank-one calculation. The double seam defect can in principle create a genuinely independent subresultant because it is an inhomogeneous second-order cocycle supported on the second infinitesimal neighborhood of (Z^p-Z=0).

But the burden of proof is exact:

[

S_p^sharpnotinsqrt{(F_p,Z^p-Z)}

]

at the symbolic transport level, while every actual Apéry zero must satisfy (S_p^sharp(r)=0). The practical discriminator is the saturated elimination ideal together with the gcd and zero-set tests above.

Even a successful independent scalar advances P3.2 only if its degree is bounded or subpower and its (p)-dependence globalizes to a nonzero characteristic-zero carrier of logarithmic height (o(n)). A degree-(Theta(p)) scalar, or one whose only source is the universal factor (Z^p-Z), merely repackages the original interpolation equation.
